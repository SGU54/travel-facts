## THE HORIZONS

*Standfirst: What has to be booked ahead, and what genuinely does not.*

Most booking advice is a counsel of anxiety: reserve everything, early. It is
poor advice in a country where a great deal remains available late and cheap, and
it obscures the small number of things that are genuinely time-bound.

Three horizons matter, and they are set by structure rather than by demand.

**Before you leave the United States.** The International Driving Permit, if you
intend to drive. It is issued by two authorised bodies and cannot be obtained
from abroad. `◇ OFFICIAL` This is the only item on any Turkey list that becomes
strictly impossible rather than merely harder once you have gone.

**Before the calendar moves under you.** The two Bayram holidays shift roughly ten
to eleven days earlier each year against the Gregorian calendar. `● ESTABLISHED`
Anyone planning from an older set of notes is planning against dates that have
already moved by a month. Check where they fall for your year before choosing
weeks, not after.

**Before the internal legs are separated.** A domestic flight bought on the same
ticket as the international one carries the carrier's obligation to re-route you;
bought separately, it does not. `◇ OFFICIAL` That choice is made at booking and
cannot be revisited at the airport.

**Before you book the thing you came for.** Where a travel policy covers an
adventure activity at all, it does so through an extension bought in advance;
an add-on cannot be applied to a flight already taken or a dive already made.
`●` The balloon, the tandem flight off Babadag and the scooter are the three
that most often sit outside a base policy, and all three are ordinarily booked
after arrival, which is to say after the moment the cover could have been added.

Everything else — hotels outside the peak coastal weeks, restaurants, most
internal transport, most tickets — tolerates being arranged closer in than
American planning instincts suggest. Our own reporting is thin on how far ahead
Bayram transport fills, and no operator publishes it; that absence is a reason to move
the dates rather than to guess the lead time.

## THE PLAN

*Standfirst: The week as a sequence of operations, not an itinerary.*

This book does not tell you where to go. The site does that at length, and a
route is a matter of taste rather than of reasoning. What it can usefully say is
which operations a Turkish week contains, and in what order they constrain each
other.

A first trip is built from three or four bases and two or three moves between
them. The moves are the operations; the bases are where you rest between them.

**Every move is a booking decision before it is a travel day.** Whether the leg
sits on the international ticket determines who owns it if it fails, and that is
decided weeks earlier at a screen, not on the morning itself.

**Most inter-region moves pass through Istanbul.** `●` It is where the network
converges, which means a connection there is the commonest single point of
contact between an internal leg and the flight home.

**The city has two airports and they are on opposite sides of it.** `●` A move
that arrives at one and departs from the other is a road journey across sixteen
million people, and no airline system treats it as a connection.

**Cash requirement is a property of how you travel, not of Turkey.** A week of
hotels and card-taking restaurants consumes almost none; a week of bazaars, small
lokantas and non-app taxis consumes it steadily. `●`

Order those four and the week plans itself. Reverse them: pick the sights first,
fit the moves around them — and the constraints arrive as surprises.

## FAILURE MODES

*Standfirst: Organised by what goes wrong, because that is what you know at the
moment it does.*

Every other chapter is organised by subject. This one is not, and the difference
is the point: a website of articles must file a problem under a topic, but at the
moment something goes wrong you know the symptom and not the category. What
follows is arranged by consequence, and the causes underneath each are drawn from
wherever in the trip they originate.

### You lose the flight home

*The most expensive failure available, and the one with the most entrances.*

**The internal leg was a separate booking.** The first flight ran late, the second
recorded a no-show, and the second carrier has no relationship with the first.
`◇ OFFICIAL` A replacement seat is a new purchase at whatever fare exists that
day.

**The connection was sized from a number nobody published.** Neither Istanbul
airport states a minimum connection time. `◇ OFFICIAL` A gap that felt generous
was chosen against a figure invented by a stranger with different luggage.

**The onward flight left from the other airport.** Both are called Istanbul in
casual speech; only the codes distinguish them. `●`

**The bag had to be reclaimed.** On separate tickets it does, which adds passport
control, a baggage hall and a second check-in to a sequence that was measured
without them. `◇ OFFICIAL`

*If it happens:* on one booking, the carrier owes you re-routing or a refund and
the choice is yours. On two, the second carrier owes nothing — and your travel
policy's trip-interruption section, not its medical section, is the one to read.

### You are uninsured at the moment it matters

*Quiet until it isn't, and decided months before the trip.*

**The activity was in the exclusions.** Standard policies treat motorised
two-wheelers, and often ballooning, as excluded families unless bought back
explicitly. `◇ OFFICIAL` The Cappadocia balloon most people book first is the
activity most likely to sit in one.

**The licence did not match the machine.** Where a policy covers two-wheelers it
typically requires a licence valid for that vehicle class. `◇ OFFICIAL` A rental
shack handing over keys is a commercial decision, not a determination that you
are licensed.

**The exclusion reached further than expected.** An activity exclusion can remove
claims *arising from* the activity — including the medical section, which carries
the largest realistic exposure. `◇ OFFICIAL`

**The police never attended.** A claim arising from an accident asks for a police
report, and that document exists only if police were involved at the time. `●` A
joint form completed between two drivers cannot be converted into one afterwards.

*If it happens:* Turkish emergency care is provided before payment is discussed.
`◇ OFFICIAL` The exposure is what comes after stabilisation, and the documents
that decide it are gathered at the time, not later.

### You are stopped at a counter

*Cheap to prevent, disproportionate to fix.*

**The rental desk wanted something Turkish law does not require.** Age, licence
duration, a credit card in the driver's name, a permit — all contractual, all
per-company, none published as a general rule. `●`

**The document was in an alphabet the reader could not use.** That is the entire
function of the International Driving Permit, and the reason to carry one is
unrelated to whether the law demands it. `◇ OFFICIAL`

**The passport had less than six months left.** Six months beyond date of entry,
with a blank page. `◇ OFFICIAL` The hundred-and-fifty-day figure that circulates
is not the American-facing requirement.

### The thing you bought stays in Turkey

*Rare, and severe when it happens.*

**It met the criteria without being ancient.** No age threshold exists in the
statute; protection attaches to scientific and cultural character. `◇ OFFICIAL`
The hundred-year rule is trade vocabulary.

**The certificate was the shop's.** A vendor document has no independent force in
an export decision — only a state museum determination binds. `●` And a
certificate that establishes age convincingly argues the object *toward* the
protected category.

**It was picked up rather than bought.** Anything found must be reported within
three days and is State property from the moment it was found. `◇ OFFICIAL`
There is no ownership step to undo.

**It cleared Turkey and met a US restriction.** The American restrictions on
Turkish material reach ethnological objects to 1923, which is more recent than
"antiquity" suggests. `◇ OFFICIAL` The two systems ask different questions and
neither defers to the other.

### The money went somewhere you did not look

*Small each time, and continuous.*

**The machine's own charge was a percentage.** At least one major Turkish bank
publishes a foreign-card ATM withdrawal charge that way. `◇ OFFICIAL` Most
travellers are braced for a flat fee.

**The conversion was accepted at the screen.** Dynamic currency conversion
substitutes the terminal provider's rate for your issuer's. `●`

**The trip needed more cash than planned.** Bazaars, small restaurants, non-app
taxis and tips are cash settings, and how many of those a week contains is a
property of the traveller. `●`

**A figure came from an older source.** Consumer prices have been rising at
around a third a year; an old number is not slightly low but unreliable by an
unpredictable proportion. `◇ OFFICIAL`

## KIT

*Standfirst: The card to print, or photograph, and carry.*

Six lines. Everything else in this book is reasoning; this is the residue.

1. **Passport: six months beyond entry, one blank page.** Not a hundred and fifty
   days.
2. **Both Istanbul airports are called Istanbul.** Check the code on the booking,
   not the name.
3. **Same ticket or separate** decides who owns a missed connection. Know which
   you hold.
4. **No published minimum connection time exists.** Any number you were given was
   invented.
5. **The rental desk's requirements are its contract, not Turkish law.** Read the
   booking terms.
6. **A shop's certificate binds nobody.** Only a state museum determination does.
7. **The eSIM carries data, not your number.** The code that unfreezes a card
   arrives by text.
8. **Bayram starts at 13:00 the day before.** Banks and government shut on the
   arife half-day, not on the holiday.
9. **Prescription medication travels with its paperwork.** A doctor's letter or
   prescription stating you must take it during the trip.
10. **Cash is a property of how you travel.** Bazaars, small lokantas and non-app
   taxis consume it; hotels and card restaurants do not.

## WHAT THIS BOOK WON'T TELL YOU

*Standfirst: The refusals, and why each one protects you.*

**It will not name a restaurant.** A named restaurant is a guess with a byline. It
is right until a chef leaves and wrong for a season after that, and you can get
one free from anyone. What outlasts it is the rule for recognising the right sort
of place, which the site gives you at length and which does not expire.

**It will not name a hotel.** A hotel pick is stale within a season. The decision
of *which area, and why* is the durable part, and the site has already made it
well enough that repeating it here would be padding.

**It will not print a price.** Prices in a currency moving at this rate are not
approximations, they are artefacts. Putting one in a book that will be read for a
year would manufacture exactly the false confidence this book was written
against. Prices live on the Field Sheet, which is dated and reissued.

**It will not republish anyone's ratings.** Aggregate scores are somebody else's
property and somebody else's methodology, and reselling them in a PDF is both
legally and editorially untenable.

**It will not tell you a number that does not exist.** This is the one that
produced the book. Where the research found no published figure — the connection
time, the hospital tariff, the rental requirement, the age threshold — you get
the absence and the reasoning, not a plausible invention. That is less
comfortable than a table and it is the only version that survives contact with an
airport.

**It will not tell you whether you are covered.** The activities chapter names
which categories insurers habitually write exclusions around, and where the
condition inside an exclusion usually sits. It stops there deliberately. Exclusion
wording varies between insurers, and between two plans sold by the same insurer,
and the only document that governs your trip is the schedule with your name on
it. A book that told you the balloon was covered would be wrong for a large share
of its readers, and wrong in the direction that costs the most. What this one can
do is tell you which page of your own policy to open, and when to open it, which
is before the thing is booked rather than after it is flown.

## CLOSING

Turkey rewards the traveller who arrives with the practical questions already
settled, because it leaves nothing between them and the country itself — which
is, on any honest accounting, one of the most rewarding places an American can
spend two weeks.

The balloons over Cappadocia at first light are worth the early alarm and the
uncertainty about whether they will fly. Ephesus in late afternoon, when the
tour coaches have gone and the marble takes the low sun, is as close as a
European ruin comes to being handed to you privately. And a Turkish breakfast on
a terrace above the Aegean, taken slowly and without a plan for the rest of the
morning, makes an argument for the whole enterprise of travel that no itinerary
can improve on.

None of that needs a decision manual. It needs only that the decisions are
already behind you.

# Quality standard

Use this order when constraints conflict: accuracy → usefulness → consistency →
readability/navigation → visual finish. Design follows a correct answer.

## Content acceptance

A consequential recommendation has a source-backed premise, a defined reader, an
executable action, and a valid alternative when an assumption changes. A route says
whether time means walking, transport, door-to-door, or the complete visit. A stay says
whether the count means nights, calendar days, arrival/departure days, or full days.

Audit the assembled itinerary, not only individual chapters. Check opening times,
transfer buffers, geographic order, fatigue, booking sequence, and fallback feasibility.
State what is estimated. Reduced mobility requires route-specific evidence; flat-looking
maps and short distances do not establish accessibility.

Maintain a revision matrix: original issue → source correction → all dependent locations
→ rebuilt output pages → verification. Search for obsolete phrases and numbers globally.
A corrected table does not excuse an outdated diagram, card, or summary.

## Reading acceptance

- Body: start around 11–12pt printed, with comfortable line spacing; increase where the
  audience or format needs it. Essential captions, table values, and map labels normally
  ≥9pt after scaling. Furniture may be smaller because it is not the instruction.
- Keep explanation in a readable proportional face; reserve mono for short identifiers.
- Headings stay with the content they introduce. Tables repeat their real headers when
  spanning pages. A row or boxed verdict should not be cut into illegible fragments.
- Distinguish links in text, keep source URLs clickable, and provide meaningful bookmarks
  and a linked contents page for longer books.
- Use searchable text, document title/author/language metadata where supported, semantic
  source headings/tables, and descriptive captions/alt text. Do not claim PDF/UA compliance
  unless the resulting file was specifically validated for that standard.
- Meaning must survive grayscale; an exception cannot be communicated by color alone.
- A phone PDF needs reflowed, larger text. Reducing a Letter page is not a phone edition.
  A wallet card must be designed and inspected at its actual cut dimensions.

## Visual review

Rasterize every page. A contact sheet assesses sequence; it cannot prove small text is
legible. Inspect all pages at readable size, with higher-resolution detail for dense
figures, page seams, and any suspect clipping. Look at the cover at 180–240px wide and
the Field Sheet at intended use size. Inspect the final PDFs after navigation is added.

Judge whitespace by purpose. A quiet opener can be deliberate. A detached tail, arbitrary
blank page, repetitive box pattern, or wall of dense tables needs composition work.
There is no minimum ink coverage, photo percentage, corpus ratio, or universal page count.

## Issue severity

- **Critical:** may cause denied access, serious safety error, major wasted expense, or an
  unusable core instruction. Fix or explicitly remove the unsupported recommendation.
- **Major:** contradicting advice, unsupported decisive claim, missing core Sheet value,
  wrong comparison, unreadable essential text, lost text, or broken core navigation.
- **Minor:** local polish that does not change use (for example an imperfect decorative
  crop). Fix when practical; an accepted minor issue needs a concrete explanation.
- **Preference:** a defensible alternative editorial/design choice, not a defect.

Only an evidence-based review can judge severity. Automated output is evidence to inspect,
not an independent editor or a guarantee of factual completeness.

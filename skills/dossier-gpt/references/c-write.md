## Voice

Default to natural English for American travelers unless the project or user specifies
otherwise. Be clear, specific and warm. Explain why a choice matters without making every
paragraph an argument against an imagined tourist. Use local detail and a sense of place
where they improve the reading experience; neither enthusiasm nor restraint needs a quota.

Distinguish an official rule, a documented observation, an estimate and an editorial
preference in the wording itself. A symbol may help orientation, but never supplies the
missing evidence. Do not claim first-hand experience, a survey or a tested itinerary that
did not happen. Avoid absolute judgments about restaurants, neighborhoods, crowds or risk
when the evidence supports only a useful heuristic or a conditional recommendation.

Let the guide demonstrate its usefulness. Remove repeated claims that a book can do what
websites cannot, that every other guide is wrong, or that the reader is being exploited.
Use the agreed title without making its metaphor a factual explanation of every topic.

## Structure for preparation and use

Choose and combine sections to fit the scope. Useful components include:

- A brief opening that states the audience, coverage and practical result.
- A working contents page and a quick-start view of the main choices.
- Orientation and comparisons before the bookings they affect.
- Pre-departure preparation and arrival instructions within the promised scope.
- A few developed decisions, with short rules or checklists where sufficient.
- Sample plans with explicit trip assumptions and realistic fallback options.
- A concise carry reference, sources and dated Field Sheet.

There is no required number of chapters, decisions, photos or pages. A chapter needs a
clear contribution; it need not repeat the book's thesis. Keep an itinerary if it is useful
and label its constraints honestly. Do not present five full sightseeing days and five
calendar days including arrival as interchangeable examples.

## Prose and repetition

Start a section with its answer or a relevant situation. Keep headings informative and
paragraphs connected. Give a factual instruction before the supporting explanation when
the reader is likely to need it quickly. Use lists and tables for genuinely parallel
information. A pull quote should add emphasis without repeating nearby text mechanically.

Explain a rule fully in its main section, then shorten it for a checklist or card while
preserving its important conditions. Search repeated recommendations as a set; a local
edit is incomplete when an old instruction remains in a caption or summary. Repetition
counts and vocabulary checks can reveal habits, but do not use numerical word-frequency
limits as editorial quality gates.

## The Book and Field Sheet

Give both documents an explicit issue or revision date. Give current inputs the check
date, scope and source required by the evidence contract. A year alone does not establish
that a volatile fact has recently been checked.

1. Supply every value or status the Book promises on the Sheet. Link it to the relevant
   decision and canonical evidence entry.
2. Put a dated worked example beside a calculation when it establishes the recommendation.
   Show the assumptions, units, inclusions and formula so the reader can update it.
3. If a value cannot be verified, name the specific source and unresolved input, explain
   how it affects the choice, and narrow the recommendation. A generic "check current"
   is a research task, not a delivered figure.
4. Distinguish information to check before committing money from information to check
   again shortly before travel or on the day. A late check cannot repair every booking.
5. Treat changing access, validity, hours and procedures as well as changing prices.
   Update the Book's affected instructions when a rule changes; the Sheet is not a
   substitute for correcting contradictory prose.
6. Promise an update frequency or ongoing service only when the user has established it
   and the delivery mechanism exists. Keep uncompleted checks and current limitations
   visible without claiming that the whole package has been verified.

Use reader-facing source names and usable links or notes, not just symbols or internal
IDs. Give observations their real provenance and dates; relabel unsupported generalizations
as a bounded preference or remove them.

## A carry reference must work at its advertised size

Choose a phone reference, printable sheet or pocket card according to the intended use.
For a print card, state the cut or fold size and instruct printing at 100%. Verify the
actual type size and physical fit. One workable pocket format is two 3.25in by 4.5in panels,
folded between them; it is a design option, not a mandatory template or wallet size.

Keep text readable at final size. Reject clipping and overflow rather than hiding them.
Reduce content or add another clearly labeled card if necessary. Do not call a full-width
page "wallet-sized" without verifying the printed result. Retain the conditions that
prevent a short instruction from becoming wrong.

## Scope and final editing

Explain material scope limits briefly and neutrally. Named hotels or restaurants may be
included when requested or useful and sufficiently supported. Their omission is also
acceptable when it matches the product's purpose. Do not argue that all named selections
are inherently unreliable or that general rules never expire.

Finish by checking the reading sequence, the most consequential instructions and the
specific promises on the cover and sales copy. Inspect rendered pages and the carry
reference at intended use size. Mechanical checks support this review; passing them does
not prove factual reliability or value for money.

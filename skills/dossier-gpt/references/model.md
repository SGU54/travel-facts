# The product model

Read this before selecting content. Build a guide that makes a real trip easier to
prepare and enjoy: accurate information, useful choices, clear actions, appropriate
exceptions, and pleasant reading. Use references/evidence-contract.md for the shared
evidence and consistency requirements.

## What the reader pays for

Earn the reader's time and money through dependable selection, explanation and practical
use. Connecting facts from several articles can be valuable; so can a well-chosen basic
rule, an honest comparison, a good route, or a checklist that saves another search.
Include something because it serves the intended reader, even if it is available free.

Do not require every item to be surprising and high stakes. That pressure encourages
invented traps, inflated consequences and unnecessary complexity. Give essential basics
enough space to be used correctly. Give difficult choices enough reasoning to let a reader
choose differently. Keep low-impact details when they improve orientation or enjoyment;
remove them when they merely lengthen the book.

Assess value against the actual audience, scope, price and delivered package. Neither page
count nor article count proves value. Do not infer willingness to pay, savings, uniqueness,
sales potential or reader satisfaction from the existence of a polished PDF.

## The editorial standard

For each section, ask:

- **Accurate:** Can its important claims be traced to suitable evidence, with the date,
  conditions and limitations that affect the advice?
- **Useful:** What will the reader understand, choose or do after reading it?
- **Fair:** Are alternatives compared on the same terms, and can a legitimate preference
  lead to a different recommendation?
- **Executable:** Are the necessary steps, inputs and fallback available where needed?
- **Coherent:** Do the main text, figure, example, summary and Field Sheet state the same
  rule, including consequential exceptions?
- **Readable:** Can the reader find the answer quickly and enjoy the explanation without
  repeated sales arguments or inflated authority?

These questions guide editorial judgment. Word ratios, keyword counts, ink coverage,
co-occurrence scores and fixed numbers of chapters are diagnostics, never proof of quality
or independent reasons to block a useful guide. Mechanical checks can find omissions and
contradictions; they cannot establish truth, good taste or commercial demand.

## Evidence before confidence

Use the site's corpus to understand its voice, topics, existing coverage and useful
connections. Treat its factual claims as research leads, not as automatically verified
evidence. Check consequential rules and figures against current primary sources. Record
source conflicts and material uncertainty rather than hiding them behind confident prose.

Maintain evidence.json as the canonical record for shared rules and values. Provide
readable citations in the publication; a provenance symbol or internal identifier is not
a reader-facing source. When evidence is unavailable, narrow or withhold the dependent
recommendation and explain the specific limitation. Never invent a survey, visit,
interview, observation, photograph's provenance or customer result.

## Give the product a clear shape

Choose a structure from the actual destination and traveler's decisions. A geographic
constraint or booking dependency may provide a useful through-line. Several related
principles are also acceptable; do not manufacture a single hidden mechanism for the
whole destination. Public taxes, official fares, private fees and personal preferences
need their own explanations.

Keep the Book and Field Sheet complementary. The Book explains choices and includes
dated examples where they help. The Sheet supplies changing inputs, current status and
specific recheck instructions. Access, eligibility, schedules and booking procedures can
change as well as prices; neither document is inherently reliable for a fixed number of
years. A change to a rule must reach every place that depends on it.

Offer a representative sample when a storefront or preview is part of the task. Let it
demonstrate the finished guide's usefulness and reading experience. Avoid claims that a
book alone can organize information, that all free advice is useless, or that hypothetical
traveler mistakes have been observed. Match the cover and sales promises to what the
finished package actually contains.

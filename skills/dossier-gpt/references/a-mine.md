# Stage A — Review the corpus and research the decisions

Read references/model.md and references/evidence-contract.md. Start from the project
state and the user's scope. The corpus is editorial material; current primary sources
establish consequential facts.

## Inventory the material

Identify article titles, URLs or stable IDs, topics, dates and recurring reader questions.
Note duplicate passages, conflicting claims and gaps. Counts can help navigate a large
export, but do not convert articles or words into an expected number of pages.

Use the corpus to recover the site's voice and existing coverage. Inspect any editorial
plans or topic hypotheses as proposals to test. Do not treat promotional hooks, repeated
keywords or numerous articles making the same claim as independent evidence.

## Build a shortlist around reader use

For each candidate section, record:

| Item | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Reader need | A question, task or useful piece of orientation |
| Intended result | What the reader can choose, do or understand |
| Applicable profile | Trip length, priorities and constraints that change the answer |
| Existing coverage | The specific corpus passages worth using or updating |
| Evidence needed | Facts or values on which the recommendation depends |
| Open issue | Conflict, missing input or condition that could reverse the advice |

A simple, well-supported rule can earn a short checklist item. A genuine comparison may
earn a full section. Do not require a minimum word count, source count or level of surprise
for either. Combine overlapping questions and avoid repeating the same explanation in
several chapters.

## Verify what changes the recommendation

Prioritize research by consequence and uncertainty. Check access, booking windows,
transport validity, entry requirements, fees, legal status and practical route constraints
with the relevant authorities or operators. Use secondary reporting to discover issues
and context; distinguish its interpretation from the underlying primary evidence.

For each important claim, record the supporting source and applicable date and scope in
evidence.json, following the evidence contract. Keep an article-to-section manifest for
editorial traceability, not as a coverage quota. Several articles repeating one source are
one evidential chain, not several confirmations.

When official pages conflict, record both, look for an effective date or specific current
notice, and qualify the advice if the conflict remains unresolved. If a decisive source is
unavailable, continue independent work and narrow the affected recommendation. An
unverified route cannot become an assured fallback merely because it appears in the
corpus. Do not transfer destination research to the user when available research tools can
resolve it.

## Check coverage before expanding

Read the shortlist as a traveler preparing and taking the trip. Look for missing steps
between the advice and the action: a title's eligibility, the right station, a ticket's
release state, or the method for checking a listing. Research gaps that affect the scope
promised to the buyer. Drop or clearly bound unsupported sections rather than padding
them with general prose.

audit-depth.py and audit-synthesis.py, if used, provide optional descriptive signals.
Their ratios and co-occurrence results do not decide whether a dossier is viable, useful
or worth its price. A short, accurate guide may be complete; a large corpus may still
lack dependable evidence for a particular claim.

## Output

Produce a concise coverage plan, source manifest, decision shortlist and prioritized open
issues. Estimate length from the selected material and usable layout only when useful
for production. Separate what is ready, what needs verification and what lies outside the
agreed scope. Proceed when the evidence supports a concrete useful draft; do not wait for
an arbitrary article or page threshold.

# A cover the reader can understand

Use the existing title and destination identity unless the user requests a change.
The cover should communicate the product, its intended reader, and a credible benefit.
Its job is to invite reading; detailed analytical proof belongs inside.

Choose one main visual device: a relevant photograph, a simplified spatial motif,
a restrained diagram, or strong typography. No approach is inherently premium or
“unclonable.” A good typographic cover may have substantial whitespace.

Keep the title readable at 180–240px width, with a shorter supporting promise. Avoid
tiny map labels, full comparison tables, competing taglines, and exaggerated savings
claims. If a simplified map is not to scale, do not imply it is a navigation map.
At most a few short meaningful diagram labels are likely to survive thumbnail size.

Show an honest subtitle. “Seven decisions scored” requires seven actual scored decisions
and an explained method; “the arithmetic” requires the relevant calculations inside.
Do not make the cover promise unsupported exclusivity, guaranteed savings, or a complete
trip plan that the product does not contain.

Build it with the same brand tokens as the interior. Check contrast, real fonts,
composition, edition/date placement, and full bleed or deliberate inset in the PDF.
Judge it both at thumbnail and final size. There is no minimum ink-percentage test.

# Stage A2 — Choose the structure and title

Choose the guide's shape from the destination, audience and verified practical constraints.
Use references/model.md and the Stage A coverage plan. Reuse design components and
editorial methods across projects; research each destination's actual choices.

## Find the useful through-line

Identify the factors that organize this trip: the distribution of places, transport
connections, booking dependencies, season, terrain or competing traveler priorities.
Consider more than one structure before selecting the clearest. A single organizing fact
is optional. Do not inflate a few unrelated charges into a hidden citywide mechanism or
assume that the corpus's most frequent keyword identifies the destination's main problem.

| Pattern to investigate | Questions it may organize | Useful visual, when supported |
|---|---|---|
| Several places within walking reach | Where to stay; when a ride helps | Locator with stated route or distance basis |
| Dispersed city destinations | Base and transport trade-offs | Transit relationships and interchange points |
| Several islands, regions or cities | Sequence, connection margins, trip length | Route options with dated operating conditions |
| One base versus moving accommodation | Time, luggage, access and cost | Comparable journeys from candidate bases |
| A corridor or circuit | Direction, commitment points, recovery options | Route and alternatives |

These are hypotheses, not geographic facts about any named destination. Verify scale,
terrain, route availability and travel-time boundaries before using them in prose or art.
Use maps for the relationship they can honestly explain; a locator is not automatically a
navigation map.

## Define the audience without stereotypes

Default to natural English for American travelers when the project is a foramericans
guide; honor the user's audience and language choices. State the actual assumptions that
matter: first or repeat visit, travel dates, adults and children, interests, mobility,
budget and willingness to change accommodation.

Check nationality, passport, residence, payment-card and driving conditions independently
when they affect a rule. Do not assume that every US resident has a US passport, that all
Americans tip alike, or that a currency-conversion issue disappears for another audience.
Use preferences as legitimate reasons to choose differently, not as mistakes to correct.

For sample plans, define calendar days, full sightseeing days, nights and arrival/departure
time explicitly. Keep the same counting convention throughout each named example.

## Set an honest scope

Select the decisions and short practical sections the reader needs. Combine recurring
topics and leave out standard chapters that do not help this destination or audience.
An itinerary, named selection or short factual checklist is acceptable when it fulfills
the agreed purpose; it does not need to be converted into a contrarian decision.

Choose the cover device after the content structure. Its promise must describe the guide's
real help without invented savings, crisis scenarios or claims of exclusive knowledge.

## Stage A3 — Name the guide and continue

Preserve an existing or user-supplied title unless the user asks for a change. If no title
exists, choose a clear working title grounded in the scope and continue the reversible
draft. Consider alternatives internally; show them when a preference would materially
improve the result. Do not create a mandatory naming approval step.

Ask only when an unresolved destination, audience or scope choice materially changes the
work and cannot reasonably be inferred. Keep live storefront changes within the user's
authorization. When a title or promise changes, update the cover, metadata, references and
any authorized sales material together.

## Output

Record the audience assumptions, selected structure, section list, useful visuals, title
and material scope limits in project state. Identify the conditions that would change the
structure. Move to drafting when these choices are clear enough to review in a real sample.

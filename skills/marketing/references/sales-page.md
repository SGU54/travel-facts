# M3 — The /guide sales page: the copy architecture

The biggest single piece of persuasion on the site. Every surface funnels here; if
this page doesn't convert, nothing upstream matters. It is a page OF the site (its
typography, its buttons — see adaptation.md), arguing the guide's case in order.

## The structure, top to bottom

1. **Hero: the cover + the thesis.** The real cover image, large — it's a designed
   artifact and the strongest proof on the page. Beside it: the name, and the thesis
   as the hook (three-part test applies here too — the hero thesis is the page's
   master hook, not a subtitle). One buy CTA (price visible).

2. **The stake — why winging it goes wrong.** Two or three of the guide's failure
   modes, stated as concrete images (never a bulleted dump of all twenty). This is
   the "wrong move" section: the reader recognizes themselves.

3. **What's inside — REAL numbers from the build.** "10 decisions · 20 failure
   modes · 9 figures drawn from real coordinates." Never invented, never rounded up
   (dossier non-negotiable #1 reaches here). Name 3-4 decisions by their question
   ("Which islands, in which order — and which boat home"), not chapter titles.

4. **The provenance story — the credibility section.** The marks (established /
   official / surveyed / volatile) explained in two sentences: every claim in the
   book carries one; no mark = it was cut. This is the moat; sell it as such.

5. **What the guide refuses to do — the brand.** No hotel names (we haven't slept in
   them), no restaurant lists (wrong by spring), no printed prices (they live on the
   Field Sheet). Stated proudly. Refusals convert skeptics better than promises.

6. **The Field Sheet promise.** Dated, quarterly, carries the volatile figures. This
   answers "won't a PDF go stale?" — the one objection every buyer has. One short
   section.

7. **Close: cover thumb + one line + the second buy CTA.** Two buy CTAs on the whole
   page (hero + close) — never between every section.

## Voice rules

- The site's editorial voice, not landing-page voice. No superlatives ("ultimate",
  "essential"), no urgency theater, no testimonials invented from nothing.
- Every claim about the guide traces to the shipped build. If the build has 9
  figures, the page says 9.
- Price framing: state it plainly next to what it buys ("$34 — the book + the
  current Field Sheet"). Anchor against the trip, not against other PDFs, and do it
  once, lightly ("less than one mis-booked ferry leg"), never repeated.
- The page never disparages the free articles — they're the proof of expertise. The
  line is: the articles answer the questions; the guide joins them into the sequence.

## Definition of done

All seven sections present, in order; numbers verified against the build; two CTAs
exactly; hero thesis passes the three-part test; the page passes the not-an-ad
screenshot test in this site's identity; metadata (title/description) built from
name + thesis.

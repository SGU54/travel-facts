# M1 — Hooks: the lines that make a reader click

A hook is the one line a surface leads with. The dossier's A3 step mints RAW hooks
(accurate, flat); this stage makes them PULL. One weak hook wastes a whole surface.

## Why flat hooks fail

The reference failure, from Greece's ferry-day swamp:

> "The wrong ferry the day before your flight loses the whole trip. Here's the sequence."

Three diagnoses — learn them, they generalize:
1. **"Here's the sequence" is a shrug.** It describes the contents instead of creating
   the need to see them. The reader thinks "okay" and scrolls.
2. **The stake is abstract.** "Loses the whole trip" is a category, not an image. The
   reader never sees themselves at the port.
3. **No open loop.** The line closes its own loop (risk → fix) in one breath. Nothing
   pulls forward. Persuasion works by opening a loop the CLICK closes.

## The three-part test (every hook must pass all three)

1. **Name the reader's specific wrong move** — the thing they are actually about to
   do. Not "a mistake"; "book the fast boat home."
2. **Deliver the stake as a concrete image** — not a category. Not "loses the trip";
   "stranded at the port," "a missed international flight."
3. **Open a loop the click closes** — a twist or question the line does NOT resolve.
   "The mistake starts three islands earlier." "Did you get it right?"

**The guardrail:** the tension must be REAL (a true swamp; an expert would nod) and
NEVER manufactured — no false urgency, no countdowns, no fear theater. Strong comes
from specificity + the open loop, never from volume. A hyped line fails the Trust
floor exactly like a hard-sell article turn does.

## Worked rewrites (the ferry-day swamp — keep as calibration)

**A — concrete image + open loop** (best for the END BAND: room for two beats)
> "Most first-timers book the fast boat home. It's the first one the wind cancels —
> and the mistake starts three islands earlier."

**B — you're-about-to-do-this**
> "You'll want the quick catamaran for the last leg home. That instinct is exactly
> what strands people at the port."

**C — stakes made vivid** (band alternative)
> "A cancelled ferry the morning of your flight home isn't a delay — it's a missed
> international flight. The sequence that prevents it starts on day one."

**D — the question hook** (best for the ASIDE: shortest, self-doubt-driven)
> "Which boat you take home was decided three islands ago. Did you get it right?"

## Per-surface calibration

| surface | shape | why |
|---|---|---|
| sticky aside | shortest; a question hook (D-style) | glanceable — must punch in one look |
| end band | image + twist (A/C-style), one more beat allowed | the reader finished; room to land the stake |
| inline marker | connective reveal ("one knotted decision") + benefit CTA | it sits mid-argument; it deepens, doesn't interrupt |
| category / tool callout | that context's door, B-style | the reader self-selected the theme |

**CTAs are benefits, not titles.** "Charted in {name}." is weaker than "See the
sequence that gets you home. →". The name appears once nearby; the CTA sells the
outcome.

## Process

1. Pull the swamp's decision + failure modes from the dossier build (they contain the
   wrong move and the real stake — never invent either).
2. Draft through the three-part test. If a part is missing, the line isn't done.
3. Check the guardrail: would an expert nod at the stake as stated? Any urgency
   theater? Cut it.
4. Calibrate per surface (table above) — one swamp usually yields 2-3 variants.
5. Ship as the `hooks` values in 5c's guide config. Data judges later; never mark a
   line proven.

# ROME, SOLVED — DECISION ARCHITECTURE (Stage B, locked)

## Organising fact (from corpus, A2)
The first-timer core is a ~30-min walk end to end; the metro deliberately skirts it;
the walking IS the sightseeing. Shape: compact city. Dominant edge (counted): EXPERIENCE
(owns 49/96 arts) > MONEY (34) > BREAKAGE(7) > EXTRACTION(6). Cover subtitle leads on
taste/timing, NOT savings.

## THE BAR: each decision must be NON-OBVIOUS (a synthesis no single free article makes)
## AND HIGH-STAKES (wrong = real money / lost day / the trip). Individual calls stated
## flatly in one article are OBVIOUS by construction — the paid value is the CROSS join.

## KEPT DECISIONS (each names the cross-article synthesis + the stake)

01 BASE — free hook. "Sleep inside the 30-min core; a metro address is a downgrade."
   Synthesis: walking-guide (core=30min, metro skirts centre) × stay-guides (Termini=cheapest,
   best transport) × pass-math. NO article joins these: the very thing that makes Termini
   "best transport" (the metro hub) is worthless when the sights the metro can't reach are a
   walk away. Staying central to SAVE the €30 transport is backwards — you pay it in the
   walkability you gave up. STAKE: every evening of the trip; the single most consequential booking.

02 TRANSIT+PASS (merged) — "The Roma Pass math is already decided by Decision 01."
   Synthesis: pass-guide says "run the transport math" but computes it in a vacuum. Walking-guide
   says core=walkable. JOIN: if you took Decision 01, your real transport spend ≈ airport + a
   handful of taps that auto-cap — the transport leg of the pass math is ~zero, which retroactively
   kills the pass for the walk-everywhere first-timer. The pass sells itself on a commute you won't make.
   STAKE: ~€30-55 pass bought "to be safe" + the myth that you need transit tickets at all.

03 ARRIVAL — "The train-vs-taxi answer flips on WHERE you sleep, not on price."
   Synthesis: airport-guide gives Leonardo €14 vs taxi €55 as a standalone money call. Stay-guide
   gives your neighbourhood. JOIN: Leonardo only wins if you sleep near Termini/a metro line —
   exactly the address Decision 01 tells you NOT to book. For a central base, €14 train + €10-15
   onward taxi/luggage-drag ≈ the €55 fixed taxi door-to-door, jet-lagged, day one. The "cheap"
   option is only cheap for the wrong hotel. STAKE: the most anxious hours of the trip; ~€40 + a bad start.

04 THE ANCIENT HALF-DAY — "One 24h ticket = one morning, and it forbids a second sight."
   Synthesis: combined-ticket article says Colosseum+Forum+Palatine share a 24h ticket AND warns
   "don't pair with the Vatican." Stay/day-plan articles pick a base. JOIN: the cluster is 3-4h on
   unshaded stone; stacking the Vatican = a death march — and WHICH of the two unavoidable half-days
   is on your doorstep vs across the river is set by Decision 01. Base decides sequence. STAKE: a
   ruined day + the trip's two biggest sights done badly.

05 THE VATICAN QUEUE — "The wait you can buy away and the wait you can't are different lines."
   Synthesis: skip-the-line article (ticket line vs security line) × st-peters-free × dome/basilica
   articles. JOIN: a timed ticket skips the TICKET line (a reseller markup skips nothing more);
   St Peter's is FREE so there's no ticket to skip — its wait is SECURITY, beaten only by the
   museum-passage exit or going early/late. Buyers pay tour prices expecting to skip a line that's
   either free-to-skip-by-timing or unskippable-at-any-price. STAKE: ~€40-80 in phantom "skip" fees.

06 BOOKING HORIZON — "What sells out, how far ahead — as one calendar, not four tabs."
   Synthesis: Borghese (mandatory timed, no walk-ins) + Vatican (sells out weeks) + Colosseum
   Underground (weeks, guided-only) + Pantheon (now timed) each stated in its own article. NO
   article assembles the HORIZONS into one bookable sequence. JOIN: the book's calendar figure.
   STAKE: the single highest-impact miss — the sight you flew for, sold out.

07 MONEY / DCC — American-specific. "One card-reader prompt, answered wrong all trip."
   Synthesis: cash-cards article (choose euros) + tipping article (light, coperto≠tip) + the
   mistakes article naming both as top American errors. JOIN: the wallet card. This is the audience
   half of the shape — a British edition wouldn't carry DCC. STAKE: ~3% skimmed on every charge +
   the reflexive 20% tip that doubles a light bill. Real but per-transaction, so it's ONE decision, not three.

08 THE EATING FILTER — "Ninety seconds' walk from the monument is a different bill."
   Synthesis: tourist-trap article (red/green flags) × the compact-core fact × neighbourhood food
   articles. JOIN: the trap density is a FUNCTION of the walk-time thesis — prime sight-facing real
   estate pays with location not cooking, and the core is small enough that 90 seconds' walk clears
   it. The rule (not a restaurant list) outlives any season. STAKE: taste — the difference between
   a great trip and a fine one, the dominant edge.

09 THE FOURTH DAY / DAY TRIPS — "Ostia beats Pompeii for a short trip — 30 min vs 12 hours."
   Synthesis: ostia article (~30 min on a metro ticket, 'Rome's Pompeii') vs pompeii article
   (~2h each way, 12h door-to-door, 'don't pair with anything'). Each argues its own trip; NO article
   puts the two ancient-port options side by side against a first-timer's actual day budget. The
   famous name (Pompeii) is the wrong default when time is the constraint. STAKE: a whole day + the
   4th day's entire value.

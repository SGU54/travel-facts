# Rome, Solved. — five art directors on two chapters (2026-10-25)

The casting test for Rome: two decisions of the shipped book composed in each of the
five directors' hands, in La Tavola's palette (wax red, plate gilt, foxed paper,
iron-gall ink, sepia). Each director keeps their own typefaces; the site gives the
colours. The owner judges, then one director recomposes the whole book.

- **Decision 01 · Where should you actually sleep?** — a map decision (the walkable
  core, Termini's 35-minute walk to scale; five bases scored by walk-time)
- **Decision 05 · What does "skip the line" actually skip at the Vatican?** — a
  photograph decision (two queues; the ledger of four sights and their real wait)

**Where the words come from.** No source for the shipped 64-page edition exists on this
machine (the July tarballs are 40–47 pages), so the two chapters were transcribed from
`_etsy/rome/files/rome-solved.pdf`, the photographs extracted from it, and FIG. 3 (the
walkable core) redrawn from the shipped figure's label coordinates. Nothing was
rewritten; the poster glosses and the figure captions are the directors' only lines.

**What Rome taught the hands.** Its paragraphs run 500–900 characters, two to three
times Barcelona's. Every two-page hand needed a lever: the Affiche takes four pages
per decision (poster, map with a lede, text, photograph), the Édition adds a closing
page (band, the rest of the prose, the forks), the Manuel gives its callout plates a
page, the Mosaïque grew its rows. Only the Atlas held at two pages. A whole-book
recompose in any hand will run longer than the shipped 64 pages.

----- 5-day-greece-itinerary (category: itineraries) -----
TITLE: 5 Days in Greece: A Short First Trip That Works
CONTENT:
<p>Five days in Greece is tight, but it's enough for a genuinely great first taste if you resist the urge to cram. The winning move on a short trip is restraint: pick Athens plus one island, or skip Athens and give a single island the whole stretch. This itinerary lays out the best way to spend five days in Greece for a first-timer — what to prioritize, what to skip, and how to end the trip without a stressful dash for your flight home.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: program=getyourguide context="primary CTA — Acropolis tickets, island day tours, wine tasting" -->
<p class="affiliate-disclosure"><em>This article contains affiliate links. If you book through them we earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.</em></p>

<h2>The big decision: one island, not three</h2>

<p>With five days, the single most important choice is to <strong>not island-hop</strong>. Each island change burns most of a day in packing, ports, and transfers, so a five-day, multi-island trip turns into a blur of logistics. The two formats that actually work: <strong>Athens (2 nights) + one island (2–3 nights)</strong>, or <strong>one island for the whole trip</strong> if you'd rather go straight to the beach. Both leave you relaxed instead of frazzled.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/5-day-greece-itinerary-1" alt="A bright, aspirational shot blending Athens and a Cycladic island" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Option A: Athens + Santorini (the classic)</h2>

<p>The most popular five-day first trip pairs the capital with the showstopper.</p>

<h3>Days 1–2: Athens</h3>
<p>Land at Athens International (ATH), base in <strong>Plaka</strong>, and spend two days on the essentials: the <strong>Acropolis</strong> and <strong>Acropolis Museum</strong>, the <strong>Ancient Agora</strong>, the old-town lanes, and a rooftop dinner with an Acropolis view. Day two can add the National Archaeological Museum or a relaxed wander.</p>

<h3>Days 3–5: Santorini</h3>
<p>Take the <strong>45-minute flight</strong> from Athens to Santorini (the ferry eats too much of a short trip). Base on the caldera in <strong>Fira</strong> or <strong>Imerovigli</strong>, watch the <strong>Oia sunset</strong>, walk the caldera trail, and fit in a winery or the Akrotiri ruins. On day five, fly back to Athens to connect to your flight home.</p>

<h2>Option B: Athens + a Cycladic island</h2>

<p>Prefer somewhere more relaxed and better value than Santorini? Swap in <strong>Naxos</strong> or <strong>Paros</strong> for days 3–5 — gorgeous beaches, real villages, and a calmer pace, reached by a short ferry or flight from Athens. It's the move for travelers who want to unwind rather than chase the famous icon.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/5-day-greece-itinerary-2" alt="A relaxed Cycladic island scene — quiet beach or whitewashed village lane" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Option C: skip Athens, do one island</h2>

<p>If you've seen Athens before, or you simply want a beach-and-village reset, skip the capital and give a single island all five days. <strong>Naxos</strong> or <strong>Crete</strong> are ideal for this — both big enough to fill the time without moving hotels, with beaches, villages, and day trips. Fly in and out via Athens (there are no direct U.S. flights to the islands), and enjoy not packing for five days.</p>

<h2>Getting home from a short trip</h2>

<p>The key logistical rule on a five-day trip: <strong>don't schedule a long ferry or an island hop on your departure day</strong>. Because there are no direct flights to the U.S. from the islands, you'll route home through Athens — so end on an island with a quick flight back to Athens (Santorini, or any island with an airport), and leave a comfortable buffer before your transatlantic departure. Booking that final island-to-Athens flight ahead is smart, as it fills up in season.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: program=booking context="hotels for a 5-day trip — Athens + one island" -->

<h2>What to skip on five days</h2>

<p>Be ruthless about what doesn't fit. Skip multi-island hopping, skip trying to add Crete <em>and</em> the Cyclades, and skip long day trips that eat into limited island time. Five days rewards depth — really seeing one or two places — over a checklist. The islands aren't going anywhere; a focused first trip is the best argument for a longer second one.</p>

<h2>When to take a short Greece trip</h2>
<p>A five-day trip is short enough that timing it well really pays off. <strong>Shoulder season</strong> — May, June, September, early October — is ideal: warm and swimmable, smaller crowds (which matters more when every hour counts), and lower prices. In <strong>peak summer</strong>, the heat and the cruise-ship crowds in places like Santorini eat into a tight schedule, and ferries and flights fill up, so book ahead. <strong>Winter and early spring</strong> can work for an Athens-focused short trip, but many island hotels and restaurants close from roughly November to March, so a five-day island trip in that window is harder. For most first-timers fitting Greece into a week off, the shoulder months line up the weather, the crowds, and the prices in your favor — exactly what you want when you don't have days to spare.</p>

----- 14-day-greece-itinerary (category: itineraries) -----
TITLE: 14 Days in Greece: The Unhurried First Trip
CONTENT:
<p>Two weeks is the dream length for a first trip to Greece — enough to see Athens, settle into several islands, and even add Crete or a slower pace without ever feeling rushed. The luxury of 14 days is that you can travel the way the islands are meant to be experienced: unhurried, with time to have a favorite beach and a regular taverna. This itinerary lays out an ideal two-week first trip, plus how to flex it to your interests.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: program=getyourguide context="primary CTA — Acropolis, island tours, wine, day trips across a 2-week trip" -->
<p class="affiliate-disclosure"><em>This article contains affiliate links. If you book through them we earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.</em></p>

<h2>The shape of two weeks</h2>

<p>Fourteen days comfortably fits <strong>Athens plus three islands</strong> at a relaxed pace, or Athens plus two islands and Crete. The structure that works best: start in Athens, move through the Cyclades from relaxed to spectacular, and either finish on the showstopper or fold in Crete for variety. Keep ferry legs short by moving between neighbors, and use a flight for any long jump (like to or from Crete).</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/14-day-greece-itinerary-1" alt="A sweeping Greece collage feel — Acropolis, a ferry, a caldera island" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Days 1–3: Athens</h2>

<p>Ease in with three days in the capital. Cover the <strong>Acropolis</strong>, <strong>Acropolis Museum</strong>, and <strong>Ancient Agora</strong>; wander <strong>Plaka</strong> and the neighborhoods; eat well and catch a rooftop sunset. With a third day, take a day trip — <strong>Cape Sounion</strong>'s sunset temple or <strong>Delphi</strong> — before heading to the islands.</p>

<h2>Days 4–6: Naxos</h2>

<p>Ferry from Piraeus to <strong>Naxos</strong>, the relaxed, authentic heart of the Cyclades. Long sandy beaches, the marble <strong>Portara</strong> at sunset, mountain villages, and great food at gentle prices. Three nights lets you slow down after the city.</p>

<h2>Days 7–9: Paros</h2>

<p>A short hop to neighboring <strong>Paros</strong>. Base in chic <strong>Naoussa</strong> for its harbor, lanes, and dining, or in <strong>Parikia</strong> for value and connections. Beaches, a day trip to tiny <strong>Antiparos</strong>, and a relaxed island rhythm fill three more days.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/14-day-greece-itinerary-2" alt="Naoussa harbor on Paros at dusk, fishing boats and waterfront tavernas" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Days 10–13: Santorini or Crete</h2>

<p>Now choose your finale. <strong>Santorini</strong> (a ferry south) delivers the iconic caldera, sunsets, wineries, and Akrotiri across four nights — the classic spectacular ending. Or, for a different flavor, fly to <strong>Crete</strong> for mountains, the Minoan palace of <strong>Knossos</strong>, the beautiful town of Chania, and food worth the trip on its own. Crete is big, so a rental car helps; either choice rewards the extra time two weeks allows.</p>

<h2>Day 14: Fly home via Athens</h2>

<p>On your final day, fly from your last island back to <strong>Athens</strong> to connect to your flight home — there are no direct flights to the U.S. from the islands, so you'll route through Athens regardless. A short morning flight beats spending your last day on a long ferry. Book this connection ahead, and leave a comfortable buffer before your transatlantic departure.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: program=booking context="hotels across Athens + 3 islands for a 2-week trip" -->

<h2>Ways to flex two weeks</h2>

<p>This itinerary bends easily to your style. <strong>Beach and relaxation focus:</strong> drop one Cycladic island and give the others four nights each. <strong>History focus:</strong> add a day trip to Delphi or the Argolis (Mycenae, Epidaurus) from Athens, and make Crete the finale for Knossos. <strong>Nightlife and glamour:</strong> swap Paros for <strong>Mykonos</strong>. <strong>Slow travel:</strong> do just Athens plus two islands across the full two weeks, with four-to-five-night stays — the most relaxing version of all. Whatever you choose, two weeks means you don't have to rush, so lean into that.</p>

<h2>Pacing tips for a longer trip</h2>

<p>Even with two weeks, a few rules keep it smooth: still move between <strong>neighboring islands</strong> to keep ferries short; <strong>fly the long legs</strong> (to or from Crete) rather than taking marathon ferries; build in a <strong>rest day</strong> or two with nothing planned; and never schedule an island hop on your final day. Because ferry and hotel prices shift with season and demand, check current rates as you book rather than relying on a fixed figure, and reserve popular Santorini stays well ahead.</p>

<h2>Budgeting for two weeks</h2>
<p>A two-week trip is where smart island choices really move the total. TITLE: Athens and Santorini in One Week
CONTENT:
<p>If there are two places you picture when you think of Greece, they're probably the Acropolis and the Santorini caldera — and a week is the perfect amount of time to do both, properly, without a single rushed ferry hop. The Athens-and-Santorini week is the most popular first-timer trip to Greece for good reason: it pairs the country's greatest ancient city with its most spectacular island, connected by a quick flight. This guide lays out exactly how to spend that week.</p>

<h2>Why this combo works so well</h2>

<p>Two destinations in a week means <strong>one move, not three</strong> — so you spend your time experiencing places rather than packing and waiting at ports. Athens and Santorini are also a study in contrast: 2,500 years of history and a buzzing capital, then a serene volcanic island and the world's most famous sunset. And the link between them is easy — a <strong>45-minute flight</strong> rather than a long ferry. It's the lowest-stress way to see the two icons of Greece on limited time.</p>

<h2>Days 1–3: Athens</h2>

<p>Land at Athens International (ATH) and base in <strong>Plaka</strong>, walkable to everything.</p>

<p><strong>Day 1:</strong> Ease off the flight — wander Plaka and Anafiotika, and have your first long taverna dinner.</p>
<p><strong>Day 2:</strong> The headliners — the <strong>Acropolis</strong> (go at opening), the <strong>Acropolis Museum</strong>, and the <strong>Ancient Agora</strong> — capped with a rooftop sunset facing the lit-up Parthenon.</p>
<p><strong>Day 3:</strong> The <strong>National Archaeological Museum</strong> and the markets of Monastiraki, or a half-day trip to <strong>Cape Sounion</strong> for sunset at the Temple of Poseidon.</p>

<h2>Day 4: Fly to Santorini</h2>

<p>Take a morning <strong>flight to Santorini</strong> (about 45 minutes) so you arrive with the afternoon ahead. Pre-book a transfer up to the caldera — the towns are a steep climb from the airport and port. Settle into your base (Oia or Imerovigli for views, Fira for value and transport), and ease into island time with a first caldera-view dinner.</p>

<h2>Days 5–6: Santorini</h2>

<p><strong>Day 5:</strong> Walk the clifftop <strong>Fira-to-Oia trail</strong> in the morning, relax in the afternoon, and watch the <strong>Oia sunset</strong> (arrive early for a spot, or pick a quieter caldera perch).</p>
<p><strong>Day 6:</strong> Tour the <strong>Akrotiri</strong> ruins and taste <strong>Assyrtiko wine</strong> at a winery, or take a <strong>caldera boat cruise</strong> to the volcano and hot springs — a sunset cruise is a memorable finale.</p>

<h2>Day 7: Fly home via Athens</h2>

<p>On your last day, fly from Santorini back to <strong>Athens</strong> to connect to your flight home — there are no direct flights to the U.S. from the islands, so you route through Athens. A short morning flight leaves your final hours relaxed instead of stuck on a long ferry. Book this connection in advance and leave a comfortable buffer before your transatlantic departure.</p>

<p>This week flexes a little. Want more island time? Trim Athens to two nights and give Santorini four. Prefer a calmer island than Santorini's crowds? This exact structure works with <strong>Naxos</strong> or <strong>Paros</strong> in place of Santorini for a more relaxed, better-value week. Honeymooners might add a sunset catamaran cruise and a caldera-view splurge room. The bones — three nights in Athens, fly to one island, fly home via Athens — stay the same.</p>

<h2>Why not add a second island?</h2>

<p>It's tempting, but on a week-long trip a second island means a second move, which eats a day and adds ferry-cancellation risk before your flight home. The beauty of the Athens-and-Santorini week is its simplicity: two great places, one easy flight between them, and no logistical stress. If you crave two islands in a week, see our seven-day two-island itinerary — but for most first-timers, this lower-stress version is the better trip.</p>

<h2>When to go for this trip</h2>
<p>The Athens-and-Santorini week shines in <strong>shoulder season</strong> — May, June, September, and early October. Athens is comfortable for climbing the Acropolis rather than baking on it, and Santorini's caldera paths and Oia sunset are merely busy instead of overwhelming. <strong>Peak summer</strong> (July–August) brings the most intense crowds and heat to both — the Acropolis at midday and Oia at sunset can be genuinely packed — plus the highest prices and the need to book flights, ferries, and hotels well ahead. <strong>Winter</strong> suits the Athens half nicely (mild, quiet, cheap, empty ancient sites), but Santorini quiets right down, with some hotels and restaurants closed from roughly November to March. For the best balance of weather, crowds, and cost on this particular pairing, the shoulder months are the sweet spot.</p>

----- cyclades-island-hopping-itinerary (category: itineraries) -----
TITLE: The Classic Cyclades Island-Hopping Itinerary
CONTENT:
<p>The Cyclades are the Greek islands of the imagination — whitewashed villages, blue domes, and a scattering of islands close enough to hop between by ferry. For a first-timer who wants the quintessential island-hopping experience, the Cyclades are the place to do it: the connections are frequent, the distances are short, and the islands are different enough to keep every stop fresh. This itinerary lays out a classic Cyclades island-hopping route, with the timing and logistics worked out.</p>

<h2>Why the Cyclades for island hopping</h2>

<p>The Cyclades sit clustered in the central Aegean, linked by frequent ferries, which makes them the easiest group to hop for a first trip. Within a single island group you get short, reliable connections and real variety — the icon (Santorini), the party island (Mykonos), and the laid-back, authentic ones (Naxos, Paros). You stay within one group, the hops stay short, and you end with a quick flight back to Athens.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/cyclades-island-hopping-itinerary-1" alt="A Cycladic island vista — whitewashed village above blue Aegean water" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>The classic route (10 days)</h2>

<p>A proven first-timer Cyclades hop, paced so you're never rushing for a boat:</p>

<h3>Days 1–2: Athens</h3>
<p>Start in the capital for the <strong>Acropolis</strong>, the museums, and the old town before heading to the port at Piraeus. Two nights is enough before the islands.</p>

<h3>Days 3–5: Naxos</h3>
<p>Ferry from Piraeus to <strong>Naxos</strong> — the relaxed, authentic anchor of the trip. Beaches, the marble Portara, mountain villages, and excellent food. The ideal place to ease into island time.</p>

<h3>Days 6–7: Paros</h3>
<p>A short hop to neighboring <strong>Paros</strong>. Chic Naoussa, good beaches, and easy connections. Two nights to enjoy a different island flavor.</p>

<h3>Days 8–10: Santorini</h3>
<p>Ferry south to <strong>Santorini</strong> for the grand finale — the caldera, the Oia sunset, wineries, and Akrotiri across three nights. Then fly back to Athens to connect home.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/cyclades-island-hopping-itinerary-2" alt="A ferry crossing between two Cycladic islands, town on the horizon" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Add Mykonos (for a longer or livelier trip)</h2>

<p>Want nightlife and glamour in the mix? <strong>Mykonos</strong> slots in neatly between Paros and Santorini, or in place of Paros. It adds the beach-club scene, the windmills and Little Venice of Mykonos Town, and a day trip to the ancient ruins of <strong>Delos</strong>. With 12–14 days you can comfortably do Naxos, Paros, Mykonos, and Santorini.</p>

<h2>How the ferries work on this route</h2>

<p>The hops here are all short and well-served, but a few things matter. Book <strong>high-speed routes and peak-summer sailings</strong> ahead, as they sell out; in shoulder season you have more flexibility but thinner schedules, so check the current timetable. Mix <strong>fast catamarans</strong> (quicker, pricier, cancel more in wind) and <strong>conventional ferries</strong> (slower, steadier, cheaper) as suits each leg. The summer <strong>meltemi wind</strong> can disrupt fast boats, so build a buffer — and never schedule a hop on your flight-home day.</p>

<h2>Sequencing and getting home</h2>

<p>The order is deliberate: <strong>relaxed islands first</strong> (Naxos, Paros) to decompress after Athens, the <strong>showstopper last</strong> (Santorini) so the trip builds. End on Santorini because it has an airport — fly the short hop <strong>back to Athens to connect to your flight home</strong>, since there are no direct U.S. flights from the islands. That final flight saves a long ferry day and keeps your departure relaxed; book it ahead and leave a buffer.</p>

<h2>Shorter and longer versions</h2>

<p>Tight on time? A <strong>7-day</strong> version drops to two islands — Naxos (or Paros) plus Santorini, with two nights in Athens. Got two weeks? Add <strong>Mykonos</strong> and give each island an extra night, or fold in a fourth Cycladic island. The principle holds at any length: stay within the Cyclades, move between neighbors, keep hops short, and finish on Santorini for the flight home via Athens.</p>

<h2>What this trip costs</h2>
<p>A Cyclades hop can flex widely depending on which islands you weight and when you go. <strong>Naxos and Paros</strong> are the value anchors — beautiful and far cheaper than the famous pair — so building the trip around them with Santorini as a shorter finale keeps costs reasonable while still delivering the icon. <strong>Ferries</strong> add up across multiple hops, but deck or economy class on the conventional boats is budget-friendly and gives you the deck views anyway. <strong>Shoulder season</strong> drops hotel, ferry, and flight prices together. The pricey stretch is always Santorini, so concentrate any splurge there and keep the other islands sensible. Because ferry and hotel prices shift with route, boat, and season, check current fares when you book rather than relying on a fixed figure, and reserve high-speed summer sailings ahead.</p>

----- best-island-hopping-routes-from-athens (category: sample-ferry-routes) -----
TITLE: The Best Island-Hopping Routes from Athens
CONTENT:
<p>Almost every Greek island trip starts at the same place: the port of Piraeus, just outside Athens, where the ferries fan out across the Aegean. The question is which way to go. Different island groups offer very different trips, and picking the right route from Athens is the foundation of a great first visit. This guide lays out the best island-hopping routes from Athens, what each one is good for, and how to choose between them.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: program=ferryhopper context="primary CTA — search ferry routes from Athens (Piraeus)" -->
<p class="affiliate-disclosure"><em>This article contains affiliate links. If you book through them we earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.</em></p>

<h2>Start at Piraeus</h2>

<p><strong>Piraeus</strong>, about 20–30 minutes from central Athens by Metro, is Greece's main ferry hub and the launch point for most island trips. From here, ferries reach the Cyclades, the Saronic Gulf, Crete, and beyond. (Two smaller Athens-area ports, Rafina and Lavrio, serve some Cycladic routes too and can be quicker to certain islands.) For a first-timer, Piraeus is the default and the most connected.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/best-island-hopping-routes-from-athens-1" alt="Ferries lined up at the busy port of Piraeus near Athens" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Route 1: The Cyclades (the first-timer classic)</h2>

<p>The <strong>Cyclades</strong> — Santorini, Mykonos, Naxos, Paros — are the quintessential Greek island-hopping route and the right choice for most first trips. The islands sit close together with frequent ferries, so hops are short, and they offer real variety: the icon, the party island, and the laid-back authentic ones. A classic run is <strong>Naxos → Paros → Santorini</strong>, finishing with a flight back to Athens.</p>

<h3>Best for</h3>
<p>First-timers who want the postcard Greece — whitewashed villages, blue domes, great beaches — with easy logistics.</p>

<h2>Route 2: Saronic Gulf (the short-trip option)</h2>

<p>The <strong>Saronic Gulf</strong> islands — <strong>Hydra</strong>, <strong>Poros</strong>, <strong>Aegina</strong>, and Spetses — sit just off the Athens coast, reachable in as little as an hour or two. Car-free <strong>Hydra</strong>, with its elegant stone harbor, is the gem. These islands are perfect when you don't have time to reach the Cyclades, or want an easy add-on to an Athens stay.</p>

<h3>Best for</h3>
<p>Short trips, long weekends, or a quick island taste close to Athens without a long ferry.</p>

<figure><img src="https://img.sgfleetmedia.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/greeceforamericans/articles/best-island-hopping-routes-from-athens-2" alt="The car-free stone harbor of Hydra in the Saronic Gulf" loading="lazy" /></figure>

<h2>Route 3: Crete and the south</h2>

<p><strong>Crete</strong> is reachable by overnight ferry from Piraeus (around 9 hours) or a short flight. It's so large it's really a destination of its own rather than a hopping stop — best as a focused trip, or combined with the Cyclades on a longer (two-week) itinerary via a flight rather than the long ferry.</p>

<h3>Best for</h3>
<p>Travelers wanting one big, varied island — mountains, Minoan history, food, and beaches — rather than multiple small hops.</p>

<h2>Route 4: The Dodecanese (for a longer or return trip)</h2>

<p>Further east, near the Turkish coast, the <strong>Dodecanese</strong> (Rhodes, Kos, Symi, and more) are a rewarding route — but they're a long way from Athens, so they suit a longer trip, a flight in rather than the long ferry, or a return visit once you've done the Cyclades. Combining them with the Cyclades on a short first trip means long, awkward connections.</p>

<h2>How to choose your route</h2>

<p>For a <strong>first trip of a week or more</strong>, the Cyclades are almost always the answer — iconic, varied, and easy. For a <strong>short trip or an Athens add-on</strong>, the Saronic Gulf delivers an island day or overnight without the distance. For a <strong>single-island deep dive</strong>, Crete. And save the <strong>Dodecanese</strong> for a longer or second trip. The golden rule holds: <strong>stay within one island group</strong> per trip to keep ferries short and reliable, rather than crisscrossing the Aegean.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: program=booking context="island hotels across your chosen route" -->

<h2>Ferry logistics from Athens</h2>

<p>A few practical notes wherever you go: book <strong>high-speed and peak-summer sailings ahead</strong>, as they sell out; check the <strong>current timetable</strong>, since schedules are seasonal; confirm which <strong>port</strong> your ferry uses (Piraeus, Rafina, or Lavrio); and build a buffer before your flight home, since the summer <strong>meltemi wind</strong> can cancel fast boats. End on an island with an airport so you can <strong>fly back to Athens</strong> to connect home — there are no direct U.S. flights from the islands. Because fares shift by route, boat, and season, check current prices when you book rather than relying on a fixed figure.</p>

<h2>How long each route needs</h2>
<p>Match the route to the time you have. The <strong>Saronic Gulf</strong> works as a day trip or an overnight from Athens — the only route that fits a very short trip or a single spare day. The <strong>Cyclades</strong> want at least a week to be worthwhile (two islands plus Athens), and reward ten days to two weeks for three or four islands at a relaxed pace. <strong>Crete</strong> needs four or five days minimum to justify the journey, ideally a week, since it's a destination unto itself. The <strong>Dodecanese</strong>, being far from Athens, really need a longer trip or a flight in to be practical. The mismatch to avoid is picking a far-flung route for a short trip — you'll spend your days in transit. When time is tight, stay close (Saronic or a quick Cyclades hop); when you have two weeks, the Cyclades or a focused Crete trip both shine.</p>

# Ingrid Vos — L'ATLAS · the information designer

**Who.** Dutch, trained in cartography before design; spent a decade drawing pilot
books and transit diagrams. Believes a page is a chart: every element has a position,
a scale and a key, and the reader should be able to *use* the page without reading it.
Her sentence: "If it cannot be cited, it was not designed." Dislikes atmosphere — "a
photograph that proves nothing is a mood board".

**Her method, chapter by chapter.**
1. Find the figure. If the chapter has a map or a diagram, that is the hero, at the
   full measure. If it has none, draw one *from the ledger*: the options as rows, their
   attributes as a matrix (■ applies / □ does not), the call as a mono column, the dose
   as a bracket. Attributes come from the ledger's own words; never invent one.
2. Sheet header: the decision number at 72px in signal red (the only large thing),
   the title in Space Grotesk 500, a mono sub-line, and a READING KEY at the right
   that explains the page's own notation.
3. The standfirst as an abstract, Inter Light 14.5px.
4. Prose in two 314px columns, Inter 10.5/15, ragged, numbered §1 … §n in the margin
   so a fork or a notice can point at a paragraph.
5. THE CALL as a signal block: 4px red rule at left, the lead sentence in Grotesk 17px.
6. Forks as a two-column IF / THEN table; the volatile note as a NOTICE box with a
   hatched red edge — the pilot book's notice to mariners.
7. Photographs as evidence plates: square or 2:1, numbered PL. 05.1–3, in a strip at
   the foot, each caption saying what the plate proves.

**Type.** Space Grotesk (display) · Inter (text) · IBM Plex Mono (apparatus, the
house mono). **Colour.** Paper #F5F6F3, ink, grey #6B7773, panel #E8EDEA, one signal
#A23520. The map keeps its sea blue and gold default because they are data.

**She refuses.** A photograph larger than a plate; a colour that is not a key; prose
that cannot be cited; a hero that is not a figure.

**Where she is weakest.** The drawn figure is only as honest as the ledger's
attributes — the writer must supply them at Stage C. Her pages carry 10–15 % quiet at
the foot; the Eye must say whether it reads as calm or as unfinished.

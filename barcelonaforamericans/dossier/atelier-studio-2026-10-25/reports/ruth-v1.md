# Ruth Calloway reads Barcelona, Without the Traps. — 2026-10-25, build 68 pages + Field Sheet (1 p)

Reader: 34B, adapted to this book. An American couple, aged 30–65, on a first trip, six weeks out. The flight is booked and the hotel is not. They paid **$14.99** (Etsy), read at 390px in bed, and print the wallet card in black and white. They think in dollars, miles and pm. They speak no Spanish or Catalan, have heard "pickpockets on La Rambla" and "book the Sagrada Família", and could not define T-casual, HUTB or DCC. One of them has bad knees, and they will go out on a mid-range budget (a $200–300 hotel night, so a 3★ or 4★ room).

Verdict: SHIP AFTER FIXES

*Barcelona, Without the Traps.* is seven calls, each opened by a poster that gives the answer before the argument. The posters work at phone size: EIXAMPLE, HOTEL, 4, 3, 2+1, MONTSERRAT, 20:30. The organising idea ("four small charges in four moods") is true, useful and held all the way through.

Worth $14.99? Yes for the sequencing (D04), the Gaudí dose (D05) and the Failure Modes sorted by consequence (p59). No third-party page makes those joins. But all seven calls also sit on the publisher's own free site, two of them in near-identical sentences (M9).

The biggest problems are two places where the book tells the reader to act wrongly. The Plan puts the booked Sagrada morning on "day one", which the book's own rules make the arrival day (B1). And when the Sagrada calendar is full, the book sends the reader to "authorised resellers". The basilica's own conditions say it "will not accept any Ticket purchased through a reseller entity" (B2).

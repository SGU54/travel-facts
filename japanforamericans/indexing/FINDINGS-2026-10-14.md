# Indexing recovery, measured — Japan and Rome, 2026-10-14

The planned action was "internal links from the indexed pages to the never-crawled
ones, plus URL Inspection requests". **The measurement says that is not the binding
constraint.** What follows is what the data actually shows.

## Japan — a crawl-DEMAND problem, not a link-graph problem

GSC, 2026-10-14 (data to 09/10):

| | |
|---|---|
| In the index | **42** |
| Not indexed | **90**, 5 reasons |
| └ Discovered, currently not indexed | **75** ← the bucket |
| └ Crawled, currently not indexed | 9 |
| └ Redirect / duplicate-no-canonical / noindex | 3 / 2 / 1 |

The 75 break down as **59 articles + 13 category pages + /guide, /sources,
/disclosures**. "Dernière exploration: Sans objet" on every row — Google has
**never fetched** them.

### Three measurements that kill the link-graph theory

1. **Every article is already reachable.** Crawled all 33 of Japan's own
   navigation surfaces (homepage + 27 category pages + the rest) and collected
   every `/article/` href: **96 of 96 published articles are linked from a
   navigation page.** Nothing is orphaned.
2. **Exposure is not what separates crawled from uncrawled.** Of the 13
   never-crawled category pages, **9 are linked directly from the homepage** —
   which is itself crawled and indexed. Meanwhile three categories with *no*
   homepage link (`etiquette-and-culture`, `what-to-eat`, `when-to-go`) **have**
   been crawled. Google sees the links and declines anyway.
3. **GSC has already built the internal graph: 365 internal links.** Adding
   contextual body links moves a number that is already 365.

### The number that is zero

```
Liens externes — Total : 0
```

**Japan has no external backlinks at all.** Internal links redistribute crawl
budget; they do not create crawl demand. With zero external signal, Google
rations what it will spend on a 128-URL site, and the 75 are what it declined.

A validation run on the "Discovered" bucket was already marked **Réussi**
(succeeded) and the 75 are still there — Google re-evaluated and kept its answer.

### Secondary: the link pass could not have covered it anyway

Under the fleet's 3-links-per-article doctrine, the 37 crawled articles have
**44 spare link slots** between them (14 are already at the cap) against **59**
targets. The pass as scoped was not arithmetically capable of giving every
never-crawled article an inbound link from a crawled one.

## Rome — the opposite disease

| | |
|---|---|
| In the index | **84** |
| └ Crawled, currently not indexed | **41** ← the bucket |
| └ Discovered, currently not indexed | 8 |
| └ duplicate-no-canonical / redirect / robots / noindex | 5 / 3 / 2 / 1 |

Rome's problem is **not** crawl budget: Google fetched the pages and chose not to
index them. The 41 include strong, high-intent articles
(`vatican-museums-sistine-chapel-tickets`, `roman-forum-guide`,
`best-time-to-visit-rome`, `colosseum-vs-vatican-where-to-stay`). That is a
quality/competition judgment on a saturated topic set, and more internal links do
not address it.

Rome also reports `Liens externes: 0` **and** `Liens internes: 0`, while holding
84 indexed pages — the internal figure looks like reporting lag, not reality.
Worth re-reading rather than acting on.

### Rome items that are closed

- **www sitemap: healthy.** `https://www.romeforamericans.com/sitemap.xml` —
  submitted, last read 14 Oct 2026, "Opération effectuée", 136 pages discovered.
  The "couldn't fetch" seen right after submission has cleared, as expected.
- **"Blocked by robots.txt" (2) is correct behaviour**, not a defect: robots.txt
  deliberately disallows `/go/`, `/api/`, `/search`.
- **"Excluded by noindex" (1 on each site)** is almost certainly the
  `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` the middleware sets on non-canonical hosts — i.e. the
  feature working. Worth one URL Inspection to confirm before anyone "fixes" it.

## What this means

The bottleneck on both sites is **off-site**, and it is the same zero on both.
No amount of internal linking, sitemap resubmission or URL-Inspection clicking
changes a site with no external links into one Google wants to crawl more of.
URL Inspection "Request indexing" is also capped at roughly ten a day and is
manual, so it cannot clear 75 and does not persist.

This is a decision for the human (hard rule 14 / the DESIGN-DECISIONS pattern),
not something to execute unilaterally — the realistic levers are off-site and
each costs either money or editorial time.

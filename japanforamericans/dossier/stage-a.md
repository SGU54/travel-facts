## Dominant edge (tallied, not assumed)
MONEY 585 · EXPERIENCE 318 · BREAKAGE 70 · **EXTRACTION 42**

Japan is a money/allocation destination, NOT an extraction destination. Rome's
"a centre engineered to separate tourists from cash" frame is actively wrong here:
no tipping, no haggling, honest pricing, near-zero scam cues in 96 articles. The
anxiety is not "will I be ripped off" — it is "am I spending my money and my days
in the right shape". The cover promise leads with allocation, not defence.

## The organising fact
**The trip almost every first-timer actually takes contains only two long train
journeys — and the national rail pass needs about four to pay for itself.**

Measured: trip-shape fact in 6/96 articles, break-even fact in 4/96, the Oct-2023
price rise in 4/96, day-trips in 34/96. **All four joined in 2 of 96 articles.**
(Greece benchmark: 2 of 80.) The facts are everywhere; the connection is nowhere.

## The shape
**Two-hub corridor with day-trip spokes** — not Greece's archipelago sequencing and
not Rome's compact-city scale. Tokyo and Kyoto, one Shinkansen leg between them,
everything else radiating locally from one of the two hubs.

Cover device: the route graph — two hubs, one long leg, the day-trip spokes, with
the leg count set against the pass break-even. The cover argues the arithmetic.

## What THIS audience gets wrong
1. Buys the JR Pass by default on decade-old advice written before the Oct-2023
   price rise (~$330/person, and it then distorts the route to justify itself)
2. US-issued Visa cards silently fail to top up mobile Suica (Mastercard/Amex work)
3. Expects snow in Kyoto in January; treats cherry blossom as bookable
4. Plans more inter-city legs than the two-base shape actually wants

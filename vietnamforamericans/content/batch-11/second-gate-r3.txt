=== ARTICLE 1 (TURN) ===
Three of central Vietnam's main destinations sit close together. Hoi An's Ancient Town, An Bang beach and the city of Da Nang are all within about an hour of each other by road, which makes it easy to treat them as one stop. Which one you sleep in shapes the trip more than those distances suggest.

Hoi An Ancient Town

Sleeping inside or on the edge of the old town means walking to most of what you came for: the lanterns at night, the tailors, breakfast, the riverside. The trade is that the Ancient Town is heavily visited, and the central streets are at their busiest through the middle of the day and into the evening.

It suits short stays and first visits, and anyone whose main reason for coming is the town itself. The town is quietest early and late, and those hours are considerably easier to use if you are already inside it.

An Bang and the beach

A short ride from the Ancient Town — ten to fifteen minutes by bicycle, less in a Grab — and a different pace entirely: low-rise guesthouses and small resorts, a wide beach, beach restaurants. You get the old town as a destination you ride to rather than a place you live in.

It suits stays of three nights or more, and families who want the beach at the door rather than a city around them. The cost is that an evening in the Ancient Town means arranging transport back.

Da Nang

A city rather than a town: a long beachfront, high-rise hotels, the Han River, a city's range of bars and restaurants, and an airport close to the centre. Hoi An is around forty-five minutes south by car.

It suits travellers who want a city, anyone arriving late or leaving early by air, families who want more than a beach to fill a week, and longer stays generally. It is a poor base for a two-night trip built around the Ancient Town, because the round trip eats into a short stay.

What each base costs you in travel

The distances are the whole argument, so it is worth being concrete. Hoi An to An Bang is a few kilometres of flat road — ten to fifteen minutes by bicycle, less in a Grab. Hoi An to central Da Nang is roughly thirty kilometres and about forty-five minutes by car. Da Nang's airport sits close to its own city centre, and around forty-five minutes to an hour from Hoi An depending on traffic.

On those figures, a Da Nang base puts about an hour and a half on the road each time you go into the Ancient Town and come back. From An Bang the same return trip is twenty to thirty minutes by bicycle, less by Grab. From Hoi An the town is a walk, and the beach is that same twenty to thirty minutes there and back. Which of those you would rather pay is the choice.

How to choose, practically

Two nights and the old town is the point: stay in Hoi An. Three or four nights with beach time: An Bang, and ride in. A week, a family, or an early flight: Da Nang, or split between Da Nang and Hoi An.

Season matters too. Between roughly September and November the central coast is in its storm season, and a beach base loses much of its advantage when the sea is rough and the sky is grey. The Ancient Town is less dependent on the weather, though it is not immune to it — the streets nearest the river flood in some years.

The split option

Two nights in Hoi An and two in Da Nang is a workable shape for four nights, and it costs you one transfer. It gives you the town at its quiet hours and a city with an airport at the end.

What is nearby from each

From Hoi An: the Ancient Town, Tra Que vegetable village, the Thu Bon river, and My Son Sanctuary roughly an hour west. From An Bang: the same list, plus the beach itself and the quieter coast north of it. From Da Nang: the Marble Mountains, the Son Tra peninsula and Linh Ung Pagoda, My Khe beach, the Ba Na Hills complex with the Golden Bridge, and the Hai Van Pass heading north.

The list from Da Nang is the longer of the three, which is the honest case for it on a longer stay. The Hoi An list is shorter, and most of it other than My Son is within a short ride.

Getting between them

Grab operates in all three. Hoi An to An Bang is flat enough to cycle. Some hotels in Hoi An and An Bang run shuttles to the Ancient Town — worth asking about before booking, since it changes how easily you get to dinner.

What this choice is actually setting

Look at what follows from it. Your base shapes how easily you can be in the Ancient Town early or late, which is the difference between the lantern-lit streets in the photographs and the middle of a busy day. It sets whether the beach is somewhere you stay or somewhere you visit. And it sets what you can do on a day the weather closes the beach — a city base gives you indoor options a beach base does not.

Those consequences are easy to miss when the three are three dots close together on a map. Where the base question gets harder is when it meets the rest of the trip — which nights you have in the region at all, and what you have committed to before and after. That wider ordering is what the complete Vietnam decision manual works through.

=== ARTICLE 2 (SEED) ===
The Hai Van Pass climbs over a spur of mountains between Hue and Da Nang, with the sea on one side. It is among the best-known stretches of road in Vietnam, and if you are travelling between those two cities you will cross it one way or another — so the question is how.

What is actually up there

The road tops out at the Hai Van Gate, an old stone fortification that guarded the route, with later military structures around it. The view back along the coast on a clear day is why people stop.

Weather matters more here than the height suggests. The pass makes its own weather and can be sitting in cloud when both cities are clear.

The tunnel

A road tunnel runs beneath the pass. It is faster, motorbikes are not permitted through it, and it sees none of the view. Much through traffic uses it by default — so if you want the pass, say so when you book.

By train

The railway runs along the coast below the road between Hue and Da Nang; allow around three hours. It costs very little, it goes where the road cannot, and it is one of the best rail stretches in Southeast Asia. Ask for a seat on the sea side when you book.

This is the simplest answer for most itineraries: it replaces a transfer you were likely making anyway, and the views from the track are arguably better than from the road.

By car with a driver

A private car over the pass, with stops, is a half day rather than a hop, and it lets you get out at the top and at the beaches on either side. Many drivers will carry your luggage directly between hotels, which makes it a transfer and a day out at once.

It is the option that gives you the most control over the day, and the one that suits families, groups, and anyone who wants the photographs without riding.

By motorbike, and the legal problem

The pass is well known as a ride, and the easy-rider model — riding pillion behind an experienced local while your bag goes by van — is a common way to do it. Riding pillion removes the licence problem. It does not remove the road risk: this is a demanding road with switchbacks, trucks, sudden cloud and wet surfaces, and road traffic is the main hazard for visitors to Vietnam.

Riding it yourself is the other problem. A US licence and an International Driving Permit are both invalid in Vietnam, driving without a Vietnamese licence can mean a fine, arrest, or jail, and your travel insurance may decline a claim after an accident.

If you ride pillion

Easy-rider operators run the route in both directions. Agree in advance what is included — the bag transfer, lunch, the stops — and expect the day to be longer than the distance suggests, because the stopping is the point.

How long it takes

Da Nang to Hue is about 100 kilometres. Through the tunnel, allow around two hours by car. Over the pass without stopping, allow a little more. With stops at the summit and a beach it becomes a half day, and an easy-rider trip takes most of one.

The train runs to a timetable and is not held up by road traffic. Plan the day around whichever you pick rather than treating any of them as a quick hop.

What to stop for

The summit is the main stop: the old gate, the bunkers, the stalls selling drinks, and the view in both directions when the cloud allows. Heading south, Lang Co beach and its lagoon sit at the northern foot of the pass and are worth stopping for. Heading north out of Da Nang, the Son Tra peninsula and Linh Ung Pagoda are a detour before the climb begins.

Expect to be approached by vendors at the summit. Expect also that the view may simply not be there — a grey crossing is a real possibility rather than bad luck.

Timing and weather

September to November is the central coast's storm season, and it is the worst stretch for this crossing — cloud on the pass, wet and slick road surfaces, and a view that may not appear at all.

Which to choose

Train if you want the cheapest and easiest version, and the one least affected by the road sitting in cloud. Car with driver if you want to stop, or have luggage and company. Easy-rider if the road itself is why you came. The tunnel if you are in a hurry and do not mind missing it.

Booking it

Train tickets for this stretch are sold at the stations and through the national railway's own booking site, and the carriage classes vary by service. Private cars and easy-rider trips are arranged through hotels and local operators in both cities, usually a day or two ahead. Whichever you book, say explicitly whether you want the pass or the tunnel.

The rest of Vietnam's long legs are a separate question, and one the complete Vietnam decision manual works through.

Task A — In-context errors

I found 16 reportable issues. These are internal contradictions, misleading implications, or certainty escalations—not findings based merely on a fact being absent from facts.json. “Better-supported” below means supported by the supplied evidence; this is not a fresh external fact-check.

Vietnam Money and Costs for Americans

1. Transport cost is dismissed as a decision variable.

“Intercity transport is cheap enough that the choice between bus and plane is about time and comfort rather than money.”

Cheap in absolute terms does not establish that the difference is immaterial to the reader. The driver article itself considers whether transport “arithmetic” favours the road. This sentence presents a budget-dependent judgment as a general fact.

2. The handoff manufactures an exact geographic equivalence.

“How much cash you carry sets which parts of the country you can comfortably reach, because the cash-only zone and the rural zone are the same map.”

The article’s own examples include urban street food, markets and independent cafes requiring cash. Its cash-only zone therefore extends beyond its rural zone. Cash availability can affect itinerary decisions; the claim that these are “the same map” is contradicted by the article’s explanation and inflates the handoff.

Tipping in Vietnam: A Practical Guide

3. App booking, payment and tipping are conflated.

“Grab settles the fare in the app before the ride, which removes the question entirely — though the app offers a tip option and drivers appreciate it on long or awkward trips.”

The money article explicitly recognises “motorbike-taxi fares paid in person,” so universal advance settlement is not established within the batch. More decisively, settling the fare does not remove the tipping question: the same sentence immediately describes an optional tip. Fare certainty and tipping expectations are different questions.

Vietnam Visa Extension Guide

4. “Usually” becomes an assured individual outcome.

“Vietnam usually grants exactly the period you requested rather than defaulting to the maximum, so asking for three weeks gets you three weeks even though 90 days was available.”

The first clause preserves the source qualification; the second drops it. The supplied source says Vietnam usually grants the requested period, not that a three-week request necessarily produces a three-week grant. The qualified source wording is better-supported.

5. An in-country restriction becomes an irreversible application decision.

“That request is final.”

And:

“Because the duration cannot be fixed later, the visa form stops being paperwork and becomes one of the first real commitments of the trip — made before you have settled the route, often before the flights.”

The source establishes that an e-visa cannot be renewed or extended while in Vietnam. It does not establish that a requested duration can never be replaced through a subsequent application before travel. The article also describes obtaining a fresh visa after leaving.

Facts.json contains the same overreach:

“The duration decision is made at application and cannot be corrected later.”

Neither version is adequately supported. The source’s in-country limitation is the defensible statement; the broader finality claim helps manufacture the handoff’s urgency.

6. The recommended border-run budget conflicts with the processing window.

“You need the new visa approved before you return, your overland crossing must be one of the gates where e-visas are accepted, and the round trip costs a day or two plus the fee.”

And:

“Budget two days and the visa fee, and do not book anything in Vietnam that assumes you will be back by a particular hour.”

Elsewhere the article says processing usually takes 3–5 working days. Facts.json additionally preserves longer processing during surges or holidays. Two days can describe travel time if approval is already secured; it cannot stand as the unqualified budget for the process presented here. Avoiding a particular return hour does not address a potential delay of several working days.

7. Airport eligibility is incorrectly used to establish cheapest cost and predictable timing.

“The cheapest version is a short flight to Bangkok or Phnom Penh rather than an overland crossing, because the air gates are all eligible and the timing is predictable.”

Eligible gates do not establish that flights are cheaper than land travel. The starting location and actual fares are unspecified. Eligibility also does not make visa-processing time predictable. This sentence turns logistical convenience into an unsupported price ranking and timing assurance.

8. Eligible crossing is presented as sufficient without the selected-port constraint.

“If you do plan to leave and re-enter, two details decide whether it goes smoothly.”

Followed by:

“And the crossing you use must be one of the gates where e-visas are accepted, which covers every international airport but only a subset of land crossings into Cambodia and Laos.”

Facts.json explicitly distinguishes gate eligibility from matching the port selected on the application. In a passage presenting the decisive requirements, the article reduces the port question to eligibility. Its closing paragraph mentions that the named port constrains the itinerary, but never connects that constraint to these border-run instructions. On the supplied evidence, the facts.json distinction is better-supported.

9. Likely overstay consequences become certain consequences.

“The State Department warns that staying beyond your visa, even by accident, means fines and delays getting permission to leave — and that a medical emergency is not treated as an exception.”

The supplied source quotation says:

“you will likely pay overstay fines and face delays getting permission to leave.”

“Means fines and delays” removes “likely.” Facts.json’s “Overstaying brings fines and delays” has the same defect. The quoted source qualification is better-supported than either restatement.

10. The recognised exit-approval alternative disappears.

“Vietnam requires valid status to exit as well as to enter, so an expired visa blocks your departure rather than merely annoying someone at the gate.”

The supplied source permits departure with a valid visa, residence card or Immigration Department approval. An expired visa can require obtaining approval and cause delays; it is not an unconditional departure bar regardless of that approval. The facts.json alternatives are better-supported than the article’s categorical formulation.

Hiring a Driver vs Riding Yourself in Vietnam

11. The excerpt converts possible nonpayment into automatic policy invalidation.

“Riding yourself is illegal for most visitors and voids insurance.”

Facts.json says:

“After an accident while riding without a valid licence, travel insurance may not pay your medical bills.”

The excerpt changes both the condition and the consequence: riding oneself replaces riding unlicensed, and possible refusal of medical bills becomes automatic invalidation of insurance. The article’s own body correctly retains “may not pay.” The qualified version is better-supported. This is a direct recurrence of the pathology named in the brief.

12. A precaution becomes a threatened inevitable charge.

“Photograph the whole bike with the renter watching before you ride off, or expect existing scratches on your bill.”

Photographing existing damage is a precaution. Its absence does not establish that existing scratches will be charged. “Or expect” turns a possible dispute into the predicted outcome, adding fear beyond the evidence presented.

13. The concluding recommendation reverses the preceding transport distinction.

“A useful split: hire a car with a driver for the long intercity legs, use Grab in the cities, and take an easy-rider for the one mountain route you actually came for.”

Earlier:

“For the genuinely long legs, neither riding nor hiring a driver is the obvious answer — flying or taking the train often is.”

The conclusion recommends a driver for the category the preceding section directs toward planes or trains. The reader receives conflicting instructions for long intercity journeys.

Vietnam Customs and Duty-Free Rules

14. Item selection is presented without the value constraint that makes it lawful.

“If you are carrying one expensive item and several cheap ones, you can place the expensive one inside the allowance and declare the remainder.”

The article establishes a 10,000,000-dong total customs-value cap. Choosing which items use the allowance does not establish that an expensive item exceeding that cap can be placed wholly inside it. The example is only valid subject to the cap. Facts.json’s value limit and selection provision must be read together; the article’s unrestricted example is misleading.

15. Declaring excess goods is made to sound cost-free.

“The paperwork is short and declaring costs you nothing; being found over the line after choosing green is a different conversation entirely.”

This paragraph covers all allowances, not just cash. Earlier, the article explicitly says excess alcohol can be dutiable. Even if filing the declaration itself carries no fee, the sentence misleadingly implies that choosing red with excess goods has no financial consequence. The article’s own duty qualification is the better-supported account.

16. “Every figure” incorrectly extends the resetting allowance concept to cash thresholds.

“Every figure here is per person, per entry, granted against your passport.”

The article also contains cash-declaration thresholds, which are reporting triggers—not allowances “granted” to the traveller. Those thresholds apply on departure too, as the cash section explicitly states. The next sentence about receiving a fresh allowance after a border run should not encompass every figure in the article.

Vietnam Money and Costs for Americans — PULL 74 / TRUST 0

The turn exists: payment arrangements connect to transport booking, withdrawal access and itinerary planning. The stake is practical access to services. The handoff points toward a system:

“Which decisions depend on which, and the order that keeps them from colliding, is worked out in the complete Vietnam decision manual.”

That earns a PULL pass, reduced because the “same map” claim exaggerates the dependency.

TRUST is automatically 0 because the article explicitly withholds a central answer to “Money and Costs”:

“We deliberately do not print a daily budget figure here: prices move, and a number frozen into an article in October is misleading by spring.”

The reader gets vague comparisons rather than enough information to estimate trip costs. Price variability explains why estimates need dates and assumptions; it does not make the cost question answered. This is answer withholding even though the article does not explicitly say the missing budget is inside the paid guide.

Tipping in Vietnam: A Practical Guide — TRUST 0

The article identifies services where tips are expected, then explicitly declines to provide the practical amounts:

“Amounts are modest and we are not going to print a table of them, because currency values shift and a figure frozen in an article ages badly.”

Its substitute is:

“The useful rule is proportionality: a tip in Vietnam is a gesture, not a percentage, and it should feel small relative to what the service cost.”

“Feel small” does not tell an unfamiliar American reader what to leave for a porter, driver, therapist or cruise crew. The article therefore reaches its handoff before fully answering the practical question. Automatic 0 applies.

The guide appears once and the seed’s intensity is restrained; those positives cannot override the withheld-answer rule.

Vietnam Visa Extension Guide — TRUST 64

The central question is answered before the handoff: the supplied evidence says an e-visa cannot be extended in-country. The guide appears once, and the connection between duration, entry count, port and itinerary is real.

The failure is inflated finality and unreliable fallback planning:

“That request is final.”

“Budget two days and the visa fee, and do not book anything in Vietnam that assumes you will be back by a particular hour.”

The first broadens the sourced restriction; the second conflicts with the processing period. The handoff then relies on that broadened restriction:

“Because the duration cannot be fixed later, the visa form stops being paperwork and becomes one of the first real commitments of the trip — made before you have settled the route, often before the flights.”

That makes the planning problem more irreversible than the supplied evidence establishes. The repeated escalation of consequences is too strong for a trustworthy seed.

A first trip to northern Vietnam usually has two days to give the landscape outside Hanoi, and the two obvious ways to spend them show you the same thing from different sides. Ha Long Bay is the limestone karst at sea, seen from a boat you sleep on. Ninh Binh is the same karst inland, seen from a rowboat on a river between rice fields. Guidebooks list both and leave the choice to you. Here is how to make it.

The short answer

Ninh Binh is the better use of two days for most first-time visitors in winter, for anyone prone to seasickness, and for anyone who wants to move at their own pace. Ha Long Bay is the better use for anyone whose dates fall in the calm months and who wants the night on the water, which is the thing the day trips cannot give.

If you have two days and are unsure, the deciding factor is the weather forecast for your dates, not the photographs.

Why not both

Because both in two days means a day trip to each, and the Ha Long day trip is the weakest version of Ha Long there is. Two days gives you one place done properly. Three or four lets you do an overnight on the bay and a day in Ninh Binh, which is the combination the longer itineraries use.

What each costs you in travel

Ha Long Bay and Ninh Binh are both reachable from Hanoi in a morning. Hanoi to Ha Long city by road is about 2.5 hours since the expressway and the Bach Dang bridge opened in 2018; guidebooks that still say four hours were printed before the road. Ninh Binh is about two hours south, by road or by train. The difference in transfer time is small; the difference is in what happens when you arrive.

At Ha Long you board a boat on a fixed schedule and the boat decides your day. At Ninh Binh you are on land with a bicycle or a driver, and you decide your day. A two-day Ha Long trip is therefore one night aboard with a short second morning. A two-day Ninh Binh trip is a full day and a half of sights, because nothing is waiting for you to embark.

The weather problem is Ha Long's alone

Ha Long Bay cruises are cancelled by the port authority, not by the operator, whenever a storm or dangerous weather is forecast: the provincial waterways port authority stops issuing departure permits to every tourist vessel, day boats and overnight cruises alike. Operators refund or reschedule, but a two-day window has no room to reschedule into. Ninh Binh's rowboats run in rain; a wet day there is a worse day, not a lost one.

The winter months in the north bring fog and drizzle that sell well in photographs and poorly in person: a grey bay with the islands half visible, and the real risk of a suspension. Late spring, and again from early autumn into November, give the best odds of clear air and calm water; summer is the busiest season on the bay and the one that brings storm cancellations. If your two days fall in a winter forecast of low cloud, Ninh Binh is the safer bet by a distance.

If the cruise is cancelled

Have Ninh Binh as the fallback in your head before you leave Hanoi. A cancelled cruise on the morning of departure can be turned into a Ninh Binh day trip from the same hotel, which is why the two places are substitutes and not just alternatives.

What kind of day you get

A Ha Long overnight is a cruise: cabins, meals aboard, a kayak session, a cave, a swim, the evening light on the water and the morning before the day boats arrive. It is passive in the pleasant sense, and it is the one experience of the bay that the crowds cannot spoil, because the overnight boats anchor away from the day-trip circuit.

A Ninh Binh day is active. The Trang An boat route passes through water caves and past temples for two to three hours; Tam Coc is shorter, prettier in the golden rice weeks, and more commercial. Between boats there is the climb to the Hang Mua viewpoint, Bai Dinh pagoda, and the countryside by bicycle. People who like to be doing something every hour prefer it, and people who want to sit still with a drink prefer the boat.

Children, older travellers and seasickness

Both work for families. The bay in calm weather is gentle; a rolling bay is miserable, and the forecast settles that. Ninh Binh's climbs are optional and its boats are flat-water. Anyone who is unsteady on their feet will find a cruise boat's stairs harder than a rowboat's bench.

How to decide

Check the forecast for your two dates. Calm and clear: book the Ha Long overnight, because the night on the bay is the better of the two experiences when the weather lets it be. Cloud, wind or a storm warning: go to Ninh Binh and spend the second night there rather than back in Hanoi. Seasick, short of time or travelling with someone who tires: Ninh Binh regardless.

Book a Ninh Binh day tour or a Ha Long Bay overnight cruise once that call is made, and hold the other as the fallback.

What this choice is really part of

Notice that the deciding factor was the month, not the place. The same month decides whether Sapa's terraces are green or brown, whether Hue is flooded, and whether the beaches at the other end of the country are in season. A trip that chooses Ha Long for its dates and then arrives at the coast in its wet weeks has answered one question correctly and the others not at all.

The month-by-region table that resolves them together, with a verdict for each pair, is the spine of the complete Vietnam decision manual.

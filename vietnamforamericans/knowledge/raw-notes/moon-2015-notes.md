# Moon Vietnam (2015) — paraphrased reading notes

Book: *Moon Vietnam*, Dana Filek-Gibson, Avalon Travel 2015, 531 pp. archive.org id `vietnam0000file`, leaf = printed page + 1 (`/page/P/mode/1up` resolves). Read 2026-10-17 in the CDL viewer. Everything below is paraphrase; page numbers are the printed page. Every figure is a LEAD (2015 vintage) until checked against a current official source.

Why this book matters differently from DK and LP: it is a US publisher (Moon/Avalon, Berkeley CA) written by an American expat living in Saigon, so its frame of reference is explicitly American ("more formal than the United States", CDC named as the vaccination authority, USD pricing). It is the closest thing to our reader's own voice among the borrowed books.

## Discover / Where to go (p7–10)
- p7: opening frame — the country "forty years after" the war, a collision of old and new; the author's hook is sensory (horns, grilled meat, motion) rather than historical. Useful as a model of how an American resident sees it; not citable.
- p8–10 region thumbnails: Hanoi = history + Old Quarter shopping + Museum of Ethnology singled out as the best intro to minority cultures. Northern coast = Halong overnight cruise, Cat Ba cycling, Lan Ha climbing, kayaking; Ninh Binh rowboat. Central = Hue tombs, Hai Van Pass, Phong Nha "within striking distance", Danang as the "ultra-cool" new city, Hoi An for shoppers/photographers/food. South-central = Nha Trang (energy, water sports) vs Mui Ne (quiet resort enclave), kitesurfing, Dalat as the region's anomaly (cool, rural, adventure). HCMC = District 1 landmarks, Chinatown pagodas, xe om, sidewalk coffee at 30-4 Park, street food "best in the country" (author's bias: she lives there). Mekong = Cai Rang floating market at dawn, An Binh Island homestay, Tra Vinh Khmer pagodas, Sa Dec, Chau Doc as the Cambodia gateway, Phu Quoc "fastest-growing island".

## When to go (p10–11) — THIRD opinion on timing
- Moon's best months: **September–October** (south rains ending, north not yet cold) **or April–May** (north thawed, south not yet raining). This differs from DK (Mar–May) and LP (varies by region, no single answer). Three books, three answers → the month question is genuinely region-dependent and the site's month×region grid is the right shape.
- High season = Nov–Mar, peaking at Christmas then Tet. Moon is the bluntest of the three on Tet: it "makes for a poor visit" — the country shuts for weeks either side, open businesses double or triple prices, transport unreliable. (DK: good for participating; LP: prices up, trains blacked out.) Lead: duration of shutdown "weeks before and after" is an exaggeration by current standards (typically ~1 week); verify.
- Summer: fewer foreigners but July–August is domestic peak at Halong and Nha Trang.
- North only: Sep–Oct or Apr–Jun; high season in the north is chilly and misty; the mist north of Hue is pretty if you aren't expecting sun.
- South: cooler Nov–Mar; heat builds to ~100°F with humidity before rains start mid/late May; May–Sep rain is predictable afternoon showers; October is the unpredictable end of the season.

## Before you go (p11–13)
- Visas (p11): "required prior to arrival"; 1- or 3-month, single or multiple entry; book quotes USD 100–180 all-in. Overland arrivals must get the visa at an embassy; air arrivals can use the "pre-approval" (VOA letter) route, cheaper, only at SGN, HAN, DAD; the book notes the government doesn't advertise it but it is legitimate via a reliable agent. Six months' passport validity. **OBSOLETE**: e-visa (2017, expanded 2023 to 90 days multiple entry) replaced all of this for Americans; the 2015 price band must not be printed. Conflict with facts.json visa keys → disputes.md.
- Phu Quoc (p11): 30-day visa exemption for foreign overnight visitors "as of 2014"; mainland still needs a visa. Lead: the exemption still exists in some form (Phu Quoc direct arrivals) — verify current rule; cross-check with facts.json.
- Vaccinations (p12): none required; CDC recommends Hep A + typhoid; rabies for cyclists/animal contact. Malaria: low prevalence, rare in the Mekong Delta; most travellers just use repellent. → This agrees with the CDC position in facts.json and is the mildest of the three books (LP said rural south high risk).
- Transport summary (p12): most arrive at SGN or HAN; for short hops the road is cheapest; for long hauls (Hanoi→central, central→Nha Trang) fly or train. Budget carriers named: Jetstar and Vietjet (Jetstar Pacific has since been rebranded Pacific Airlines — obsolete name). Overnight trains from hard seat to a/c sleeper.
- Motorbike-the-length-of-the-country culture (p12): long-term travellers buy a bike and sell it at the end; the book presents this as the "most independent" option with no licence/insurance caveat here. Outside coastal cities, little English; bring phrasebook and map. **Cars cannot be self-driven by foreigners under the rules as the book states them — a Vietnamese driver is required.** (Agrees with DK/LP "never self-drive".)
- Packing (p12–13): sunscreen hard to find and dear — bring it; contact-lens solution and feminine products hard to find. Dress: Vietnam "more formal than the United States"; rural women cover shoulders and wear long trousers; city women dress Western; shorts/tank tops fine in tourist areas; at pagodas and national sites both sexes cover shoulders and knees as a mark of respect.

## Best of Vietnam: "Down the Dragon's Spine" (p14–18) — two-week spine, NORTH→SOUTH
- Framing (p14): the country is at points barely 30 miles wide but the north–south distance is likened to Boston→Miami, on worse and busier roads — a US-scale analogy worth adopting in our own words. Two weeks brisk, three relaxed.
- Direction: HAN in → SGN out (same as DK, opposite to LP). Moon gives no reason either. Three books: 2 say north→south, 1 south→north; none argue it. → routing swamp still has no published argument anywhere.
- Day 1–2 Hanoi (weekend): Old Quarter by cyclo on day one ("from the safety of your seat" — the cyclo as a way to survive traffic on arrival), Hoan Kiem, cathedral cafés, Hoa Lo, Women's Museum, French Quarter, water puppets or ca tru. Day 2: pho breakfast, mausoleum queue early, stilt house, One Pillar, Military History Museum, Temple of Literature, bia hoi at rush hour.
- Days 3–4 Halong: 2-day/1-night cruise; four hours' drive each way (2015 — the expressway has since cut this to roughly 2.5 h; obsolete figure), lunch aboard, caves, sunrise if clear, kayaking, dock back at Ha Long City around noon. Then overnight train to Danang OR a budget flight — "roughly the same cost" (lead; today flights are often cheaper than soft sleepers). Train leaves in the evening; flyers can stay another night in Hanoi.
- Alt days 3–4 Sapa instead of Halong (for non-boat people): overnight train to Lao Cai, minibus up; museum, Cau May street, Cat Cat village, hire a motorbike for the waterfalls and Tram Ton Pass; arrange next-day trek with a local guide or town outfit; trek a few hours with lunch; back by overnight train or bus. (Moon 2015 predates the Noi Bai–Lao Cai expressway buses that now make the bus the default; obsolete framing.)
- Days 5–7 Hoi An (three days, "long weekend"): arrive Danang late morning by train or noon by air, cab to Hoi An. Order tailored clothes on day one to leave time for fittings — the one concrete sequencing rule in the chapter. Day 6 bicycle: Tra Que vegetable village, An Bang (quiet) vs Cua Dai (busier) beaches. Day 7: Marble Mountains + Danang (Cham Museum, Son Tra, Linh Ung) OR stay for a food tour / cooking class / photo tour. Leave by night bus or Danang→Nha Trang train.
- Days 8–9 Nha Trang: arrive early morning; beach or mud baths; Long Son Pagoda, Po Nagar; nightlife (Sailing Club, backpacker bars). Day 9 full-day boat tour (snorkel/dive/cruise), then overnight train or flight to HCMC.
- Days 10–11 HCMC: Notre Dame, Post Office, Reunification Palace, Turtle Lake, War Remnants Museum; rooftop bars; Tan Dinh for dinner. Day 11: Cholon (Binh Tay Market, Thien Hau), Ben Thanh, Le Loi, Rex rooftop.
- Days 12–13 Mekong (whirlwind): bus to Can Tho, Ninh Kieu Park at night, Cai Rang at dawn, bus back same afternoon. Last night jazz/rooftops in HCMC.
- Observations: Hue is NOT in Moon's two-week spine at all (DK gives it two days, LP one night). Phong Nha not in the spine. Three books, three Hue weightings — a real fork (decision gap).

## Best beaches (p16)
Con Dao (best diving, hardest to reach), Mui Ne (resort, kitesurf capital), Nha Trang (party; Doc Let to the north quieter), Ninh Chu at Phan Rang (sleepy, "authentic", anonymous), Phu Quoc (couples and families; Long Beach lively, Sao Beach pristine, Ganh Dau/Bai Dai for nature in the north). Matches LP's Doc Let aside; adds Ninh Chu which neither DK nor LP pushes.

## Touring the Mekong Delta (p19–21) — the most detailed Delta routing in any of the three books
- Delta pitched as "authentic, off the beaten track", slower, less infrastructure, fewer English speakers. Range: one day (My Tho or Vinh Long) to a week along the Tien River to Chau Doc and on to Cambodia.
- Route A (long weekend from HCMC, 4 days): Western bus station → Vinh Long; homestay on An Binh Island (arrange via a travel outfit, at the ferry, or just cross and ask — Moon is relaxed about walking up unbooked); cycle the island, church, orchards, Viet Artisans workshop, sunset, dinner with hosts. Day 2 choice: Cai Be floating market early (vibrancy gone by late morning) OR linger and go to Can Tho. Can Tho: Ong Pagoda, Khmer wat, museum; arrange Cai Rang tour (independent boat at Ninh Kieu Pier vs hotel/tour); Open Tour CT nightlife tour. Day 3 Cai Rang before sunrise, most of the morning; bus to Tra Vinh. Day 4 Tra Vinh by bicycle: Ba Om Pond, Ang Pagoda, Khmer Cultural Museum, Ong and Ong Met pagodas, Hang Pagoda.
- Route B (to the Cambodian border, 6 days): Vinh Long/An Binh → Cai Be market → Sa Dec (Huynh Thuy Le house, Kien An Cung pagoda, flower gardens) → Cao Lanh → Tram Chim NP early for sarus cranes (230+ species; "best before late morning") → Chau Doc (Sam Mountain via Tay An Pagoda steps or xe om; Cave Pagoda; Lady Xu temple) → Bassac boat (fish farms, floating houses, floating market), Cham quarter and Mubarak Mosque, Victoria Chau Doc for a drink → Tra Su bird sanctuary (cajuput forest, rowboat, watchtower), Ba Chuc for history → cross to Cambodia next day.
- Why it matters: our Mekong swamp (day vs overnight) has only the My Tho/Can Tho axis; Moon gives a second axis (Tra Vinh Khmer culture, Sa Dec, Tram Chim birds, Chau Doc Cham) that neither DK nor LP's planning chapters develop. Coverage gap: Tra Vinh, Sa Dec, Tram Chim, Chau Doc's Cham community.

## Essentials — Getting there (p491–492) — the only "From North America" section in any of the three books
- Three international gateways in 2015: SGN, HAN, DAD (Cam Ranh/Nha Trang, Phu Quoc, Da Lat have since gained international service — obsolete list).
- Airfare is the single biggest cost. 2015 pattern: monitor 6–8 weeks out; round trips "hover around" USD 1,000, ±100–200; West Coast slightly cheaper, East Coast and middle of the country should expect four figures. All 2015 LEADS; do not print. The *structure* is still usable: West Coast gateways cheaper, transpacific via Seoul/Hong Kong/Tokyo/Taipei hubs, a minority of routings via Europe/Middle East. Trick named: fly a budget domestic hop to LAX and start the transpacific leg there.
- Carrier quality opinion (2015): China Eastern/China Southern cheapest but weak on service/safety reputation; EVA, JAL, Emirates, Cathay, United, Singapore, Qatar reliable "for a few extra dollars" and worth it on a 14-hour flight. Book opinion; United no longer serves Vietnam nonstop in this era, but the lesson (pay for the carrier on a 14+ h flight) is durable.
- Overland crossings open to foreigners in 2015: 5 Cambodia, 6 Laos, 3 China. Lead: counts change. No visa at any land border; no international railway (the Nanning–Hanoi through-train existed then and now — Moon is wrong or ignoring it; conflict with LP which lists the China rail link). Daily international buses.
- Regional budget carriers (AirAsia, Jetstar, Tiger) from Bangkok/KL/Singapore; fees for everything. Flights from Laos/Cambodia dearer than from big hubs.
- Organized tours (p492): cheap mass-market day trips (book quotes ~VND 100,000 in 2015, lead) are "cookie-cutter"; her rule: do it yourself unless it's a Sapa-style trek where a guide is worth paying. Independent boutique outfits dearer but good value split among several people.

## Essentials — Getting around (p492–495)
- Air: three domestic carriers in 2015 (Vietnam Airlines, Jetstar Pacific, Vietjet); Moon rates Jetstar best on fares+service and Vietjet cheapest on sales (as low as VND 100,000 — lead/obsolete); Vietnam Airlines wider network, exclusive to Con Dao and Phu Quoc in 2015 (Phu Quoc now has multiple carriers; Con Dao still essentially one — Bamboo entered later; verify).
- Rail: single coastal line SGN–HAN via Nha Trang and Danang. Sleepers reasonably comfortable, dearer than sleeper buses which are "slightly less safe" but more frequent. Her argument for overnight trains: the fare replaces a hotel night. Train preferable in the mountainous north where roads make buses unpleasant (i.e., Sapa). Recommends seat61.com — a durable, citable third-party resource we can name.
- Bus (p492–493): cheapest. Named dangerous routes: **Hue→Hanoi** and the **road up to Sapa** — take train/motorbike/hired car instead. Reputable operators named: Sinh Tourist, Phuong Trang (Futa). Rule: pay the few dollars for the big operator; breakdowns on cheap lines cost time. Theft on overnight buses is a real pattern — keep bags on your lap/body; travellers wake up missing things.
- Taxi (p493): abundant; flag down in the street; Mai Linh the most reputable national brand; always check for a working meter; 2015 flagfall VND 10–15k (lead; today ~VND 11–20k and Grab dominates — obsolete). **Never bargain a taxi fare** — bargaining is never to your advantage with a metered cab.
- Xe om (p493–494): the catcall "You! Motorbike!" is part of the street; compared to Manhattan cabbies; the helmets provided are useless but legally required — insist on one anyway; write the address down; agree the price before boarding; drivers may try to renegotiate mid-ride — hold to the agreed figure. **Night rule: taxi, not xe om** — robberies and passengers thrown off bikes after dark, driver sometimes complicit; drunk drivers. (Grab has since largely absorbed xe om for tourists; the night rule still applies to street bikes.)
- Motorbike rental (p494): 2015 rates VND 80–200k/day (lead/obsolete). Collateral: deposit or passport — businesses have been known to invent fees once they hold your passport, so use recommended shops only; check brakes and fuel gauge; agree terms. Daily rentals are for in-town use; taking one on the highway without telling the shop puts you at fault if anything happens. Exceptions she names: Hoi An↔Danang, Nha Trang→Dai Lanh. Buy-and-sell for the length of the country is common in HCMC/Hanoi backpacker districts.
- Hired cars (p494–495): always with a driver — foreigners cannot drive without a local licence. Agree exactly which fees are in and out (fuel, tolls, driver's meals, parking) before leaving. → confirms decision-gap #52 (who pays fuel): Moon says negotiate it, closest to a rule.
- **Driving law (p495)**: a Vietnamese licence is required, so tourists driving is illegal; enforcement varies; most expats and many Vietnamese ride unlicensed; children ride. Driving on the right, motorbikes in the far-right lane. **"Turning right on red is permitted, except in Hanoi"** — CONFLICTS with LP (no right turn on red) and with the fact-wishlist assumption; needs a current primary source (Vietnam road traffic law 2024 revision). Honking is defensive signalling. Buses/trucks "play chicken" with bikes. Police rarely stop foreigners outside Hanoi/HCMC; the legal remedy is impound + fine but in practice money changes hands.
- Bribes (p497): the only bribe a tourist is likely to face is a traffic stop; "refusing is a bad idea"; the police can legally impound the bike. Frank where DK/LP are coy. For the site this is a sensitive framing — our line stays: don't ride unlicensed; if stopped, be polite; US Embassy guidance governs.

## Visas and officialdom (p495–496) — ALL OBSOLETE, record for conflict only
- 2015 regime: visa required before arrival for all foreigners; 1 or 3 months, single/multiple; USD 100 (1-mo single) to 180 (3-mo multiple) at embassies/consulates (DC, SF, Houston, NYC; in-person applications mornings only); fees in USD; extensions via agencies (~USD 30 agency vs USD 10 official — lead). What Moon calls "e-visa processing" is the VOA approval-letter route (2–4 business days, two-page letter, passport photo, stamping fee USD 45–95 + agency USD 15–30, all cash in USD, only at SGN/HAN/DAD). Moon notes the government did not publicize it. **Everything here is superseded** by the real e-visa (2017; 90-day multiple-entry since Aug 2023, fee USD 25/50). Record in disputes.md as the third book contradicting facts.json on visas; useful only to warn readers off 2015-era advice still floating online.
- US Embassy Hanoi / Consulate HCMC listed with 2015 addresses/phones (lead — the embassy has moved to a new compound; verify before printing any address). Note: US consular services cannot help with Vietnamese visas.
- Police (p496): Transparency International survey cited — 37% of Vietnamese named the police the most corrupt institution (2015 lead). Officers avoid foreigners because of language; bring a Vietnamese speaker to any police contact.

## Accommodation (p497–498)
- Noise rule: lower floors and street-facing rooms are louder; windows are not guaranteed; asking to see the room first is normal. Hot water + a/c standard; elevators not.
- Discounts by season/length/occupancy; Agoda/Booking sometimes cheaper, sometimes dearer than the desk — check both. Confirm reservations once before arrival; OTAs lose bookings.
- **Passport at check-in** (p497): hotels must register guests with police; holding the passport is standard and partly insurance against walkouts; you may ask for it back after the form is filled, but may then be asked to prepay. This is the clearest statement across three books: the copy-vs-original fork (decision gaps #30/#53) resolves to "ask for it back after registration; expect to prepay".
- No reservation needed in big-city backpacker zones (Pham Ngu Lao, Old Quarter); book ahead if you want a nicer place; ask the price up front.
- Types: khach san (hotel; government star rating tracks building size not quality — 2–4 star is a lottery; boutiques better and dearer), nha nghi (guesthouse, 5–6 rooms, bare-bones, cheapest), hostels only in big cities and few are good, **homestay** increasingly means a remote guesthouse with meals — less family contact than the word implies; Hoi An "homestays" are really B&Bs. Camping = bungalow/cabin/tent in parks or on beaches; wild camping not accepted.

## Food (p499–501)
- Structure: rice or noodle base + greens + meat/tofu; portions smaller than US; cheap enough for seconds.
- Street food: carts and shoulder poles everywhere; her transparency argument — you can see an open-air kitchen, so you can judge cleanliness, which a restaurant hides. Street-only dishes: xoi (sticky rice, coloured with leaves, sweet or savoury), hot tofu pudding with ginger, fried mung-bean rice cakes.
- Three regional cuisines (p499–500): North meat-heavy (chicken, beef), chao porridge, the "officially" best pho, bun cha. Centre spicy — bun bo Hue and Hue's bite-sized banh (beo, duc, loc, hoi). South sweeter, seafood-heavy, more frying (banh xeo, Vung Tau banh khot).
- "Stranger side" box (p500): century eggs; dog (mostly north; Saigon has one street; government ban + rabies risk + stolen pets + smuggling — she frames it as declining and ethically bad); balut (hot vit lon, 19–21-day embryo, eaten with salt/lime and beer); pigeon and forest birds in the north; rice-paddy rat in the Delta (seasonal, "cleaner than city rats"); snake ritual in the north (blood and bile shots, heart swallowed by guest of honour). → Answers decision-gap #33 vocabulary need: the words to know are *thit cho* (dog), *hot vit lon* (balut), *chuot dong* (field rat), *ran* (snake).
- Beer (p500–501): Saigon Red (south), Huda (centre); bia hoi/bia tuoi fresh lager in 100-litre kegs, ~VND 4,000 a glass in 2015 (lead/obsolete), must be drunk the day it's made; a Hanoi after-work ritual worth doing for the scene not the beer. Rice wine (ruou) up to ~22% ABV, home-made, ubiquitous in the countryside and at Tet; befriending villagers earns you shots.
- Drinking culture box (p501): mostly male; slogans "100%" and "can't go home till drunk"; "mot hai ba, dzo!"; beer with ice; do nhau drinking snacks (snails, grilled meat). Danger: drink-driving is routine — never ride pillion with a drinker; methanol poisoning from counterfeit spirits/home brew is rare but can disable or kill. → safety article material: counterfeit spirits is in none of our articles.

## Conduct and customs (p501–503)
- Politeness culture: voices rarely raised; **awkward laughter is the standard response to a problem** — not mockery; showing anger reads as loss of self-control and makes the other party shut down. Queue-jumping is common; a polite "excuse me" usually works, with feigned surprise.
- Dress (p502): conservative outside big cities; below-the-knee for women and long trousers for men in offices and temples; nightlife dress is anything-goes. Her rule of thumb: the same instinct as the US — you wouldn't wear flip-flops to church or work.
- Body language (p502): bowing matters less than in Thailand/Cambodia; hand things over with both hands, or right hand with left hand on the right elbow; crossed fingers is rude; beckon palm-down; don't touch heads (esp. children); don't point soles at people or the Buddha.
- Table manners (p502–503): slurping, talking with a full mouth, and shouting "em oi!" for the waiter are normal — servers don't check on quiet tables and only bring the bill when asked; at local places napkins/toothpicks go on the floor if the floor already shows it; family meals: your bowl gets refilled, people put food in your bowl, take what's nearest, lift the bowl to your mouth for the last rice, food goes into your bowl before your mouth. Chopsticks: parallel, never crossed, never a V, never pointed, never upright in rice (incense = death).

## Travel tips (p503–505)
- Language (p503): simplify requests — drop the politeness padding that confuses weak English speakers; "Excuse me, where is the toilet?" beats the American long form. Agree cost and expectations before any service. Patience and calm earn respect.
- Packing (p503–504): sunscreen rarely used locally, overpriced and weak when found — bring it; same for repellent; tampons rarely stocked; breathable clothes, sturdy shoes; one long-sleeved/knee-length outfit for pagodas; hat for the shadeless Delta and Nha Trang; one nice outfit for city nightlife; raincoats optional — plastic ponchos are everywhere and cheap.
- Work/study (p504): ESL teaching path, requirements (bachelor's, TESOL/CELTA, police check, medical), bring notarised degree and police check from home. Out of scope for us except as a "moving to Vietnam" lead.
- **Accessibility (p504–505)**: hard for wheelchair users — few elevators outside big cities, broken pavements, sights need mobility; HCMC, Danang, Hanoi best equipped; Danang/Hoi An businesses used to older travellers and better set up. → coverage gap "accessibility" gets its first substantive source; agrees with LP's "car with driver" verdict.
- Solo women (p505): safe; marriage-proposal banter and occasional pestering; polite-but-firm then ignore.
- LGBTQ (p505): accepting in cities, conservative countryside; PDA discouraged for all couples; 2015 law change — the ban on same-sex *weddings* was lifted (Law on Marriage and Family 2014, in force Jan 2015) but marriage is not recognised; she flags the distinction. Context, durable.

## Health and safety (p505)
- CDC named as the authority (a US-reader tell): routine + Hep A + typhoid; Hep B, Japanese encephalitis and rabies pre-exposure for remote/long stays.
- Malaria (p505): **Moon contradicts itself** — p12 says rare, Delta only, most skip tablets; p505 says CDC recommends prophylaxis for rural southern areas and names drug-resistance and side effects (nausea, photosensitivity, anxiety, hallucinations, seizures — i.e., mefloquine era). Both books now (DK, LP, Moon) hedge differently → the dossier verdict must come straight from the current CDC Vietnam page, which lists specific provinces. Our disputes entry: three books, three readings.

## Health and safety continued (p506–509)
- Allergies (p506): shellfish and peanuts pervasive; cross-contamination unmanageable at carts; explanations may not be understood; carry an EpiPen. → No article covers food allergies; coverage gap.
- Traveller's diarrhoea (p506): she rejects the "no street food, no ice, no greens" rule as expensive and joyless. Her decision rule: skip carts whose kitchen looks dirty or whose food has sat out; hot, fried, grilled dishes are fine; a good stall cooks in plain sight; herbs come on the side with pho/bun bo/banh xeo so you can decline them. Ice in backpacker/higher-end places is safe; tap water for showers and teeth only; everyone drinks bottled water or tra da. Buy Imodium at home; US pharmacy safer than Vietnamese. → resolves decision-gap #31 (street-food rule) as book opinion, and agrees with LP on ice.
- Dengue (p506): present in cities and countryside, no vaccine (2015; Qdenga exists now but is not US-approved — verify CDC), acetaminophen only, never aspirin/ibuprofen/naproxen; warning signs listed (breathing trouble, clammy skin, vomiting, nose/gum bleeding, red spots) — the danger window is as the fever recedes. Durable medical context; cite CDC.
- Methanol (p506–507): counterfeit spirits sold to bars that may not know; symptoms up to 72 h later and mimic a hangover; worst in backpacker zones, named Saigon and Nha Trang; rules — too-cheap drinks are suspect, buy spirits at supermarkets/chain stores not corner shops, stop if a drink tastes too sweet/harsh or looks off, higher-end bars safer, fall back to beer and wine. → safety article gap; the rules are durable.
- Rabies (p507): people still die of it yearly; monkeys, cats, dogs, bats; don't touch pets; wash with soap then seek care; 5 post-exposure shots unvaccinated vs 2 if pre-vaccinated (lead — current schedules are 4 and 2; verify CDC); vaccine widely available in-country. Dogs chase cyclists.
- Insects (p507): cover at dawn/dusk; buy repellent at home or in Thailand; windows shut, a/c or net; bed bugs occasionally in dorms.
- Medical services (p508): public hospitals overcrowded; international clinics in big cities fine for minor cases; serious cases airlifted to Singapore/Bangkok; insurance must cover it. Cash-up-front hospitals; evacuations "hundreds of thousands of dollars" (lead; order of magnitude is right). **Most travel insurance excludes motorbike accidents if the rider is unlicensed** — the clearest statement of the insurance point in any of the three books; matches facts.json getting_around.unlicensed_insurance. Travel Guard named (book's pick; not ours).
- Crime (p508–509): mostly business rule-bending; violence rarely touches foreigners; petty theft is the big one, worst at night and in backpacker areas; motorbike bag-snatching that drags victims — walk away from the kerb, bag on the far side, both backpack straps, bag hugged to chest when riding pillion, necklaces tucked in, step into a shop to use a phone. "Robbed multiple times on a single trip" happens. Night rule: don't walk alone, no alleys, taxi not xe om. If robbed, let it go. Drugs: widely available and zero-tolerance; large possession can mean death.

## Information and services (p509–512)
- Currency (p509): dong notes VND 500 to 500,000 (coins absent from her list — they had already vanished from circulation). **Refuse torn notes, especially VND 10,000+** — shops reject them. 2015 rate ~VND 21,000/USD, businesses using 20–22k (lead/obsolete; now ~25–26k). Oanda/XE for rates.
- Changing money: kiosks not bank-affiliated but safe — counterfeits "not a major problem"; gold shops exchange; swap worn notes.
- ATMs (p509–510): everywhere; foreign banks ANZ/HSBC/Citi/Commonwealth (ANZ and Commonwealth have since left retail; obsolete) and domestic Sacombank/Techcombank/Vietcombank; fees "rarely exceed VND 10,000" (2015; now typically VND 20–50k plus low caps — conflict with corrections #1 → disputes.md). Bank hours 8–5, domestic close 4–4:30 and **break for lunch 11–1:30**; some Saturday mornings.
- Traveller's cheques (p510): nearly useless even in 2015; bring cards.
- Costs (p510): everything negotiable; street meal VND 15–20k vs restaurant 200k in 2015 (lead/obsolete) — you pay for atmosphere, not better food; **ask the price before eating street-side; doubling/tripling the bill at the end is the known trick**. Tourist-heavy places (Phu Quoc, Hoi An) dearer; Delta and Central Highlands a fraction.
- Bargaining (p511): expected where no price tag; survey a few vendors then offer 20–40% below the going ask (book heuristic); never haggle unless you intend to buy — haggling for sport provokes anger.
- **Tipping (p511) — a FOURTH opinion**: not required or customary outside high-end restaurants; some places add a service charge; spas/massage are the exception where tips are expected and a gratuity form may appear (VND 40k+ in 2015, lead); elsewhere whatever you like. This is the most restrained of the four book positions (DK hotel US$1 / DK restaurant 10% / LP "appreciated" / Moon "not customary except spas"). → disputes.md tipping entry gets a fourth line; dossier still owes a single rule.
- Maps (p511): road atlas "Tap Ban Do Giao Thong Duong Bo Viet Nam" ~VND 300k (obsolete — Google Maps era). Tourist offices: Hanoi/Hoi An saturated with agents; Con Dao has one or two English-speaking businesses.
- Post (p511): postcards cheap, 2–3 weeks; parcels dear and unreliable → ship purchases via a courier, not the post (relevant to decision-gap #34).
- Phones (p511–512): +84; 2015 area codes (since renumbered in 2017 — all numbers in the book are obsolete); pay-as-you-go SIMs sold with cheap handsets (eSIM era now). Wi-Fi widespread even in 2015. English media: Tuoi Tre News, Thanh Nien/Vietweek (closed), Saigon Times, expat monthlies (AsiaLIFE, The Word, Oi), Saigoneer, Hanoi Grapevine — several defunct; Saigoneer survives.
- Metric throughout; Celsius, km, kg.

## Hanoi — services and transport (p71–75)
- Medical (p71): Hong Ngoc (local, simple), Family Medical Practice (international, 24 h), International SOS (pricey). Pharmacies (nha thuoc tay) stock tampons and lens solution — contradicts her own packing advice; Hang Gai and Phu Doan pharmacies. US Embassy at 7 Lang Ha with consular services in the Rose Garden Building by appointment only (2015; the embassy relocated — verify before any address). Laundry ~VND 25k/kg street vs 35k hotel; damp winters slow drying (small, true, durable).
- Noi Bai (p71–72): 20 miles north, ~45 min by taxi (now 30–40 min via the Nhat Tan bridge; obsolete). Hotel pickup from VND 350k (2015). **Airport taxi scams** named: extortionate flat quotes; "your hotel is closed/full" to divert to a commission hotel; rigged fast meters. Her rules: write the hotel name and address down; use the official taxi stand with its posted flat rate; reconfirm the price; Mai Linh Airport; "should not exceed" VND 400k in 2015 (lead/obsolete; today's metered fare and Grab are both lower). Vietnam Airlines minibus VND 40k to the downtown VNA office — the cheap option for light packers (minibus no longer runs; replaced by Express Bus 86 — obsolete). Public bus 17 to Long Bien (VND 5k, 2015) and bus 7; Google Maps' Hanoi bus data was accurate even in 2015.
- Train station (p72): Hanoi station serves the south and the Sapa overnight; buy at the station, online, or via agencies (some charge excess commission). Station taxis: several times the price, fast meters; Mai Linh or a fixed price; ~VND 50k to Hoan Kiem in 2015; walk a block away and prices normalise.
- Bus stations (p73): Giap Bat (south: Ninh Binh, Hue), Luong Yen and Gia Lam (Halong, Haiphong — Luong Yen since closed), My Dinh (Lao Cai, northwest). Or skip the stations and take Sinh Tourist / Hoang Long coaches from Old Quarter offices. **"Sinh Cafe" copycats** — only two genuine Sinh Tourist addresses; everything else is an impostor (same copycat pattern DK flagged for hotels).
- Bus-station taxi swarm (p73): drivers crowd the bus door; walk to the street and a block away before taking a cab.
- Taxis in town (p74): Mai Linh and Taxi Group reputable; hail away from Ma May / Ly Quoc Su tourist streets; meter always, flat rates never favour you; carry near-exact change (drivers claim no small notes); if the meter is clearly rigged, stop, pay what's fair, get out — don't argue.
- Xe om (p74): "Hello! Motorbike!"; Old Quarter to most central sights ≤ VND 40k in 2015 (lead); insist on a helmet.
- Cyclo (p74): the safest way to see the Old Quarter on arrival; about an hour; bargain; ~VND 100k/hour in 2015 (lead); expect to pay more than a motorbike because a human is pedalling.
- Public bus (p74–75): VND 5–7k; buses 9 and 14 from the north end of Hoan Kiem reach the mausoleum, Temple of Literature, Flag Tower, Museum of Ethnology; routes end 8–9 pm. Electric cars from Dong Xuan Market (VND 150k/35 min, 250k/hour per 7-seat car, 2015).
- Motorbike hire (p75): Ngo Huyen and Dinh Liet; foreign-owned shops ~VND 210k vs Vietnamese shops ~100k (2015); collateral = ID or cash; lap the block first; helmet required by law. Old Quarter has few lights or stop signs; one-way streets; **"turning right on red is forbidden in the capital"** — consistent with p495 (allowed elsewhere). → Moon's position: right-on-red legal outside Hanoi. Needs the 2024 Road Traffic Order/Safety Law check.
- Perfume Pagoda (p75): 40 miles west, 30+ pagodas, cave at the summit, VND 50k + boat (2015), Hanoi's most popular day trip. Van Phuc silk village (p78): 1,000+ looms, 9th-century origins, Nguyen royal supplier, finished goods cheaper than in the city, raw silk the same; shops 8/9–5; buses 1 and 2 via Highway 6.

## Sapa (p78–80)
- Framing: minority cultures (H'mong, Dao, Tay, Phu La, Giay), terraces, Fansipan. French hill station from the early 20th c.; Kinh settlement from the 1960s; real growth only in the 1990s.
- **Best months: September–October, before the harvest strips the terraces, or early spring for valley views.** Nov–Feb cold (only Vietnamese town that sees snow), heavy fog spoils the point. → settles decision-gap #45 against LP's "November": two of three books (Moon, reality) say Sep–Oct.
- Sights: market (6–6, local staples, northwestern greens/teas), museum (stilt house, French-era history, thin on minorities — get a guide for that), Our Lady of the Rosary (1926, steps as a meeting place), Cat Cat village (Black H'mong, VND 40k 2015, 3-mile round trip on paved paths, "more tourist attraction than village").

## HCMC — transport (p360–362; p359 OCR blank, photo/map page)
- Tan Son Nhat taxis (p360): metered, not flat; ~40 min and VND 150–180k to District 1 in 2015 (lead/obsolete); tell the dispatcher your destination. **Xe om from the airport** for solo light travellers: walk out past the overpass to the road, ~25 min, ≤ VND 80k (2015) — a Moon-only tip; today Grab bike does the same job from a marked pickup zone. Public bus 152 to Ben Thanh, VND 5k, 40 min, no English (bus 152 still runs; 109 added later).
- Saigon station (p360): District 3, terminus of the Reunification line; trains from Nha Trang arrive early morning, Phan Thiet late evening; 15 min to downtown; bus 7 to Ben Thanh.
- Buses (p360): tourist buses from Can Tho / Nha Trang / Dalat / Mui Ne drop at Pham Ngu Lao (23-9 Park, De Tham); Phuong Trang and Sinh Tourist offices on De Tham. Three public stations: An Suong (NW, 9 miles), Mien Tay (west, 9 miles, Mekong), Mien Dong (east, 4 miles, everything else — the long-distance Mien Dong has since moved far out to Thu Duc; obsolete). You will rarely need them.
- Riding in (p360–361): Highway 1 is the direct and worst route; from the Delta take Vo Van Kiet to Ben Thanh; overshoot and the Thu Thiem tunnel dumps you in District 2.
- Taxis in town (p361): **Mai Linh (green/white) and Vinasun (green/white/red), especially at night**; others fine by day; long-routing is the common trick; never bargain, always meter; hail from the street; late at night have a Vietnamese speaker phone one. (Grab/Xanh SM have since changed the calculus; the two brands remain the honest street-hail choice.)
- Xe om (p361): fastest way around; agree and hold the price; ask a local what they'd pay; **at night, taxis**.
- Cyclo (p361): drivers often speak English and act as guides for District 1; pay more than a xe om; not for anyone in a hurry. (LP's HCMC-cyclo mugging warning is absent in Moon — the author lives there; note the disagreement, decision-gap #56.)
- City buses (p361): green, 5 am–7:30/9 pm, ~VND 5k in town, saigonbus.net map, paper maps at the Ben Thanh station; blue signs or covered stops.
- Motorbike hire (p362): VND 100–120k/day in Pham Ngu Lao (2015); test-ride; **check you have the registration card**; Chi's Cafe and Trung's named; buying a bike — have a mechanic inspect it first. "Essentially no rules" on the road — obey lights and laws as at home anyway.
- Cu Chi (p362): VND 90k (2015); dug by hand from the 1940s against the French; ~155 miles by the 1960s; initially bomb shelters, then recycled unexploded ordnance into grenades; free-strike zone; four levels, 2.5–4 ft wide, 2.5–6 ft high; kitchens, hospitals, theatres, births underground. Useful context for the Cu Chi article's "why it matters" paragraph; the framing "American War" is the Vietnamese usage, which Moon adopts.

## Background — wildlife and history (p479–482)
- Wildlife (p479): Hoan Kiem's Yangtze giant softshell turtle (the lake's last one died in 2016 — obsolete as stated); 200+ snake species, 60 venomous; 850+ bird species, most endemics in mainland SE Asia; Edwards's pheasant critically endangered; Tra Su and Tram Chim for birders.
- History frame (p479): struggle and resilience; the 20th century alone had wars with France, the US, Cambodia and China; nationalism therefore runs deep and crosses age, religion and politics; post-war poverty then boom; citizens ignore politics while advancement is possible. → aligns with DK's "two thousand years of China, fifteen of America" thesis and LP's "Vietnam Today"; Moon adds the Cambodia and China wars explicitly, which our history article should too.
- Ancient (p479–480): Funan in the Delta (c. AD 100–6th c.); Champa on the south-central coast (2nd c.–mid-1600s, Hindu, matriarchal, red-brick towers, absorbed by the Vietnamese); Dong Son in the Red River Delta (1st millennium BC, bronze drums, irrigation); Hung kings semi-mythical, 18 generations; An Duong Vuong 3rd c. BC.
- Trung sisters box (p480): AD 39 revolt after the elder sister's husband was executed; queen for ~3 years; Chinese return AD 42; suicide in a river; "Hai Ba Trung" on street signs nationwide; emblem of nationalism and of powerful women. → street-name decoder material (a coverage gap: "why every city has the same street names").
- Chinese millennium (p480): 208 BC–907; early Han laxity, then taxes and forced sinicisation; Ngo Quyen takes independence when the Tang fall.
- Independent Vietnam (p480–481): Ly dynasty — first civil-service exams 1075, mandatory by 1089; Buddhism as state religion; conquest of Cham Vijaya 1079; Tran dynasty from 1225; three Mongol invasions; Tran Hung Dao's stakes at Bach Dang. Ho Quy Ly's 1400 coup invites the Ming (1407–1428, harsh sinicisation); Le Loi expels them and sends the losers home with ships.
- Nguyen/Trinh split (p481): from 1545 Nguyen south, Trinh north; Alexandre de Rhodes 1619–1630, 6,000 converts, credited with the quoc ngu Romanised script. Tay Son brothers (p481–482) from Binh Dinh, 1771 revolt, Robin Hood policies, rule the whole country; Nguyen Anh escapes to Gia Dinh (Saigon), bishop Pigneau de Behaine fails to get Louis XVI's help, raises his own force; Tay Son defeated 1801; Gia Long crowned, unified Vietnam after 200 years; Minh Mang expels missionaries and kills Catholics.
- French (p482): Danang 1858, Gia Dinh 1859; the "protect the missionaries" pretext found no Catholic uprising; full control by 1874, formal colony 1883; Tu Duc discredited; extraction economy, rice exports, profits to France; scholars boycott; WWI conscription; Ho Chi Minh founds the Revolutionary Youth League in Guangzhou 1925.

## Background — 20th century to today (p483–486)
- 1930 conference; Nghe An / Ha Tinh risings bombed. Ho returns 1941 after 30 years abroad; Viet Minh exploit French weakness under Japanese occupation; March 1945 Japanese takeover; August Revolution; Bao Dai abdicates; independence declared 2 Sep 1945 in Hanoi.
- Franco-Vietnam War (p483–484): 1946–54; French hold cities, Viet Minh the countryside; PRC arms from 1949; Dien Bien Phu besieged March 1954 with 100,000+ troops, falls as Geneva opens 8 May; partition at the 17th parallel with 1956 elections nobody expected to happen.
- Diem (p484): US-backed Catholic with little political base; rigged 1955 referendum; strategic hamlets backfire and breed NLF recruits; 1963 Thich Quang Duc self-immolation, Madame Nhu's "barbecue" remark; generals sound out the US, coup and assassination November 1963; JFK killed weeks later; Johnson escalates; Gulf of Tonkin 1964; 485,000 troops by end 1967; Agent Orange.
- Tet 1968 (p485): 31 Jan, five cities, 36 provincial capitals, 64 district capitals, two dozen airfields; militarily reversed, politically decisive in the US. Nixon withdraws from 1969 (500,000+ at peak), Vietnamization, Paris talks; My Lai and the Cambodia bombings become public; 27 Jan 1973 agreement, US units out by end of March; South collapses; 30 Apr 1975 tanks at the palace gates. "30-year conflict" is Moon's framing (1945–75).
- After 1975 (p485–486): isolation and paranoia; China border clashes and the two-year Cambodia war, resolved by end 1979 (Moon's compression — the Cambodian occupation ran to 1989; lead/inaccurate); re-education camps as hard labour; surveillance; austerity economy fails; doi moi mid-1980s; opening by 1990; poverty 60% → 17% in two decades (lead; World Bank series would verify).
- Government (p486): one-party state; National Assembly 500 seats, five-year terms, meets twice a year, elects president who appoints the PM; Politburo; two-term limits (since loosened in practice — lead). Elections: only the Assembly is popularly elected and candidates are pre-selected.
- Economy (p486–487): agriculture to industry via doi moi; FDI concentrates in HCMC; wealth on the coast, poverty in the mountains and interior falling hardest on minorities; top-10 remittance recipient (durable: the Vietnamese-American remittance link is a real "who our reader is" angle); ultra-wealthy class of USD 30m+ (2015).

## People and culture (p487–489)
- 54 ethnic groups; Kinh ~87%; population 88–90 million in 2015 (now ~100m; obsolete figure); loosely enforced two-child norm with signage; 90%+ literacy.
- Geography of minorities: Khmer and Muslim Cham in the Delta; Gia Rai, E De, Churu in the Central Highlands; Dao, Giay, Thai, H'mong in the northwest; "montagnards" was the French term; most diverse and poorest areas coincide.
- Religion: Mahayana Buddhist majority with Taoist/Confucian strands; sizeable Catholic minority; Caodaism (Victor Hugo among its saints — Moon also says Elvis, which is wrong/jokey; don't repeat) and Hoa Hao from the Delta; ancestor worship across all; temples to national heroes as collective ancestors.
- Language (p487–488): six tones, 11 vowels; chu nom replaced by de Rhodes's Roman script — the only Roman-alphabet language on the SE Asian mainland; Hanoi dialect considered standard; southern slower and more fluid; central (Hue, Hoi An) the hardest for other Vietnamese; minority languages confined to the homelands; all public business in Vietnamese.
- **Death rituals box (p488–489)**: afterlife needs food, clothes, money → household and shop altars with offerings and cigarettes; paper "Bank of the Dead" dollar bills burned on holidays, seen blowing in the street (don't pick them up — bad luck to keep); multi-day home funeral under a street tent, white headbands, portrait altar, pre-dawn (4–5 am) procession with trumpets and cymbals — the thing that wakes tourists; death anniversary (dam gio) celebrated rather than the birthday, as a family party not a mourning. → culture-etiquette material: "what is that tent/noise/fake money" is a genuine first-timer question none of our articles answer.
- Arts (p488–489): lacquer and enamel fused with Chinese and French influence; cheo (northern satirical theatre), cai luong (southern opera); Trinh Cong Son and Pham Duy as karaoke staples; V-pop chasing K-pop; Vietnam Idol / The Voice clones; **Paris By Night** — the diaspora variety show filmed in France/Canada/US, technically banned yet universally watched. The Paris By Night point is a direct Vietnamese-American cultural bridge for our reader.

## Region openers — planning your time and seasons (p95–98, 143–145, 227–229, 305–307, 389–391, 458–460, 170)
- **Halong (p97–98)**: 2–4 days; most short-schedule travellers choose Halong OR Ninh Binh ("Halong Bay on land"); a day cruise costs you two travel days from Hanoi, an overnight 2–3; Cat Ba minimum 3 days; Ninh Binh 1–2 nights (+1 for Cuc Phuong langurs, also a Hanoi day trip). Season: June–August is a "madhouse" of domestic holidaymakers with prices up; Sep–Dec quiet and calm; late Dec–late Feb quieter still but Chinese weekenders and **weather cancels cruises**. **Verdict: September to early December.** → the clearest Halong month rule in any of the three books; directly feeds the halong swamp. Lan Ha (off Cat Ba) and Bai Tu Long named as the quieter alternatives; "Halong or Ninh Binh" framed as a genuine either/or for short trips (decision gap). Trash in the water and the relocation of floating fishing villages acknowledged (2015). 1,969 islands; New7Wonders 2011; Bach Dang 938 and 1288.
- **Central provinces (p143–145)**: Hue + Hoi An are the highlights; allow a week for the region on a cross-country trip; My Son and the DMZ as day trips; take a day for Hue→Hoi An with Lang Co beach or the Hai Van Pass; **Phong Nha = "the country's best up-and-coming destination", a full day's travel each way, at least two days there, so budget 4–5 days**. Highway 1 via Danang is the standard route. Weather: the region gets the worst storms; Hoi An's **November floods** (locals by canoe); avoid Sep–Jan; August brutally hot; **Feb–May best**. → Moon's central-coast verdict matches the DK climate chart and contradicts DK's one-line Mar–May only partially (Moon starts in Feb). Quang Tri/DMZ: Ben Hai River boundary, Khe Sanh, Hamburger Hill, Rockpile; Vinh Moc tunnels (600 residents, unknown to the US); Hien Luong bridge; Truong Son cemetery (largest, 60 miles north of Hue); Gio Linh graves marked "name unknown"; Long Hung church shell from the 1972 Easter Offensive (81-day battle). Hoi An = Faifo, 16th-c. Chinese/Japanese traders, silted up in the 19th c.; Gia Long moves the capital to Hue 1802, 13 Nguyen emperors; Bao Dai hands over 1945.
- **Phong Nha (p170)**: 130 miles north of Hue; three cave systems (Phong Nha, Vom, Nuoc Mooc) ~90 miles of passages, 3–5 million years old, World Heritage; Son Doong discovered by British cavers 2009, 5.5 miles long; independent visits limited to certain caves, the best need certified operators; Son Doong capped and ~USD 3,000 (2015 lead); cheap day trips from Hue exist but (text cuts) are rushed. → coverage gap confirmed with specifics for a future article.
- **South-central coast (p227–229)**: Nha Trang is the hub; Dalat as the inland contrast 80 miles away; Phan Rang and Quy Nhon for anonymity "without leaving the beaten track". **Best Feb–April** (warm, pre-rain); sunny through August but oppressive heat late summer; autumn rainy with wind in the south of the stretch and cooler Nha Trang/Quy Nhon. Semi-arid pockets (Phan Rang) are clear year-round. A week covers the highlights. Champa history: 2nd c.–mid-1600s, four states, maritime trade (eaglewood, silk), capital west of Hoi An, destroyed in Binh Dinh in the 15th c., remnant in Ninh Thuan/Binh Thuan, diaspora in Cambodia; **landmines still around Phan Thiet/Mui Ne** (lead; verify with current UXO data — relevant to off-road dune driving).
- **HCMC (p305–307)**: 7.5m official / 8–10m real (2015; now ~9–10m official); "the future of Vietnam"; founded 1674 on the Ben Nghe River with Ming-refugee Chinese in District 5 (Cholon) and Vietnamese farmers in District 1; French from 1859 left churches, theatres, post office; Diem and Nhu killed after hiding in a District 5 church; Saigon itself mostly safe during the war and a press hub; renamed 1975; doi moi and tourism from the 1990s. Her warning: "a blessing and a curse", livelier and "occasionally dangerous" vs the coast towns — the author concedes HCMC's edge even while living there.
- **Mekong (p389–391)**: as much water as land; "River of Nine Dragons"; rice bowl, second-largest exporter (2015); Khmer, Chinese, Cham communities; tourism spread thin; **Can Tho is the hub** — overnight for Cai Rang; Chau Doc and Vinh Long 1–2 days each; **Phu Quoc 2–3 days minimum** because the plane or speedboat cost makes a short stay wasteful; add a day each for Tra Vinh, Tram Chim, Sa Dec; Rach Gia/Ha Tien 1–2 days; day trips from HCMC to My Tho, Ben Tre, Vinh Long. Little English west of Saigon; uncomfortable buses; **avoid Apr–Oct rains and extreme flooding** (Moon's Delta window = Nov–Mar). History: youngest region, Kampuchea Krom taken from the late 1600s, Mac Cuu founds Ha Tien and seeks Nguyen protection 1708, Thai pirates, Viet Minh and NLF bases in the cajuput forests, heavy fighting, then the Khmer Rouge war.
- **Phu Quoc (p458–460)**: high season Dec–Jan, visit Nov–Apr; 3–4 days to "get the gist"; **pricier than the mainland and built for couples/families — solo travellers pay more**; Long Beach south of Duong Dong is the base; speedboats from Rach Gia and Ha Tien land at Bai Vong; roads under construction (2015) — pillion or guide if you're not a confident rider; Phu Quoc prison, fish-sauce factories, pepper farms, national park in the north; Ridgeback dog box (one of three ridged breeds). Ha Tien's Hon Chong / Father-and-Son rock (father fell in a 2006 storm) — Moon says don't waste beach time on the mainland when Phu Quoc is 21 miles away.

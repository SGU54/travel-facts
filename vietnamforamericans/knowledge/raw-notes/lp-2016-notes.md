## Welcome (p4–5)
- p4: LP's four hooks: sensory overload (crossing a Hanoi street takes ten minutes), "a nation not a war" forty years on, food as the region's best (north = Chinese-influenced soups, south = spice, centre = herbs and technique, "the epicurean epicentre"), thrills (Hai Van by motorbike, Mui Ne kitesurf, Sapa/Bac Ha hikes) and spas at every price.

## Need to Know (p18–19) — all figures 2016 LEADS
- p18: Visas "complicated and fast-changing": some nationalities need one in advance, some get 15 days free (2016 — the 15-day waiver never included the US; see corrections #5). ATMs everywhere incl. small towns but "charges quite steep"; cash is king; cards in many hotels. Local SIMs work in most unlocked phones. UTC+7. WHEN TO GO: LP's seasons — HIGH Jul–Aug (coast prices +up to 50%, book ahead; hot, humid, summer monsoon everywhere but the far north); SHOULDER Dec–Mar (Tet = whole country moving, prices up; cool north of Nha Trang, cold far north; south clear and sunny); LOW Apr–Jun + Sep–Nov "perhaps the best time to tour the whole nation", typhoons on central/northern coast until November. NOTE: LP's "low = best" is the same March–May verdict as DK but adds Sep–Nov; neither resolves the central-coast autumn rain against it.
- p19: Websites named: Vietnam Coracle (independent, long-term resident — still the best English blog, LEAD), Rusty Compass, The Word. Dial-in: drop the 0; mobiles start 09/01 (2016; now 10-digit 03/05/07/08/09, LEAD). 113 police. DAILY COSTS 2016: budget <US$40/day (bia hoi from ~US$0.50, hour of local bus US$1–1.50, cheap hotel US$10–15, noodle meal US$1.50–2.50); midrange US$40–100 (double US$25–50, smart meal from US$8, hour massage US$6–20, 10-min taxi US$4); top end >US$100 (luxury from US$80, gourmet from US$20, internal flight US$30–100). OPENING HOURS: banks 8–3 weekdays + Sat morning; offices/museums 7 or 8 to 5 or 6, museums often shut Monday and some close for lunch; restaurants 11.30–9; shops 8–6; temples 5am–9pm. ARRIVING 2016: SGN taxi ~190,000d / ~30 min, bus 152 6,000d every 15 min 6am–6pm ~40 min; HAN taxi ~400,000d / ~50 min, Jetstar shuttle 35,000d, VN Airlines minibus 50,000d hourly, bus 17 to Long Bien 5,000d. GETTING AROUND verdicts: train "reasonably priced, comfortable enough… no real express trains"; plane cheap if booked ahead, comprehensive, cancellations happen; car always comes with a driver, good for own pace and thin-transport regions; bus frequent on highways but not relaxing, "in the sticks things deteriorate rapidly"; open-tour buses very cheap, worth considering.

## First Time (p20–21)
- p20: Checklist: visa in advance maybe; passport 6 months past arrival; immunisations; insurance; PRE-BOOK internal flights and trains; tell card issuer. Pack: good footwear (streets bumpy), DEET repellent, rain jacket, adapter, torch, flip-flops, binoculars. Tips: traffic "biblical" — stay calm, get a massage after long journeys; scams are mostly overcharging; UXO rare but real; hustle from vendors/cyclo drivers in Hue, Sapa and tourist beaches, none off the track; arrive with "bargaining head" on. WHAT TO WEAR: no serious cultural dress concerns, but cover legs and no sleeveless in religious buildings, government offices, formal dinners. North of Hoi An Oct–Mar can be COOL — pack a fleece or two; otherwise shorts/T-shirt/sandals. SLEEPING: book a day or two ahead, several weeks ahead in high season (Tet, Jul–Aug, Christmas). Minihotels, hostels (main centres only), guesthouses (family-run).
- p21: Tours help with culture shock — street-food tours in Hanoi, Hoi An, Danang, Nha Trang; motorbike tours popular. BARGAINING: markets, souvenir shops, cyclos/xe om; hotels discount if asked; restaurants fixed; some bus drivers overcharge foreigners. TIPPING (LP 2016): hotels not expected (small gratuity for cleaners optional); restaurants not expected, 5–10% in smart places or if very pleased, "locals don't tip"; guides a few dollars per day trip, more for longer; taxis not necessary, a little extra appreciated at night; bars never. → CONFLICT with DK's "10% appreciated"; agrees with disputes.md that no primary source exists. LANGUAGE: English not widely spoken; hotel/restaurant staff in tourist areas speak a little; a few phrases go far. ETIQUETTE: the most senior diner pays for everyone; shoes off in homes; no head-touching; don't point feet at people or sacred objects. EATING: reservations rarely needed except national-park restaurants and top city places; scruffy-but-busy local restaurants = fresh food; tourist-area "international" places tone the local food down; cafés serve snacks not meals.

## Month by Month (p25–27)
- p25: January: bitterly cold far north, snow possible; milder southward; Tet late Jan or Feb; Dalat Flower Festival "early in the month" (LP says January; DK said December — the festival has floated, now biennial late Dec; LEAD). Tet: "Christmas, New Year and birthdays rolled into one", transport booked solid, businesses shut. Quang Trung festival (Dong Da mound, Hanoi, 5th day of 1st lunar month). February: north of Danang grey "Chinese winds", south sunny. March: grey/cool north of Hoi An until late month; south dry season ending; Buon Ma Thuot coffee festival (March, biennial odd years — LEAD); Saigon Cyclo Challenge mid-March (charity race); Thanh Minh (first three days of third moon). April: "generally an excellent time to cover the nation", winter monsoon over, flights moderate unless Easter.
- p26: Hue Festival biennial (2016, 2018; now annual four-season format — LEAD), most events inside the citadel. Danang International Fireworks Festival, last week of April (LP) — now runs over several weekends May–July (LEAD). May: fine for centre and north, clear warm days, sea warming, quiet month. Buddha's birthday (Phong Sinh) 15th of 4th lunar month — big at Bai Dinh (Ninh Binh) and Jade Emperor Pagoda (HCMC). Nha Trang Sea Festival end May/early June (biennial — LEAD).
- p27: June: good, just before domestic peak; humidity punishing — plan coast time; Tet Doan Ngo 5th of 5th moon (sticky-rice wine). August: PEAK month for domestic + international tourism, book flights and rooms well ahead, "hot, hot, hot". Wandering Souls Day 15th of 7th moon (second to Tet). September: "excellent time to tour the whole nation", coast less crowded, temperatures and humidity drop; National Day Sep 2 (Ba Dinh rally + fireworks, boat races on Hoan Kiem). October: good for the far north (clear, mild); winter wind/rain starts in the centre; south often dry. Mid-Autumn 15th of 8th moon (Sep/Oct), big in Hoi An and Hanoi, moon cakes. Kate (Cham new year) at Po Klong Garai, 7th Cham month (~Oct). Oc Om Boc 15th of 10th moon (late Oct/Nov), boat races Ba Dong beach (Tra Vinh) and Soc Trang river. December: quiet until mid-month then resorts fill — book Christmas well ahead; steamy south, chilly north. Christmas not a holiday but big, esp. Phat Diem (Ninh Binh cathedral) and HCMC midnight Mass.
- LP's month verdicts, distilled: best whole-country months = April, (May–June centre/north), September; worst for booking = August and Christmas–Tet; the centre's bad months are Oct–Dec (DK's chart says the same, LP's text says it too — this is settled, only the one-line "dry season" summaries get it wrong).

## Itineraries (p28–35)
- p28–29 "Coastal classic" (bookended by the two cities, SOUTH→NORTH): HCMC 2 days + Cu Chi day trip → Nha Trang 2 days (bars, three mud-bath spas; quieter bases Doc Let north or Bai Dai south) → Hoi An 3 days ("certainly warrants three"), with My Son or a rural motorbike loop → Hue 1 night → Phong Nha → long road/train leg to Halong → Hanoi 2+ days. Advice: take an internal flight or two to save time. NOTE: LP's default direction is south→north, the opposite of DK's — the direction question is genuinely open between the two books (north-south-routing swamp).
- p31 "Northern mountains loop" (~3 weeks, best on two wheels, doable by public transport with patience): Hanoi → Mai Chau 2 nights (White Thai) → Son La overnight → Dien Bien Phu 2 nights (new museum) → Tram Ton Pass → Sapa ~4 days → Bac Ha 3 nights (Flower Hmong market) → Ha Giang province slowly (Yen Minh, Dong Van, Lung Cu flag tower, Vuong Palace, Ma Pi Leng pass, Meo Vac — NO public transport on that stretch, hire xe om or car) → Bao Lac → Cao Bang → Ba Be NP 3 nights in Tay homestays, trek/kayak → Cao Bang → Hanoi. This is the Ha Giang coverage DK lacked; still no article of ours.
- p32 "South: delta + highlands + coast": HCMC 2 days → Ben Tre (bike the canal lanes, boat the islands) → cargo boat to Tra Vinh (Khmer pagodas) → Can Tho 2 days (floating markets, museum) → Chau Doc (border countryside by bike) → Phu Quoc 3 days → fly/bus back to HCMC → Cat Tien NP 1 night (gibbons, crocodiles, birds) → Dalat (canyoning, MTB, kayak) → Dalat→Mui Ne road "one of the nation's finest", ideally pillion with an Easy Rider → Mui Ne 2–3 days (kitesurf, sailing course) → HCMC final night. Caveat: avoid the southern rainy season if beaches are the point.
- p34–35 "Off the beaten track" map (OCR mostly rotated/garbled): legible picks — Hang Va cave (Phong Nha, ranger-led), Tam Hai island (Quang Nam: beaches, Cham ruins, whale cemetery), Sinho (NW mountain village, hard by public transport), Dong Van / Vuong Palace / Lung Cu (Ha Giang), Cham Islands homestay (short boat from Hoi An), Ganh Da Dia (Phu Yen volcanic rock coast, good beaches), Bai Xep / Quy Nhon bungalows ("Vietnam's Phu Quoc without the crowds"), Ke Ga lighthouse (south of Mui Ne, French-era), U Minh forest (Mekong, last natural forest), Phu Quoc's quieter north. Theme: the quiet coast between Hoi An and Nha Trang.

## Vietnam Outdoors (p36–42)
- p37: Best trekking: Sapa (superlative views, CROWDED — "some villages see hiking groups hourly"; to go remote you need an expert local guide), Dong Van, Bac Ha (lower, less rainy, less trampled, village homestays, but less dramatic scenery), Mai Chau. Best diving: Con Dao (remote but best), Phu Quoc (visibility iffy), Nha Trang (pro schools). Best cycling: Dalat→Mui Ne two-day descent, Hoi An flatlands, Mekong back roads. WHEN: surf and kitesurf peak in winter Nov–Apr; dive visibility best Jun–Aug; DON'T climb Fansipan May–Sep (rain); snorkel/dive poor Nov–Apr. Three rules: don't leave established paths (UXO), hire guides (cheap, language, culture), ankle boots. Sapa rents sleeping bags/boots/waterproofs. 
- p38: Ha Giang = "the nation's Tibet", guides in Ha Giang city or Dong Van. Moc Chau plateau emerging (plums, tea, dairy). Ba Be rugged karst trails to minority villages; Cat Ba 18 km hike + Butterfly Valley. Phong Nha new trails via Jungle Boss, routes combining trek + cave up to Son Doong. Cuc Phuong forest trails to a minority village. Bach Ma; Ba Na short trails; Hoi An operators run tribal-area treks west. Yok Don mammals; Cat Tien needs a guide (crocodile lake, night hikes, Wild Gibbon Trek popular); Dalat → Bidoup Nui Ba NP. South otherwise flat/hot — Con Son island the exception. CYCLING: basic bikes US$1–3/day, good MTB US$6–15 (2016); Mekong back roads (Chau Doc lanes; the Chau Doc→Ha Tien road along the Cambodian border; Vinh Long islands); AVOID HIGHWAY 1 (insane traffic, dangerous).
- p39: Use the inland Ho Chi Minh Highway (Hwys 14/15/8) instead — scenery, little traffic. Hoi An base for craft villages; Hue for temples + Perfume River by bike. MOTORBIKING: LP's "no better way" — repair shops everywhere; if not confident, hire a rider (Easy Riders, Dalat-born). Avoid Hwy 1 ("exhaust fumes and barged by trucks"); Ho Chi Minh Highway alternative loses the ocean; Duc Tho→Phong Nha stretch = karst, forest, empty, paved. ALT-HIGHWAY-1 coastal loops: east of Hue Thuan An→Vinh Hien; Chi Thanh→Hon Gom peninsula; south of Nha Trang to Cam Ranh airport; Phan Thiet→Vung Tau. NOTE: LP never mentions the licence/insurance problem in this section — it's buried in the Transport chapter; corrections #2 stands.
- p40: Two most dramatic southern rides: Hai Van Pass, and Nha Trang→Dalat (1,700 m pass). North: Sapa/Dien Bien Phu valleys; Ha Giang→Dong Van→Bao Lac "the ultimate". SURF: not a destination (Apocalypse Now's wave was the Philippines); boards from odd guesthouses; season Nov–Apr with the winter monsoon, typhoons make the biggest swells; China Beach (30 km, clean 2 m peaks, pollution after rain); Bai Dai 27 km south of Nha Trang (left-hander); Mui Ne for beginners; Vung Tau inconsistent. UXO and pollution warnings for remote breaks. KITESURF: Mui Ne = Asia hotspot; 2-hour beginner lesson from ~US$100 (2016); best Nov–Apr; mornings for beginners, afternoons 35 knots; Manta sailing school. LP's BEST BEACHES list: Sao and Long Beach (Phu Quoc), Bai Dat Doc (Con Dao), Doc Let, Bai Mon (Vung Ro bay, Phu Yen), An Bang, Thuan An (Hue), Mui Ne, Minh Chau (Quan Lan island, Bai Tu Long), Bai Dai, Nha Trang, My Khe (Danang).
- p42: DIVING: "not a world-class dive mecca" — less life, less visibility than Indonesia/Australia. Nha Trang most popular (several reputable operators; ALSO "an excess" of dodgy shops with fake PADI credentials — stick to recommended schools); Hoi An schools go to the Cham Islands (macro life); Phu Quoc popular. 2016 prices: two fun dives US$70–80; snorkel day trips US$25–40; PADI Open Water US$325–500; Con Dao = best (marine life, reefs, a wreck) but ~US$160 for two dives. KAYAK/SUP: Halong "exploded" — most tours include a paddle; Cat Ba, Con Dao, Phong Nha, Dalat, Hoi An rivers; rentals on Nha Trang beach. RAFTING: infancy; Dalat (Phat Tire, Langbian river, class II–IV by season, from US$67). CAVING: Phong Nha — Oxalis the ONLY operator licensed for Son Doong; cheaper: Hang Toi, Hang Va, 7 km inside Paradise Cave, 2-day Hang En + Ban Doong village, Tu Lan swim-through system. CLIMBING: Asia Outdoors (Cat Ba) the pioneers; Dalat canyoning; Hoi An marble-cliff rappel.

## Eat & Drink Like a Local (p43–49)
- p44: "A day in Hanoi" eating rhythm: breakfast bun rieu cua (paddy-crab noodle soup); mid-morning strong coffee, iced lemon tea in summer; lunch bun cha (THE Hanoi midday dish); afternoon banh goi fried pastries; evening bia hoi with roast duck / dried squid. First-night primers: Secret Garden (HCMC), Vy's Market (Hoi An), Quan An Ngon (Hanoi). Street-food tours exist in HCMC, Hoi An, Danang, Hue, Hanoi, Dalat (2016; all still trading in some form — LEAD). Fusion: La Badiane (Hanoi), Xu (HCMC), Nu Eatery (Hoi An), Spice House (Phu Quoc). VEGETARIAN: com chay restaurants everywhere, usually next to Buddhist temples; around the 1st and 15th of the lunar month some stalls swap in tofu (useful: those are the easy vegetarian days).
- p45: Minority cuisines: Chim Sao and Quan Kien (Hanoi), Hill Station (Sapa, Hmong), Pleiku's pho kho ("two-bowl pho") and beef in bamboo. Coffee heritage cafés: Duy Tri (Hanoi, 75+ years), Pho Co (hidden balcony over Hoan Kiem), Xua & Nay (Quy Nhon, 1832 house), K'Ho (Dalat plantation). Cooking schools: Green Bamboo (Hoi An, personalised), Hanoi Cooking Centre (market visit, kids' classes), GRAIN (HCMC, Luke Nguyen). REGIONAL THESIS: north→south is "a journey that begins in China and ends in Southeast Asia". NORTH: Chinese imprint, mild, soy as often as fish sauce, vinegar not lime/tamarind, black pepper not chilli, long cooking; banh cuon, bun cha (Hanoi version = pork belly slices + shoulder patties), pho bo (broth from bones + shallot, ginger, fish sauce, black cardamom, star anise, cassia; purists frown on lime/basil/sprouts).
- p46: "Ultimate food tour" south→north: HCMC by Vespa/XO (banh xeo, Cyclo Resto class, Pasteur Street beer) → Phu Quoc night market seafood → Hoi An cooking schools (Morning Glory, Red Bridge) + Eat Hoi An tour + An Bang seafood + cao lau at Mermaid → Hanoi (Old Quarter tour, cha ca at Cha Ca Thang Long, pho at Pho Thin, Hanoi Cooking Centre, minority dishes) → Sapa Hill Station class + ruou tasting.
- p47: CENTRE: balance and moderation EXCEPT chilli; everything smaller (mini baguettes, mini herbs); Hue imperial = dainty; banh beo steamed rice cakes = royal legacy on the street; shrimp sauce (mam tom) and lemongrass; banh khoai (dessert-plate crepe, fermented-soybean sauce); bun bo Hue (annatto-orange, lemongrass, shrimp sauce, beef + pork); com hen (clam rice, Hue).
- p49: SOUTH: abundance, sweet side, coconut milk, big-leaf herbs, goi salads (papaya, pomelo, lotus stem); canh chua ca = "the Mekong in a bowl" (snakehead/catfish, pineapple, tomato, okra, taro stem, tamarind); banh mi = French + Chinese legacy but "100% Vietnamese" (pâté, sausage, mayo, soy); banh xeo 12–14-inch crepe wrapped in lettuce/mustard leaf. Meal pattern: three meals, breakfast = noodles or chao; lunch social with workmates; dinner leisurely with family; constant snacking in cities. Venue ladder: single-dish stalls → com shops → food courts (Quan An Ngon) → fusion restaurants.

## Regions at a Glance (p50–52)
- p50: LP's frame: north = cooler winters, character and cuisine follow (soy, bamboo); south = tropical, coconut, fish sauce, "always humid, hot and sticky", food sweet/spicy/aromatic. HANOI = food + temples + culture (street food, a millennium of religion predating communism, an art scene "finding its voice" under a restrictive government). NORTHERN = landscapes + culture + adventure (Halong in morning mist; Ha Giang "arguably even more spectacular"; Sapa/Bac Ha trek + homestay; Cat Ba climbing, Lan Ha Bay kayaking).
- p51: CENTRAL = food + history + landscapes (Hoi An/Hue street food, imperial cuisine; Hue citadel survived bombing in parts; Ninh Binh and Phong Nha karst). SOUTHEAST COAST = beaches + Cham temples + seafood (Mui Ne and Nha Trang the big hitters, "hundreds of kilometres of empty beaches", Con Dao; Po Nagar, Po Klong Garai; table-side barbecue seafood). SOUTHWEST HIGHLANDS = adventure + wildlife + culture (Easy Riders between Dalat and Hoi An; Cat Tien gibbon trek; Yok Don elephants from Buon Ma Thuot; Kon Tum village homestays). HCMC = tours + history + nightlife (two-wheel night food tours, street-art walks, heritage-apartment cafés; Cu Chi, War Remnants, Reunification Palace; Pham Ngu Lao "virtually 24/7"). HCMC chapter starts p311.
- p52: MEKONG = beaches (Phu Quoc's Sao and Long Beach as the delta's "trump cards") + boat trips (children swim in life jackets all day; the river so wide you lose the far bank) + pagodas (Sam Mountain, Khmer temples). Mekong chapter starts p366. Siem Reap p417 (Bayon, Ta Prohm, Banteay Srei; Pub Street; balloon/microlight/helicopter).

## Hanoi (p54–55)
- p55: Name history: Thang Long 1010; Gia Long moved the capital to Hue 1802 so Hanoi was a regional capital for a century; named "Hanoi" (river interior) by Tu Duc 1831; capital of French Indochina 1902–53; capital of the DRV declared Aug 1945; French back until 1954. American War: heavy bombing, Long Bien bridge (1,682 m) the prime target, rebuilt after each raid — legend says the bombing stopped when US POWs were put to work on it; the bridge = symbol of Hanoian tenacity. Early 1990s the city still ran on bicycles and Soviet buses; today: booming population, "soaring pollution", "inefficient public transport" (2016), conservationists vs growth — LP's "get in quick" line. MUSEUMS: some shut Mondays and take a TWO-HOUR LUNCH BREAK — check hours. "Hanoi in one day": dawn Hoan Kiem walk → pho bo breakfast → Mausoleum complex + HCM Museum → Military History Museum → coffee (Cong Caphe) → Fine Arts Museum → Temple of Literature → Old Quarter / Women's Museum → bia hoi → water puppets → dinner. Day two: Museum of Ethnology (suburbs) → Revolution + History museums → Cha Ca Thang Long → drinks.

## Northern Vietnam (p99–101)
- p99: "Big-sky country"; the karst runs from Halong inland to Ba Be and Ha Giang then into the NW evergreen hills; hill-tribe heartland (Dzao scarlet headdresses, Black Hmong indigo). Road-tripping country. WHEN TO GO (Halong City chart): MAY = blue skies, calm seas, "perfect for Halong Bay", before high season; LATE AUG–SEP = sunny days, few crowds; NOV = hike Sapa/Bac Ha at harvest (NOTE: rice harvest in Sapa is actually Sep–early Oct; LP's "Nov harvest" is questionable — LEAD). Best markets: Dong Van, Bac Ha, Na Giang, Sin Cheng, Sinho. Highlights: Halong kayaking, Sapa trekking, Ba Be lakes, Cat Ba adventure, Mai Chau rice valleys, Ha Giang "the north's greatest road trip".

## Region openers — Why Go + When to Go (p54, 152, 233, 287, 311, 366)
- p54 HANOI (pop 7.1 m, 2016): a government that "as recently as the 1990s kept the outside world at bay"; knock-off merchants vs real Old Quarter; t'ai chi at dawn on Hoan Kiem; luxury for little. WHEN: Jan–Apr cooler + Tet colour; Jun–Aug heavy heat/humidity = LOW season in Hanoi, hotel bargains; OCT–DEC clear, sunny, cooler = "the ideal time to visit Hanoi". (Contrast: DK/LP national summaries push Mar–May; for Hanoi specifically LP says Oct–Dec.)
- p152 CENTRAL: Hue citadel/tombs/street food; Hoi An; DMZ; Danang "fast emerging as one of the nation's most dynamic cities"; Phong Nha "must-visit", three giant cave systems; An Bang beach; central cuisine "the nation's most complex". Access: "improving highways and upgraded international airports at Hue and Danang" (2016). Scenery picks: Tam Coc, Phong Nha, Bach Ma, Hai Van, Cham Islands. WHEN (Hue chart): late Apr Danang fireworks; MAY temperatures rise, beaches "come into their own"; SEP summer heat relents, crowds gone, sea balmy. (Implicit: Oct–Jan is the bad window — the chart's rain bars.)
- p233 SOUTHEAST COAST: "Vietnam's premier beach-holiday destination"; Nha Trang + Mui Ne headline, but "hidden coves and lonely lighthouses"; Con Dao "ultimate off-grid". Best beaches: Doc Let, Bai Mon, Mui Ne, Bai Dai, Nha Trang, My Khe. WHEN (Quang Ngai chart): JUL "the perfect time" for Nha Trang; OCT Kate at Po Klong Garai; DEC Christmas in Mui Ne = best kitesurf/windsurf. → The south-central coast's good season is the north's bad one and vice-versa: Jul is peak sun here, Oct–Dec is kite season not swim season.
- p287 SOUTHWEST HIGHLANDS: Ho Chi Minh Highway through coffee, pines, lakes, hill-tribe villages — "the challenge of a two-wheeled journey"; gibbons, pygmy loris, wild elephants; Dalat cool climate, last emperor's palaces; Bahnar rong houses, Jarai grave-feeding rites (Kon Tum). WHEN (Dalat): MAR coffee festival / Don elephant races; OCT "autumn in Dalat", best for activities; DEC Cat Tien trek/cycle in cooler weather.
- p311 HCMC (pop 7.9 m, 2016): "Vietnam at its most dizzying"; alleys and incense temples beside designer malls; the war a generation ago. WHEN: FEB least rain and humidity, Tet blooms; MAR low rain, cyclo challenge; DEC "a whisper cooler", comparatively dry. (Dry season Dec–Apr; the chart's wet bars May–Nov.)
- p366 MEKONG: "rice bowl", water world, towns bustle while countryside is "soporific"; two-wheel lanes, river fish; mangroves with Viet Cong bunker remains; Khmer pagodas; offshore islands. WHEN (My Tho): JAN while the north shivers, Phu Quoc beaches temperate and dry; MAR avoids Tet madness and the summer heat/rain; NOV dry season starts + Khmer longboat festivals (Tra Vinh, Soc Trang).

## Hanoi — Getting There & Around (p94–95) — 2016 LEADS

## Understand: People & Culture (p454–455)
- p454: National character as LP sees it: industrious, proud, stubborn, mischievous, quick to laugh; direct questions get evasive answers; personal disclosure to strangers is un-Vietnamese; Confucian respect for tradition, family, state. "Team harmony" ideal vs "everyone for themselves" on the road and in business — the two things outsiders find most alien. FACE: giving way is hated; bluff and bluster to get through. GENERATIONS: elders remember the war years, restraint; youth want success, the motorbike, the haircut, the iPhone. NORTH–SOUTH: "southerners think then do; northerners think then think some more"; mutual caricatures (hard-faced vs frivolous). KINSHIP ADDRESS worked example: a man in his 20s calls a mid-30s man anh (big brother) and himself em; calls a 40s man chu (uncle) and himself chau (nephew); friends can agree on ban. Book rec: Shadows and Wind (Templer).
- p455: Climate explains the divide: three rice harvests a year in the Mekong vs the north's grey drizzly winter — "Maine and Alabama"; plus 50+ years of communism north vs two decades of American free-for-all south. FACE: families spend ruinously on weddings for face; NEVER lose your temper — it ends any chance of a resolution; equally, Vietnamese traders shouting/tugging at tourists is culturally unacceptable — "walk on". LIFESTYLE: generations under one roof; a 1960s-style youth rebellion but everyone still comes home to Mum and Dad; idleness despised, early risers, t'ai chi at dawn, offices full by 7am. FAMILY status > salary; extended family to third cousins. BUSINESS PRACTICES: Westerners' top complaint — price hiking to outright scams; "impossible to get the local price". VIET KIEU: traditionally maligned as cowardly/arrogant/privileged; official policy now welcomes them and their money.
- p456: "Do as the Viets" box: shorts to the knee and covered shoulders at religious sites; shoes off in temples; no topless/nude sunbathing; BUSINESS CARDS exchanged for even small transactions; chopsticks upright = incense for the dead; shoes off in homes; never point soles of feet at people or Buddha images; hat off and head bowed to elders and monks; never touch heads. Feng-shui: a failing business may call a geomancer, move a door — or an ancestor's grave. OVERCHARGING EXPLAINED: concentrated in tourism, rare off the "banana-pancake trail"; the mentality: Westerners won't learn the price or the language and leave in two weeks; short-term thinking is shifting as repeat business and online reviews bite; context: 2 million dead in the 1940s famine, the post-war poverty, and the STATE ITSELF set official foreigner prices 4–10x local for trains and hotels until recently — the government taught the habit. PEOPLES: Chinese occupation permeates everything; southward expansion absorbed Champa then the Khmer east; 50+ NW minorities came from Yunnan and Tibet "in the past few centuries" into mountains the lowlanders considered uncultivable; ethnic Chinese = the commercial class, viewed with suspicion, many left as boat people, now central to the economy.
- p457: 53 minorities, ~14 million, mostly in the north along the China/Laos borders. Government pushes lowland resettlement and cash crops (tea, coffee) with subsidies; many stay up. Northern women still weave/embroider — girls learn before they walk; Central Highlands dress is mostly gone. PREJUDICE: media shows minorities as primitive/exotic; some seen as "subversive" because some sided with the US in the war; minorities = 14% of population but 50% of poor households (WHO 2015); early marriage, big families, early death. GROUPS: Tay 1.6 m (lowland NE, Vietnamese-style beliefs + spirits, famous arts); Thai 1.5 m (Hoa Binh–Muong Lay river valleys, stilt houses, Red/Black/White); Muong 1.4 m (Hoa Binh, gongs, folk literature); Hmong ~1 m (far north, animist, dry rice; Black/White/Red/Green/Flower dress codes). Village picks: Sapa (Red Dzao, Black Hmong), Bac Ha (Flower Hmong), Mai Chau (White Thai homestays), Cao Bang (Hmong/Nung/Tay), Kon Tum (Bahnar homestays), Bho Hoong (Co Tu, west of Hoi An). Book rec: Paradise of the Blind (Duong Thu Huong).
- p458: CIDG: many minorities fought with US Special Forces; some militias resisted Hanoi into the 1980s — the root of the "subversive" prejudice. Nung 800k (NE, basketry); Jarai 350k (south-central highlands, animist, carved grave effigies); Sedang 150k (no family names, sexual equality, grave abandonment). RELIGION: surveys say only ~20% consider themselves to have a faith; Tam Giao fusion; Mahayana (Zen the biggest sect; Pure Land in the south); Theravada among Mekong Khmers. TET BOX: "Christmas, New Year and birthdays rolled into one" (same line as p25); everyone turns a year older; falls 19 Jan–20 Feb; three official days, most take the week; rites start seven days before; debts paid, cleaning "the national sport"; New Year tree = kumquat, peach or apricot blossom; midnight = maximum noise; New Year's Day behaviour sets the year — no anger, no sweeping, sewing, swearing, breaking; the FIRST VISITOR of the year matters (a wealthy married father ideal) — "foreigners may not be considered auspicious" (etiquette point: don't be the first through a door on Tet morning); banh chung at home; shops shut, "virtually all transport ceases"; greeting chuc mung nam moi.
- p459: Taoism (am/duong = yin/yang); Confucianism = obligations to family, society, state, "pillars of the nation"; Cao Dai founded 1920s, prophets incl. Buddha, Confucius, Jesus, Moses, Mohammed, Joan of Arc, Shakespeare, Victor Hugo, 2–3 million, HQ Tay Ninh; Hoa Hao founded 1939 Mekong by Huynh Phu So, no intermediaries; CATHOLICS 8–10%, second-highest concentration in Asia; Protestants ~200k, mostly highland minorities; ~70k Cham Muslims (south; traditionally Friday-only Islam, now more orthodox); ~60k Cham Hindus around Phan Rang. Mega-temples: Bai Dinh (Ninh Binh); giant Buddhas on the Danang and Vung Tau coasts.

- p468: sinh to smoothie stalls (avocado treated as fruit); goat hotpot, frogs; seafood kept live in tanks = freshness guarantee; paddy crabs → bun rieu cua; snails (oc) → bun oc, stuffed with lemongrass; snakehead, catfish, hen clams. Vegetables: banana blossom and lotus-stem salads, bac ha taro stem, squash blossoms, water spinach, wild mushrooms in the rainy season, fern tips off the track; leafy greens as wrappers. SWEETS: rice-flour + coconut + lotus/sesame/peanut; mung bean; French crème caramel; kem ice cream; thach agar jellies (pandan, coffee-coconut); sweetened yoghurt in glass pots.
- p469: COFFEE CULTURE box: phin drip over condensed milk on a tiny stool — "can't be rushed"; variants: ca phe sua da (iced, condensed milk), ca phe da (iced black), ca phe den (black), ca phe sua chua (yoghurt coffee), ca phe trung (egg coffee — LP says egg white). MOBILE: "excellent, comprehensive network", packages "extremely cheap"; GSM 900/1800 "not compatible with North America" (2016 — obsolete, modern US phones are fine; LEAD); SMS 500–2,500d, calls 3–6k/min, 3G 1 GB from 50,000d; cheap handset ~300k with credit; THREE carriers Viettel, Vinaphone, Mobifone with offices nationwide (DK only named two).
- p485: Roaming "outrageously expensive". UTC+7, no DST. TOILETS: if there's a bin beside the toilet, paper goes in the bin (sewage can't handle it); no bin = flush; paper provided except at bus/train stations — carry your own; squat toilets persist in public/rural places; scarcity hits women harder; men urinate in public; attendant fee a few dong. TOURIST OFFICES = state travel agencies selling tours, not information ("don't come here hoping for independent travel information"); agents, backpacker cafés and other travellers are better; fairly helpful offices in Hanoi/HCMC. DISABILITIES: not easy despite many war-disabled Vietnamese; chaotic traffic, pavements blocked by bikes and stalls; some budget and most mid/top hotels have lifts; BATHROOM DOORWAYS can be too narrow for a wheelchair over 60 cm; trains not geared for wheelchairs, open-tour buses "doable"; a private car + driver makes "almost anywhere instantly accessible"; Vietnamese always willing to help lift; blind travellers need a sighted companion for the traffic. VISAS 2016: "very complicated… fluid — always check"; 15-day exemptions for some Western nationalities (NOT the USA — LP names "the USA, Australia or New Zealand" as non-exempt, consistent with corrections #5); exemption users can't extend and can't re-enter on exemption within 30 days; tourist visas 30 or 90 days.
- p486: 2016 EXEMPTION TABLE: Myanmar/Brunei 14; Belarus, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, S. Korea, Norway, Russia, Spain, Sweden, UK 15; Philippines 21; ASEAN 30. Fees: single-entry 30-day US$20, 3-month multiple US$70 (2016 — obsolete; facts.json: e-visa US$25/US$50). ONLINE VISA AGENTS = "preferred method for most arriving by air": agency fee ~US$20 + Visa-on-Arrival approval letter + stamping fee at the airport; only at the FIVE international airports, not land crossings (OBSOLETE — e-visa replaced the letter; the named agents are now the intermediary market the visa articles warn about). MULTIPLE-ENTRY needed to pop into Cambodia/Laos and return; agents ~US$50 + fees, up to 7 days. EXTENSIONS 2016: "if you've got the dollars, they've got the rubber stamp" — officially from US$10 via agents, ~7 days, 30 or 90 days; more in a different city from arrival; smoothest in HCMC/Hanoi/Danang/Hue. (CONFLICT with corrections #4 — e-visas cannot be extended today; this 2016 picture is exactly what the intermediary sites still sell.) Embassy visas: pricier, slower (2–3 working days in Asia, ~a week in Europe/N. America).
- p487: WOMEN: "relatively free of serious hassles"; thousands travel alone and love it; Vietnamese women lead free lives, sexes mix; East Asian-looking women with Western men may, very occasionally, be mistaken for sex workers by "ill-educated locals"; sleeveless tops draw attention away from the beach; many Vietnamese women cover up partly against the sun. WORK: cash-in-hand bar/restaurant jobs; dive/adventure instructors; English teaching the main option, US$10–25/hour (2016).

## Survival Guide: Transport (p488–497)
- p488: Most arrive by plane or bus; train links from China; Mekong boats from Cambodia. Air formalities smoother than land; Cambodia/China crossings "relatively stress-free", Laos slow. Passport 6 months' validity. Vietnam Airlines: 17 countries (2016), modern fleet, "good recent safety record". FIVE INTERNATIONAL AIRPORTS 2016: Cam Ranh (36 km south of Nha Trang: HK, Chengdu, Seoul), Danang (Laos, KL, Siem Reap, Singapore, Tokyo, China), Noi Bai, Phu Quoc (Singapore), Tan Son Nhat; Hue "officially international" but no overseas flights. Tet flights hard to book.
- p489: BORDER CROSSINGS: open ~7am–5pm (Cambodia 8–8); official exchange booths now on the Vietnamese side (USD, RMB, kip, riel) — avoid black marketeers; occasional "immigration fee" of a dollar or two (a small bribe). CAMBODIA: 7 crossings, 1-month Cambodian visa on arrival US$30 (2016), overcharging common except at Bavet. CHINA: 3 (Friendship Pass/Huu Nghi, Lao Cai, Mong Cai), Chinese visa in advance, China is +1 h. LAOS: 7, 30-day Lao visas at all; GOLDEN RULE: direct city-to-city international buses, never step-by-step local buses (overcharging, drivers renegotiating mid-nowhere); remote borders only with time and patience. TABLE (crossing → VN town → far town): Cambodia: Le Thanh–O Yadaw (Pleiku–Ban Lung), Moc Bai–Bavet (HCMC–Phnom Penh), Vinh Xuong–Kaam Samnor (Chau Doc–Phnom Penh, river), Xa Xia–Prek Chak (Ha Tien–Kep/Kampot), Tinh Bien–Phnom Den (Chau Doc–Takeo); China: Lao Cai–Hekou (Kunming), Mong Cai–Dongxing, Dong Dang–Pingxiang (Lang Son–Nanning); Laos: Bo Y–Phou Keua (Kon Tum/Pleiku–Attapeu), Cau Treo–Nam Phao (Vinh–Lak Sao), Lao Bao–Dansavanh (Dong Ha/Hue–Savannakhet), Nam Can–Nong Haet (Vinh–Phonsavan), Tay Trang–Sop Hun (Dien Bien Phu–Muang Khua). BUS: Moc Bai the Cambodia standard; Laos: "the long nightmare bus" Vientiane–Hanoi via Cau Treo, or the easier Savannakhet–Hue via Lao Bao. (Map p490 adds Loc Ninh–Trapeang Sre and Na Meo–Nam Xoi.)
- p491: Two daily Hanoi–Nanning buses; everyone gets off at borders. CAR/MOTORBIKE across borders: theoretically possible with Cambodia/Laos only, bureaucratic; easy to take a Vietnamese bike OUT, very hard to bring one IN; nothing into China; needs registration, liability insurance, IDP, carnet. TRAIN: daily Hanoi–Nanning (on to Beijing); scenic Hanoi–Lao Cai–Kunming (4 daily Hekou–Kunming); no rail to Cambodia/Laos. RIVER: regular fast boats Phnom Penh–Chau Doc via Vinh Xuong; luxury riverboats to Siem Reap. CHINA GUIDEBOOK box: LP China guides confiscated at the border over the Taiwan map. DOMESTIC AIR: good, growing, cheap if early; cards accepted; CANCELLATIONS COMMON — never rely on a regional flight to make a same-day international connection, go a day early; Vietnam Airlines least likely to cancel but dearer. Jetstar 16 airports, Vietjet 15, VASCO = Con Dao + delta. BICYCLE: countryside welcome; much of the country flat/moderate, major roads good, safety the concern; bikes go on bus roofs/train baggage (~US$1–1.50); buy decent bikes only in Hanoi/HCMC, better bring your own; a loud bell is "mandatory"; rentals US$1–3, better from US$6; perfect for Hoi An, Hue, Nha Trang outside the rains.
- p492: "FARE'S FAIR?" box — the structural guide to who overcharges: AIRFARES no foreigner premium; BOAT ferries/hydrofoils fixed, small local boats (Mekong, Cham Islands) charge foreigners more; BUS fixed and cheap if bought at the station, boarding en route invites overcharging, remote drivers ask 4–10x local; local bus prices posted by the door but Danang–Hoi An route overcharges foreigners; RAIL fixed by class; TAXIS metered, cheap, occasional fast meters; XE OM/CYCLO never fixed — "bargain. Hard." BOAT: Mekong the big one; day trips on rivers at Hoi An, Danang, Hue, Tam Coc, HCMC; sea: Halong "a must", Cham Islands, Nha Trang islands, Con Dao. BUS: "never speedy" — reckon on ~50 km/h even on Highway 1 (2016; expressways have changed some legs — LEAD); most travellers never see a bus station, they use open-tour buses. Cities have several stations split by destination direction and service type; stations now post official prices. DELUXE/SLEEPER buses: allocated seat, reclining or flat beds, non-smoking, some wifi, TVs with kung-fu films, karaoke — "earplugs and eye masks"; sleepers "a good alternative to trains, costs comparable"; Mai Linh Express and Sinh Tourist named. LOCAL buses leave when full, few after ~4pm.
- p493: Local buses stop constantly and conductors overcharge foreigners. OPEN TOUR: HCMC–Hanoi through-ticket US$30–75 (2016), hop-on/off at major cities, book the next leg a day ahead; depart from central hostels/cafés not stations; some stop at sights (Po Klong Garai); downsides: herded with backpackers, no locals, stuck on the banana-pancake trail, kickbacks to sister hotels/restaurants; point-to-point tickets cost a bit more but keep flexibility (train, motorbike). Popular: HCMC–Mui Ne–Dalat–Nha Trang. Rural runs overcharge 2–10x; benchmark ~US$2–3 per 100 km (2016). Reservations: buy the day before AT THE OFFICE, "drivers are notorious for overcharging". DRIVING LICENCE (LP 2016): rules changing late 2015; LP claims foreigners became permitted to drive on an IDP paired with local insurance, and adds that in practice neither police nor rental shops ever ask for one — CONFLICT with corrections #2 / facts getting_around.driving_licence (State Dept: IDP invalid). This is the 1968-Convention framing we rejected; LP 2016 pre-dates the State Dept's current flat statement. No self-drive cars; all rentals with driver. Fuel ~20,500d/L (2016); roadside sellers dilute fuel — use real stations.
- p494: CAR + DRIVER 2016: US$80–120/day standard car, US$120–135 4WD (needed for the NW); agencies in Hanoi, HCMC, tourist centres. MOTORBIKE: rent anywhere; some keep your PASSPORT as deposit; sign a written agreement (what, cost, compensation); for the north get a road/trail bike; local driver-guides ~US$20/day; 2016 rates: semi-auto US$5–7, automatic US$6–10, trail/road US$20+. INSURANCE: tourist vehicles insured by the rental firm; bike owners "should" have some; Baoviet third-party policy named; many rentals make you sign a theft valuation — use guarded parking; "do not even consider renting a motorbike… without travel insurance". ROADS: two-lane highways "more and more dangerous", high-speed head-on collisions "sickeningly familiar"; major highways paved but seasonal flooding, typhoon potholes "the size of bomb craters"; remote roads become mud; mountain roads — landslides, rockfalls, runaway vehicles. EMERGENCIES: no efficient rescue system; help is slow and far; you or your guide get yourself to hospital. ROAD RULES: "basically there aren't many"; size wins; children and livestock on the road ("hit a cow on a motorbike and you'll both be hamburger"). HIRING A DRIVER box: tailor-made touring, comfortable, stops anywhere; most drivers don't speak English — find a driver-GUIDE; meet first; the driver pays own food/lodging, you pay petrol (CHECK — differs from DK's version where the driver pays his own costs and fuel is in the price); get the itinerary in writing from the agency as leverage; say no to tourist-trap restaurants/shops; tip if good.
- p495: Police "almost never" stop foreigners on bikes but speed guns exist; urban limit 50 km/h (blue skyscraper sign); NO RIGHT TURN ON RED in cities (a specifically American habit to unlearn); honking = safety signal not aggression; no national seat-belt law (2016 — now mandatory front and rear; LEAD); two per motorbike, enforced in cities only. HELMET LAW box: compulsory including passengers; local "eggshell" helmets useless — buy a decent imported one from ~US$30 for highway/mountain rides. Spare parts easy for Japanese/Chinese bikes. LOCAL: city buses rarely used by travellers; CYCLO dying out, hang around hotels/markets, bring a map, bargain first; 10–20k short, 20–40k longer/night, ~40k/hour (2016); TRAVELLERS HAVE BEEN MUGGED BY CYCLO DRIVERS IN HCMC — day use only there, metered taxi home from bars. TAXI 12–15k/km (2016), go-fast meters around bus terminals; Mai Linh and Vinasun nationwide. XE OM = "motorbike hug"; fares like cyclos; "they will find you before you find them". TOURS: bottom-end tours in HCMC/Hanoi "often terrible — you get what you pay for"; quality operators named: Handspan, Ocean Tours, Buffalo Tours (seaplane Halong), Exo Travel (10-day Hue–Nha Trang cycle), Sinhbalo (Mekong cycling), Grasshopper (cycling).
- p496: MOTORBIKE TOURS: "one of Vietnam's defining moments" (the north); from ~US$100/person/day all-inclusive at four people (2016); first-timer tuition offered; foreign guides cost far more than local; operators: Hoi An Motorbike Adventure (Minsks, from US$50 local rides), Explore Indochina (Urals/Minsks, 7-day NE from US$1,240), Offroad Vietnam (guided/semi/self, Ha Giang 8-day ~US$1,192), Cuong's, Free Wheelin', Moto Tours Asia (from US$90/day). TRAIN: Vietnam Railways "ageing but dependable… relaxing"; air-con sleeper "beats a hairy overnight bus along Hwy 1". CLASSES: SE trains = fastest/smartest, TN = slower/older; four classes × air-con/non (air-con only on SE); some SE wifi (slow); hard seat packed, smoky. PRIVATE CARRIAGES Hanoi–Lao Cai: Orient Express, Victoria (pricey), Livitrans from US$52 + six others; Livitrans Hanoi–Hue US$75, –Danang US$85; Golden Trains HCMC–Nha Trang US$35–43 (all 2016 LEADS). SLEEPERS: hard = 3 tiers, 6 per compartment, UPPER CHEAPEST, lower dearest; soft = 2 tiers, 4 berths, same price; bring a sleep sheet/pillowcase though linen is provided. Children under 2 free, 2–9 half price; no discounts Hanoi–Lao Cai. Freight: bikes/motorbikes ~375k per overnight, may travel on a different train — note which.
- p497: REUNIFICATION EXPRESS box: 1,726 km Hanoi–Saigon line begun 1899, finished 1936; 1930s trip 40 h 20 min at 43 km/h; sabotaged by Viet Minh, bombed by US; VC attacks 1961–64 (795) forced abandonment incl. the Dalat spur; bomb craters still visible at every northern bridge/station; link restored and the Reunification Express inaugurated 31 Dec 1976 after 1,334 bridges, 27 tunnels, 158 stations repaired; TODAY ~50 km/h average, "only slightly faster than the 1930s", mostly single-track, <1% of north–south freight; high-speed plan "shelved", gradual upgrade to ~90 km/h by 2020 (2016 — the high-speed line was approved Nov 2024; LEAD). BOOKING 2016: dsvn.vn took only Vietnamese cards then (now takes international — LEAD); Bao Lau (baolau.vn) accepts foreign cards, e-tickets, 40,000d commission; Vietnam Impressive delivers to hotels; reserve 60–90 days ahead on long trips; sleepers a week+ ahead; "fast" trains HCMC–Hanoi 32–36 h. SAFETY: petty theft as trains pull out of stations — tie bags, esp. at night. Routes: main line + three spurs (Haiphong; Lang Son→Nanning; Lao Cai→Kunming). Schedules change often — check VR, Bao Lau, seat61. TET: bare-bones schedule, most trains suspended for NINE DAYS, four before to four after Tet.

## Survival Guide: Health (p498–502)
- p498: Cities low risk with good facilities, rural "another matter"; serious infectious illness rare; ACCIDENTS (traffic) cause most life-threatening problems; a bout of sickness common. Insurance: evacuation bills of US$100,000 "not unknown". Only required vaccine = yellow fever, and only if you were in a YF zone within six days (matches facts safety_health.vaccines_required). Before you go: labelled medications, doctor's letter, syringe letter, ECG copy for heart patients, spare meds; vaccines need 2 weeks, see a doctor 4–8 weeks out. Medical kit list (antibacterial cream, antifungal, antihistamines, antiseptic, DEET, loperamide, first-aid, paracetamol/ibuprofen, hydrocortisone, sunscreen). Sites: WHO, CDC, travelhealthpro.
- p499: Rural clinics basic — if seriously ill get to HCMC, Danang or Hanoi fast; for surgery fly to Bangkok, Singapore, Hong Kong. PRIVATE CLINICS first (Hanoi, HCMC, Danang near developed-country standard; can organise evacuation). STATE HOSPITALS overcrowded and basic; a facility needs a special licence to treat foreigners — few have it. Self-treat minor things only; malaria suspicion → nearest quality facility immediately. OTC medication not recommended — fakes and expired stock common. BIRD FLU in clusters among poultry workers, rarely fatal; eggs/poultry vanish from menus during outbreaks. DENGUE "increasingly problematic", several hundred thousand hospitalised yearly in Vietnam, fatality <0.3%, no vaccine (2016), prevention = bites. WHO vaccine list: diphtheria/tetanus booster if none in 10 years; hep A (booster → 20 yrs); hep B now routine; MMR two doses; typhoid unless <1 week in developed cities only (~70%, 2–3 yrs); varicella if never had chickenpox. Long-term/special risk: Japanese B encephalitis (3 shots), meningitis, rabies (3 shots), TB skin test before/after. (CDC list in facts.json governs; this corroborates.)
- p500: TAP WATER box: heavily chlorinated in cities but still avoid; bottled everywhere; ICE "generally safe in cities and resorts" (settles the tap-water dispute direction: don't drink tap, ice is OK in cities). Dengue mosquito bites DAY and night; symptoms; paracetamol not aspirin (haemorrhage risk). Hep A: all travellers vaccinate. Hep B: up to 20% carriers in parts of SE Asia. HIV: official line limits it to sex workers/drug users; condoms widely available. JE: rare in travellers, vaccinate for extended rural stays. MALARIA (LP 2016): "enormous misinformation"; cities and resorts minimal-to-no risk incl. Danang, Hanoi, HCMC, Nha Trang; most rural areas = risk outweighs tablet side effects; isolated high-risk areas named: Ca Mau and Bac Lieu provinces, the rural south — carry a treatment dose. Prevention list (screens, permethrin, nets, DEET on ankles, coils, light long clothes). Drugs: chloroquine+Paludrine not recommended; doxycycline (sunburn, thrush, pill interference, 4 weeks after); mefloquine ~90% effective, psychiatric side effects; Malarone best for divers and short high-risk trips. (CDC in facts.json: rural only, rare in deltas; LP's "rural south" risk is broadly consistent; CDC governs.)
- p501: Standby treatment option (Malarone 4 tablets × 3 days) — still need a facility within 24 h of fever. Measles still a problem incl. Hanoi. RABIES: dogs and monkeys; wash, iodine, immediate post-exposure treatment; pre-travel vaccine simplifies it; unvaccinated need immunoglobulin fast (scarce). Schistosomiasis from freshwater swimming (test 3 months after). STDs. TB rare for short-termers. Typhoid vaccine for >1 week or outside major cities, ~not 100%. Typhus rare. TRAVELLERS' DIARRHOEA hits 30–50% within two weeks, 80%+ bacterial → antibiotics work; hydrate; loperamide only a "stopper" for bus days, never with fever/blood. Amoebic dysentery rare; giardia relatively common ("eggy burps" not specific). AIR POLLUTION severe in the major cities — consult doctor if respiratory problems (matches facts safety_health.air_quality). FOOD: restaurants are the biggest diarrhoea risk; freshly cooked, avoid shellfish and buffets, peel fruit, cooked vegetables, busy high-turnover places.
- p502: Snakes (boots, don't poke logs; pressure-immobilise, no tourniquet, antivenom only in major cities); ticks (doxy); HEAT: take it easy on arrival, rehydration solution, salty food; heat exhaustion vs heatstroke (>41°C, emergency); prickly heat; bedbugs (move hotel); jellyfish mostly harmless — vinegar; leeches — iodine; fungal rashes; cuts infect and won't heal — wash and antiseptic immediately; SUNBURN even on cloudy days, SPF 30+, hat, avoid 10am–2pm. WOMEN'S HEALTH: sanitary products in cities; bring birth control; pregnancy: second trimester ideal, avoid remote areas, insurance for premature labour, no antimalarial fully safe, azithromycin safe for diarrhoea.

## Language (p503)
- Vietnamese ~85 million speakers worldwide; Mon-Khmer; MUONG is its closest relative. Six tones; in the SOUTH the low-rising and high-broken tones merge into one, which is why southern speech has effectively five. Monosyllabic words, no stress issue. North/south vocabulary differences marked (N)/(S) — e.g. "yes" is vang (N) / da (S). Pronunciation guide uses English approximations. Phrase lists not transcribed.

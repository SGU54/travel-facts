## Discovering Vietnam (pp8–13)
- p8: The book's own suggested itineraries: two 2-day city tours (Hanoi, HCMC) and one two-week Hanoi→HCMC run down Highway 1 hitting Halong, Hue, Hoi An, My Son, Quy Nhon, Nha Trang, Mui Ne, ending Saigon. Note the book defaults NORTH→SOUTH.
- p10: Hanoi airport Noi Bai given as 22 mi / 35 km north; taxi ~45 min, bus/minibus 1h+ (LEAD). Hanoi's sights cluster in Old Quarter, French Quarter, Ba Dinh — walkable. 2-day Hanoi plan: Day 1 Hoan Kiem + Old Quarter morning, French Quarter (Metropole, Opera, History Museum, Hoa Lo "Hanoi Hilton", Ambassador's Pagoda) afternoon. Day 2 Ba Dinh (Mausoleum, Stilt House, One Pillar, Citadel, Military History or Fine Arts), Temple of Literature + Ethnology Museum or West Lake afternoon. Extension: Thay & Tay Phuong pagodas west of city, half/full day.
- p11: HCMC airport Tan Son Nhat ~4 mi / 7 km north of centre, taxi ~30 min, longer at rush hour (LEAD). Book stresses locals still say "Saigon". 2-day plan: Day 1 Lam Son Sq (Municipal Theatre, Continental, Caravelle), People's Committee, Notre Dame, Reunification Palace; afternoon War Remnants Museum, Ben Thanh, Bitexco Skydeck. Day 2 Jade Emperor Pagoda, Le Van Duyet temple, History Museum, Botanical Gardens/Zoo; afternoon Cholon (District 5): Binh Tay market, Quan Am & Thien Hau pagodas, Giac Vien pagoda in D11. Extension: Cu Chi + Cao Dai Holy See day trip.
- p12: Two-week itinerary logistics: fly into HAN, out of SGN (open-jaw). Book's transport advice: Halong by tour from Hanoi, then CAR + DRIVER for the rest, because the train follows Highway 1 and stops at Hue, Nha Trang etc but NOT at My Son or Mui Ne. (Decision: train vs private car for the coast — car wins if you want the off-rail stops.) Day-by-day: D1 Hanoi; D2–3 Halong overnight; D4–5 Hue (Imperial City, capital 1802–1945; Perfume River boat; royal tombs, Tu Duc and Minh Mang best); D6 Danang (Marble Mountains, Cham Sculpture Museum, Dragon Bridge breathes fire); D7 Hoi An; D8 My Son (groups B & C best preserved, bombed in 1960s); D9 Quy Nhon.
- p13: D9 Quy Nhon pitched as "real Vietnam", few hawkers, Thap Doi Cham towers; D10–11 Nha Trang (cathedral, Long Son Pagoda, Oceanographic Institute, Po Nagar towers; island boat trip day 2); D12–13 Mui Ne (12-mi / 20-km beach, kitesurfing/windsurfing lessons, sand dunes); D14 HCMC. Extension: Mekong Delta 1 day to 1 week.

## Putting Vietnam on the Map (pp14–15)
- p14: Country sits 11°N of equator at its southern end; area ~128,000 sq mi / 331,000 sq km; coastline ~2,040 mi / 3,260 km; population ~92 million (2015 figure, LEAD — now ~100m); 58 provinces + 5 municipalities (LEAD, admin reorganised since). Hanoi capital, HCMC economic engine. Map page.

## A Portrait of Vietnam (pp16–21)
- p17: Three-region self-image: North (Red River Delta, Hanoi, mountains on three sides), Centre (beaches, Hue, Hoi An, Danang, DMZ remnants; highlands around Pleiku/Dalat in its lower half), South (HCMC + Mekong Delta, the rice belt). Country only ~31 mi / 50 km wide at narrowest. 54 recognised ethnic groups; Viet/Kinh ~86% of pop, coastal plains and deltas.
- p18: Minorities: highland groups in north and centre (e.g. Hmong), Hoa (ethnic Chinese) in lowland cities, Cham and Khmer in south coast + Mekong. Society Confucian-hierarchical, patriarchal in structure, elders respected, education prized; women formally equal but run the home. ~1,000 years of Chinese occupation shaped customs/architecture — love-hate: borrow the culture, reject the politics. French legacy = city architecture + food (baguette). Viet Kieu (overseas Vietnamese) returning to do business; older generation of exiles often refuse to go back. Youth westernised (English, phones, jeans); ao dai still worn for school, ceremonies, formal settings.
- p19: Religion = "Tam Giao" triple religion (Buddhism + Taoism + Confucianism) layered with ancestor worship, spirit beliefs, Cham-Hindu traces; large Catholic population; home-grown Cao Dai and Hoa Hao. Tolerated as long as they don't challenge the Party. Language: tieng Viet first language of ~87%; chu nom (adapted Chinese characters) from 11th c; quoc ngu Romanised script devised by European missionaries 17th c, now standard; strong regional dialect differences. Literature: Tale of Kieu (Nguyen Du) the national classic; Bao Ninh's Sorrow of War the war novel to know.
- p20: Economy: doi moi reforms 1986; poverty 58% (1993) → under 16% (2008) per World Bank; world's #2 rice exporter (2015 claim); ASEAN 1995, WTO 2006; slowdown from 2008 as SOE reform stalled. Politics: one-party state, Party congress every 5 years; dissent punished; social media blocking from 2009; corruption slows reform. (Named leaders are 2015-era — obsolete.) Conservation: ~30,000 ha forest lost per year (2015 claim).
- p21: Tourism opened early 1990s; war-image problem the country actively pushed against; hotels/restaurants privatised; book notes road and rail "need major upgrading" but airports and national airline good (2015 view — note the 2015 road/rail picture is now dated: new expressways and the north-south high-speed rail plan). Tourism growth ~20%/yr pre-2008. Traditional arts (water puppetry, festivals) revived by tourism money.

## Landscape & Wildlife (pp22–23)
- p22: Four landscape zones: the deltas (Red River = almost entirely farmed; Mekong = farming plus marsh/mangrove wildlife), Central Highlands (red volcanic soil → coffee, tea, rubber around Pleiku/Kontum; Yok Don NP for elephants), central coastline, northern mountains. Best birding = Mekong Delta (Tam Nong / sarus crane); best big national parks = north (Cuc Phuong etc); coral islands offshore.
- p23: Butterfly seasons: Cuc Phuong NP clouds of butterflies April–May (a seasonal hook); Tam Dao 300+ species, Cat Tien 440. Central coast = long narrow flat strip, best beaches around Nha Trang. Northern mountains ring the Red River Delta on three sides, the least accessible part of the country; logging/roads threaten it.

## Peoples of Vietnam (pp24–25)
- p24: 54+ recognised groups; Kinh ~86%, of southern-Chinese origin, on coast + deltas, sharing plains with Hoa (Chinese), Khmer, Cham. ~50 other groups in northern + central highlands. Northern groups (Thai, Hmong) migrated from China; Central Highlands groups largely indigenous. Khmer Oc Om Boc festival has a masked monkey dance. Bahnar communal houses (nha rong) with tall tapered roofs. Cham Bani = indigenous form of Shiite Islam.
- p25: Thai = second-largest minority, Black/White/Red subgroups by clothing + rivers; Black Thai farm NW upland paddies. Flower Hmong famed for layered embroidery (Bac Ha market reference pp202–3). Red Dao women shave hair and eyebrows, red turbans; literate in a Chinese-derived script. Muong weave under stilt houses. Bru and Mnong in Central Highlands (Mnong = former elephant catchers, matrilineal). Etiquette relevance: minority villages are living communities, not shows.

## Religions of Vietnam (pp26–27)
- p26: Tam Giao = Mahayana Buddhism + Confucianism + Taoism blended; also Theravada (Khmer areas). Incense burning everywhere, even in Catholic churches. Family tombs sit in paddy fields — part of the landscape. Confucian hierarchy extends to distant cousins.
- p27: Cao Dai founded by civil servant Ngo Van Chieu; seances with "divine agents"; saints include Joan of Arc, Pasteur, Chaplin; ~3 million followers; priests in yellow/blue/red for the three teachings; Divine Eye in triangle; Holy See at Tay Ninh (pp78–9). Ancestor altars in most homes; ghost money (often fake USD) and paper goods burned for the dead. ~9 million Christians, 90%+ Catholic (2015 figure). Hoa Hao = puritanical Buddhist sect in Mekong Delta, anti-communist during the war. Cham: Hindu on central coast, Muslim in Mekong. Animism among hill tribes — spirit houses and shrine offerings.

## Music, Theatre, Architecture (pp28–31)
- p28: Five-tone scale; Chinese opera + Cham/Indian rhythm influences. Quan Ho = spring-festival call-and-response courtship singing (Bac Ninh). Ca Tru = chamber "singing for reward", UNESCO urgent-safeguarding list 2009. Hat Chau Van = trance ritual singing. Dan bau single-string instrument is the iconic one. Nhac tai tu = southern chamber music behind cai luong theatre.
- p29: Theatre forms: cheo (village moral theatre, Red River Delta), roi nuoc water puppetry (post-harvest entertainment; Hanoi venue ref p163), tuong/hat boi (court classical, Chinese-opera influenced, red face = loyal, white face = villain), cai luong (southern "reformed" theatre, early 20th c, spoken scenes, social issues). Nha nhac Hue court music: 13th c origin, peak under Nguyen, revived; UNESCO 2003.
- p30: Pagoda anatomy: single-storey timber halls on ironwood columns, front/central/altar halls ascending; pond, bell tower, garden; dragons everywhere. Tran Quoc on West Lake claimed as oldest (6th c, relocated after erosion). One Pillar Pagoda: lotus-on-a-pillar design, burned 1954, now on concrete. Multi-tier towers are Chinese-derived.
- p31: Hue citadel = Chinese square-wall + feng shui + French Vauban military engineering (moats, bastions). Ngo Mon gate has five openings, the centre for the emperor only. Hanoi was the capital of French Indochina → villas, Parisian-style ministries, Gothic cathedral; Presidential Palace 1900–06. TUBE HOUSES: from Later Le era (1428–1788), as narrow as 2 m / 6.5 ft, up to 80 m deep; modern "rocket buildings" are the same deeds gone vertical — explains the skinny-tall streetscape an American notices immediately.

## Tet (pp32–33)
- p32: Tet Nguyen Dan = lunar new year, the single biggest festival. Week before: debts cleared, tombs cleaned, homes decorated with peach blossom (north) or kumquat trees, offerings to the Kitchen God/Jade Emperor. The THREE main days are domestic — families at home. Ancestors believed to visit; paper offerings burned. Flower markets explode in the run-up (photo opportunity).
- p33: Tet foods: banh chung / banh tet sticky-rice cakes, chicken, dried fruits, watermelon. Human chess played only at Tet. Lion/unicorn dance. FIRECRACKERS BANNED SINCE 1994 (even recordings) — paper-firecracker garlands paraded instead (LEAD: check current law; organised state fireworks displays do happen now).

## Vietnam Through the Year (pp34–37)
- p34: Traditional festivals follow the lunar calendar (dates shift each year); secular holidays are fixed Gregorian and mostly revolutionary anniversaries. Tet officially 3 days but businesses often shut for a WEEK. Spring (Feb–Apr) is the most festive season. Month-1 festivals: Tet; Party founding Feb 3; Tay Son festival (Binh Dinh, elephants, drums, martial arts); Yen Tu pilgrimage (mid-Feb–end Apr); Lim festival (Bac Ninh, quan ho singing, 14 days after Tet); Perfume Pagoda festival runs Feb–May, three months of pilgrim crowds (so the Perfume Pagoda is PACKED in spring — timing decision). Month 2: Hai Ba Trung (Hanoi, early Mar); Ba Chua Kho (Bac Ninh, symbolic money-borrowing). Month 3: Thay Pagoda festival (water-puppet patron saint).
- p35: Hon Chen (Hue, 3rd and 7th lunar months, boat procession on Perfume River). Thanh Minh (grave-cleaning, early Apr). Hung Kings festival (Phu Tho, Apr, 3 days; public holiday). Liberation Day Apr 30 (fall of Saigon 1975). Summer (May–Jul) hot and wet: Labor Day May 1; HCM birthday May 19; Buddha's birthday (Le Phat Dan, lanterns); Tra Co village festival far NE. ZODIAC table: Vietnamese zodiac has CAT not rabbit; Horse year = 2026 (Tet 2026 = Horse). 60-year cycles.
- p36: Tet Doan Ngo (5th month, solstice, "killing inner insects"). Autumn (Aug–Oct): north cools, best time for northern festivals. Trung Nguyen / Vu Lan (7th month, mid-Aug, "wandering souls", second most important festival after Tet; paper money burnt). Le Van Duyet temple festival HCMC late Aug. National Day Sep 2 (Ba Dinh parades). Do Son buffalo fighting (Haiphong, early Sep; animals slaughtered after — sensitive for US readers). Trung Thu mid-autumn (mid-Sep, children, lanterns, moon cakes). Whale festival (Phan Thiet, Sep).
- p37: Kate festival (Cham, Po Klong Garai towers, Phan Rang, Sep–Oct, Cham calendar). Keo Pagoda (Thai Binh, mid-Oct). Confucius birthday = Teachers' Day (Nov 20 in modern VN — book says late Oct/early Nov). Winter (Nov–Jan): cold wet north, fewer festivals. Oc Om Boc + ngo boat races, Soc Trang, mid-Nov (Khmer moon festival; canoes from single trees). Nguyen Trung Truc festival An Giang late Nov (re-enacts sinking of French ship Esperance). Dalat Flower Festival Dec (LEAD: now biennial, odd years, check). Christmas celebrated big in cities. Jan 1 a holiday but minor. 2015 public holiday list: Jan 1; Tet (3 days official); Feb 3 Party day (not a day off); Hung Kings (10th day 3rd lunar month); Apr 30; May 1; May 19 (not a day off); Sep 2. LEAD: current law gives Tet 5 days off + Sep 2 two days; verify.

## Climate (pp38–39) — chart page, see screenshots
- p38 text: monsoon rains May–Oct generally, dry Nov–Feb; hot season Feb–Apr up to 35°C/95°F with 80–100% humidity. South: warm/humid all year, downpours in rainy season. Central coast: TYPHOONS Jul–Nov, winters rainy and cool. North: cold wet winters Nov–Mar (snow possible on Fansipan), warm humid summers. Note the book's "dry Nov–Feb" generalisation is wrong for the central coast (wettest Oct–Dec) — the chart itself shows it.

## History of Vietnam (pp40–53)
- p41: Narrative frame: ~1,000 years of Chinese occupation ending AD 938/979, independence, then colonial loss, then 1975 reunification — the book frames it as "constant invasions and a determination for autonomy". Origin myth: Lac Long Quan (dragon lord) + Au Co (mountain fairy) → 100 sons, half to mountains, half to the sea; eldest = first Hung King; Van Lang kingdom at Phu Tho ~80 km NW of Hanoi; Dong Son bronze drums linked to this era. 258 BC Au Lac kingdom, capital Co Loa near Hanoi = first historical Viet state.
- p42: 207 BC Trieu Da founds Nam Viet (capital in Guangdong) → start of ~1,000 years Chinese rule; 111 BC Han province of Giao Chi. Viets adopted Confucianism/Taoism/Buddhism/education but refused absorption. AD 40 Trung Sisters' uprising (3 years), the first and most famous. AD 679 Tang name "An Nam" (Pacified South), capital at Tong Binh (Hanoi). AD 938 Ngo Quyen beats Chinese fleet at Bach Dang River with iron-tipped stakes — the founding independence story.
- p43: Southern kingdoms: Funan (Mekong Delta, 1st–6th c, Indian-influenced, port of Oc Eo near Rach Gia, traded as far as Rome) then Chen La (Khmer). Champa from AD 192, Java-origin seafarers along central coast, at peak Vinh→Mekong; My Son religious capital 4th–13th c; 1471 crushing Viet defeat reduced Champa to Nha Trang–Phan Thiet strip, finally gone 1720 (king fled to Cambodia). Explains the Cham towers at Nha Trang, Phan Rang, Quy Nhon and the Cham Muslim/Hindu minority.
- p44: Dinh (968–980), Early Le (980–1009), Ly (1009–1225, first fully independent dynasty; 1010 capital moved to Thang Long = Hanoi, capital for ~800 years; 1070 Temple of Literature; Buddhism state religion, Confucian administration). Tran (1225–1400): 1288 Tran Hung Dao beats the Mongols at Bach Dang with the same stake trick. 1407 Ming invasion; 1428 Le Loi expels them → Later Le (1428–1788); 1471 defeat of Champa pushes frontier south of Quy Nhon.
- p45: 1527 Mac usurpation; from 1539 two warlord clans, Trinh (north, Thang Long) vs Nguyen (south, Hue), country divided 200+ years; Nguyen absorb Khmer Prey Nokor = Saigon; Mekong Delta conquest 1680–1757. Europeans: Portuguese 1545, Dutch then French 17th c; Jesuit Alexandre de Rhodes (quoc ngu script, mass conversions, expelled). Tay Son Rebellion 1771–92 (three brothers; Quang Trung beats a Chinese invasion). 1802 Nguyen Anh = Emperor Gia Long, capital Hue, Nguyen Dynasty (last dynasty, to 1945); name "Gia Long" fuses Gia Dinh (Saigon) + Thang Long (Hanoi).
- p46: Minh Mang (1820–41), Thieu Tri, Tu Duc (1847–83) all anti-Catholic; French pretext = missionary executions. 1858–9 Danang occupied; 1862 Saigon; 1865 Cochinchina colony; 1883 Annam + Tonkin protectorates (book garbles which is which: Tonkin = north, Annam = centre); 1887 Indochinese Union with capital Hanoi. Colonial economy: salt/alcohol/opium monopolies, corvée labour, coffee and rubber plantations. Governor Doumer 1897–1902. 1940 Vichy collaboration → Japanese occupation. Ho Chi Minh born 1890 near Kim Lien (Nghe An). VNQDD nationalist party modelled on China's 1911 revolution.
- p47: (OCR poor) 1924 Ho at Comintern; 1941 Viet Minh; 1945 August Revolution and independence declaration; 1946 First Indochina War; 1954 Dien Bien Phu; Geneva splits the country at the 17th parallel — North (Hanoi, Ho) and South (Saigon, Catholic Ngo Dinh Diem).
- p48: Vietnamese call it the "American War". US advisors 1960; NLF/Viet Cong formed 1960; 1963 Buddhist self-immolations and Diem assassinated; 1964 Gulf of Tonkin; 1965 first combat troops + bombing of North; ~500,000 US troops by 1967, mostly one-year conscripts. Ho Chi Minh Trail (p155 ref) — relevance to DMZ/Khe Sanh tours.
- p49: 1968 Tet Offensive — Hue held by communists 25 days (why Hue's citadel is so damaged); March 1968 My Lai (memorial near Quang Ngai, p123); 1969 Hamburger Hill (A Shau valley near Laos), Ho dies; 1972 napalm photo, Haiphong harbour bombed; 1973 Paris Accords, ~500 POWs released; April 29–30 1975 helicopter evacuation and fall of Saigon.
- p50: 1976 reunification as Socialist Republic; forced collectivisation in the south; re-education camps; Saigon renamed HCMC — book notes the name was "never fully accepted in the South"; Hoa merchants persecuted; boat people from 1977; US embargo after 1975; 1978 Vietnam invades Cambodia and ousts Pol Pot; 1979 China's punitive invasion of the north; 1980s near-starvation.
- p51: 1986 Le Duan dies → doi moi at 6th Congress; 1991 USSR collapse forces opening; 1994 US embargo lifted; 1995 US diplomatic relations + ASEAN; 2000 Clinton visit + stock exchange; 2001 trade normalisation; 2003 first US warship visit to HCMC; 2006 WTO-era PM Nguyen Tan Dung; 2008 inflation; 2011 land-grab protests; 2012 American citizen Nguyen Quoc Quan jailed for pro-democracy activity (a reminder for US readers: political activism is not tolerated); 2012–14 South China Sea tensions and anti-China riots. The book closes its history on an upbeat note: ordinary Vietnamese are freer now than under any earlier regime.
- Useful framing for an American reader: Vietnam's defining enemy in its own telling is China (1,000 years + 1979), not the US; the American War is one 15-year chapter. That is why Americans are welcomed without rancour — worth saying in the etiquette/history articles.

## Vietnam at a Glance (pp54–55)
- Book's six regions: HCMC (56–87), Mekong Delta & South (88–105), South Central (106–123: Mui Ne, Nha Trang, Dalat, Quy Nhon, Central Highlands), Central (124–155: Hoi An, Danang, Hue, DMZ), Hanoi (156–181), North (182–205: Halong, Sapa, Ninh Binh, Dien Bien Phu). Sapa terraces = Hoang Lien mountains. Old Quarter = "36 Streets", 13th-c origin as palace suppliers. Tra Vinh = Khmer-Buddhist delta town. Mui Ne: 20 km beach; WINDY Oct–Feb = kitesurf season (so the "dry season" there is also the windy season — beach-vs-kite tradeoff). Rex Hotel rooftop = classic HCMC view.

## Ho Chi Minh City (pp56–61)
- p57: Khmer trading post → Nguyen provincial capital (Saigon, 18th c) → French Cochinchina capital ("Paris of the Orient") → capital of South Vietnam 1954 → renamed 1975. ~7 million (2015; now ~9–10m, LEAD). Dong Khoi + District 1 = tourist core: museums, colonial buildings, cafes, motorbike gridlock. Nightlife capital.
- p58: Layout logic: District 1 around Dong Khoi (Municipal Theatre, Notre Dame, GPO); north = Jade Emperor Pagoda; west = Cholon (District 5/6), Hoa Chinese, herbs/medicine, oldest pagodas. Region also covers Vung Tau, Long Hai, Ho Coc beach, Binh Chau hot springs, Nui Ba Den, Cu Chi, Cao Dai Holy See, Cat Tien NP, Thu Duc. Hotels the book treats as sights: Caravelle, Continental, Rex. Bus stations: Mien Dong (east/north routes), Mien Tay (Mekong routes) — both shown on the map.
- p59 Getting Around box: Dong Khoi and Cholon each walkable, but moving between them isn't; the book's default = xe om (motorbike taxi), "a few dollars" anywhere in town (2015 LEAD — now Grab app pricing); radio-dispatched metered taxis "common now" (2015 view: Mai Linh / Vinasun era; Grab has since displaced much of it); travel agencies arrange out-of-town trips. NOTE: the book predates Grab's dominance and the Metro Line 1 (opened Dec 2024) — both are coverage gaps in the 2015 picture, which our articles already fill.
- p60: Dong Khoi = former Rue Catinat, setting of The Quiet American; shut under early communism, revived after 1986. GPO interior as a cool refuge; Notre Dame built of local stone with French red tiles (LEAD: cathedral under long renovation since 2017 — check status before sending readers); People's Committee Building = ex-Hotel de Ville, photogenic but not enterable.

## Mekong Delta & Southern Vietnam (pp88–91)
- p89: Mekong ~4,500 km from Tibet; Vietnamese name Cuu Long = Nine Dragons (nine mouths). Rice basket + fruit basket (coconut, longan, mango). History: Cambodian claim; 1978 Khmer Rouge border massacres; Agent Orange; frequent floods. Can Tho and Rach Gia modernising; Khmer/Viet/Chinese pagodas everywhere. Nature: Ha Tien beaches + karst; Bac Lieu bird marshes; Phu Quoc and Con Dao as eco destinations (2015 view — Phu Quoc has since become a mass-resort island; LEAD).
- p90: Delta logic for a visitor: My Tho = nearest launch pad from HCMC (the day-trip default), Vinh Long just south (homestay country), Can Tho = largest city + floating markets (Cai Rang), Chau Doc = floating houses + Cambodia border, Soc Trang/Tra Vinh = Khmer culture, Con Dao NP, Phu Quoc reefs, Ha Tien secluded beaches. Towns listed: My Tho, Ben Tre, Tra Vinh, Vinh Long, Cao Lanh, Can Tho, Soc Trang, Bac Lieu, Rach Gia, Chau Doc, Ha Tien; islands: Phoenix Island (My Tho), Con Dao, Phu Quoc.
- p91 Getting Around: airports at Can Tho, Con Dao, Rach Gia, Phu Quoc (2015; all still operate, LEAD on routes). Highways reliable, rural roads bumpy, local buses erratic. Agencies in HCMC and the delta sell coach/car trips. Book's verdict: BOAT is the way to see the delta; ~2,800 km of canals; public boats exist but private tours are more comfortable. Bikes/motorbikes rentable everywhere. DECISION the book leaves open: public boat vs private tour vs coach day-trip — it says private is "more comfortable" and never weighs cost or depth. NOTE: book predates the HCMC–Can Tho expressway (Trung Luong–My Thuan–Can Tho, opened in stages 2010–2023) and the Cambodia–Chau Doc speedboats growth; LEAD.

## South Central Vietnam (pp106–109)
- p107: Region = old Champa heartland. Champa peaked 4th c AD from Ngang Pass to the Mekong; shrank from AD 1000; last principality Panduranga (Phan Rang–Phan Thiet) fell 1832. Cham minority still in Phan Rang area; Kate festival early fall. Phan Thiet beach 18 km / 11 mi to Mui Ne; Mui Ne "fastest-growing resort" (2015). Nha Trang = seafood + offshore islands + watersports. Dalat = French hill station, cool, "honeymoon" town. Buon Ma Thuot and Kontum = Bahnar/Ede/Jarai villages, nha rong longhouses. Son My / My Lai memorial near Quang Ngai.
- p108: Coast gets far more visitors than interior. Mui Ne and Nha Trang = bases. Northern fishing towns (Quy Nhon, Sa Huynh, Quang Ngai) skipped by most, but have beaches + Cham towers. Dalat most pleasant highland base; Buon Ma Thuot for Yok Don NP (largest reserve); Kontum roads "less traveled", access to some minority areas "still limited due to unrest" (2015 — LEAD, Central Highlands permit situation has eased but check). Dambri Falls, Ta Cu Mountain, Lak Lake, Po Klong Garai towers at Phan Rang–Thap Cham.
- p109 Getting Around: Highway 1 and the HCMC–Hanoi railway are the straightforward spine; coastal buses + hotel/agency minibuses (the "open tour bus" model). Dalat easy; Route 27 from the coast (Phan Rang → Dalat) is scenic. Highlands: minibuses exist but car+driver or motorcycle "might serve better"; Route 14 from HCMC upgraded but winding/steep north of Buon Ma Thuot. Doc Let beach north of Nha Trang (Hon Heo peninsula) flagged as a white-sand alternative. NOTE: Dalat has an airport (Lien Khuong) the box doesn't mention; Cam Ranh (Nha Trang) airport likewise — LEAD.

## Central Vietnam (pp124–127)
- p125: Four UNESCO sites in one region: Phong Nha (cave/karst), My Son, Hue Citadel, Hoi An Old Quarter. Settlement confined to the narrow coastal strip; Truong Son range = Laos border. Hai Van Pass = the scenic vantage. Hoi An: Chinese, Japanese, French trader buildings from 16th c. Hue: citadel + royal tombs, Nguyen 1802–1945. My Son 4th–12th c. All show war damage. Ho Chi Minh's childhood villages Hoang Tru / Kim Lien (Nghe An). DMZ just north of Hue; Khe Sanh and Vinh Moc = "pilgrimage" sites for both Vietnamese and Americans.
- p126: Book's base logic: HUE is the base for everything north of the pass (DMZ day-trip, Phong Nha, Lang Co beach — "one of the best in the region"); HOI AN / Danang for south of the pass (My Son, Ba Na hill station, China Beach, Cham Islands, Son Tra peninsula, Bach Ma NP). Sights: Hoi An pp128–33, My Son 134–6, Danang 138, Hue 142–9, Dong Hoi, Kim Lien, Vinh Moc tunnels, Khe Sanh, DMZ, beaches China/Lang Co/Thuan An.
- p127 Getting Around: book's first choice = RENT A CAR (meaning car + driver), fallback = agency minibuses for day trips (DMZ from Hue; Hai Van Pass en route Hue↔Danang). Reunification Express also serves the region. Hoi An and Hue explored by bike or on foot. Perfume River boat from the Le Loi Street wharf in Hue. NOTE: book predates the Hai Van tunnel-vs-pass choice being framed as a motorbike "Easy Rider" experience; our hai-van-pass-guide covers it. LEAD: Danang–Quang Ngai expressway (2018) and the Hue–Danang train scenic section still run.

## Hanoi (pp156–159)
- p157: Founded 1010 by Ly Thai To as Thang Long near Co Loa; citadel at centre, guild settlement to the east became the Old Quarter by 16th c. French demolished parts of citadel/temples for the European quarter but left the colonial architecture. Hanoi's centre escaped the First Indochina War; capital of independent Vietnam 1954; bombed in the American War. Food = French + Chinese + Viet blend. "A little run down but structurally sound" (2015 tone).
- p158: Orientation: Hoan Kiem Lake is the pivot; Old Quarter ("36 Streets") north of it; French Quarter boulevards south; Temple of Literature + Ba Dinh/Mausoleum west; West Lake (Ho Tay) + Truc Bach north-west. Day-trip sights in the Hanoi chapter: Thay + Tay Phuong pagodas, Hung Kings' temples, Co Loa citadel, Museum of Ethnology (Cau Giay). Kim Ma bus station marked. Hotel-as-sight: Metropole.
- p159 Getting Around: Old Quarter + Hoan Kiem on foot. ## Phrase Book (pp300–303) — language facts only; no phrase lists copied
- p300: Vietnamese = Mon-Khmer/Austroasiatic; Hanoi dialect = standard; central/southern dialects differ in tones (fewer) and vocabulary, not grammar. Chinese chu han for admin for centuries → chu nom → quoc ngu (17th c Jesuits), made official under the French, first resisted as colonial then accepted as easy. SIX TONES; "ma" has six meanings. KINSHIP ADDRESS: anh (older brother → young man), chi (older sister → young woman), em (anyone younger), ong/ba (grandfather/grandmother → respectful to elders), co (madam).

Which base first, which last, and where does the boat go?

Free sampleDecision 02 of 11, given away

This is one of the eleven connections the book makes. It is given away whole, figure and arithmetic included, so you can judge the rest of it before paying for it.

Ask where to stay in Bali and every answer is a list of places. The list is the easy half. The order the places come in is decided by three things the trip cannot move: the hour the long flight lands, the sea under the one boat crossing, and the drive to the airport on the last morning. Most people settle the order by booking the beach first, because the beach is the picture, and only later discover that each of the three pins has landed in the worst place it could.

The reason this is a decision and not a preference is that the three pins are not symmetrical. Jet lag from the United States is about as strong as jet lag gets, and it has one useful property: it wakes you early. The site's day-plans use it deliberately, opening in Ubud so that the first mornings fall among rice terraces and spring temples before the coaches arrive, and so that the thing you most want to see is not scheduled for a version of yourself that is not fully present. Spend those mornings on a west-facing beach and they are spent asleep, because the beach's hour is sunset and the body's is five a.m. ●

The sea is the second pin and the least forgiving. Every island in a Bali trip is reached by small fast boat across open water, and the harbour master's office for the Sanur route issues a sailing approval for each departure and has told operators not to sail in poor weather. No cancellation rate exists, so none is printed; what the site does print is the margin: at least one full day between the boat back and an international flight, two in July and August or the wet season. The trap is that the dry season is not the calm season. Travel guides describe the sea between Bali and Lombok as roughest in July and August, when the swells are strongest, and the fast boat that capsized at Sanur in August 2025 was in waves forecast at two to four metres. A boat landing at Sanur at midday with a flight that evening looks comfortable on paper and assumes the morning sailing ran. From the Nusa islands, if it did not, the only way back is still by sea.

The third pin is the only drive on the island with a deadline. The airport sits on a neck of land in the south, four road miles from Kuta, seven from Seminyak, eleven from Sanur, twenty-two from Ubud and sixty-one from Amed, and every one of those passes through the busiest roads on the island. The site prints no drive times because none would be true at the hour you read it; what it does say is that the leaving time is an output of where the last night is, not a separate choice. A last night on the coast makes departure day short and dull. A last night in the hills, or on an island, makes it long and tense. ●

Put the three together and the order writes itself, which is why the site's own week, ten-day and fortnight routes all run the same way: Ubud, then the island or the far side, then the coast. The exception the site allows is a late-night arrival, where a drive inland in the dark after twenty-four hours of travel argues for a first night on the coast instead, close to the terminal, and a move inland after breakfast. That is a one-night adjustment, not a reversal of the order.

Everything else about the itinerary is negotiable. These three are not, and a booking made in the wrong order cannot be corrected by anything bought later.

The call

Inland first, the island in the middle, the airport's coast last. Open in Ubud or its villages, where the dawn waking of jet lag is spent among terraces and temples that are at their best at that hour and at their worst at eleven. Put the boat, if there is one, in the middle third, so the return crossing has at least one full day of slack behind it, two in July, August or the wet months. Finish on the south coast, seven to eleven road miles from the terminal, with no boat and no long drive between the last full day and the flight. The same ten nights in the reverse order spend the dawn hours asleep on a beach, put the only weather-dependent link against the flight home, and end twenty-two miles inland on the morning of a hard deadline. Travel guides put the crossing from Sanur at roughly thirty minutes to Nusa Penida and thirty-five to forty-five to Lembongan, and the Padang Bai boat to the Gilis at about an hour and a half; none is an official timetable.

When the other call is right
  • then coast first → the flight lands late at night; take the shortest drive to a bed and move inland in the morning
  • then no island at all → seven nights, or a traveller uneasy in small boats; East Bali gives the quiet without the sea
  • then the island nearer the end → a Gili leg done by air via Lombok, flown back two days before the international flight; the strait is out of it
When ours is wrong for you
  • then reverse it → a surf trip in the dry season where the west coast is the whole point and Ubud is a day trip; even then, sleep the last night near the airport
  • then skip Ubud → a resort honeymoon in Nusa Dua that came to rest; the dawn hours are spent at the pool, knowingly
  • then keep the boat out of it → a wet-season week; the crossing costs more of a short trip than it gives

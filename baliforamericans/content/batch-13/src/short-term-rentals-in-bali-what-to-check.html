Before booking a villa or short-term rental in Bali, check four things: that the property is licensed accommodation, that the listing is real, what the final price is once tax and service are added, and what the deposit and cancellation terms say. Bali's rules for visitors require a stay in licensed accommodation. Paying through a platform with a card protects you better than a bank transfer to a stranger.

The license requirement

Bali's rules for foreign tourists, set out in the governor's circular of 2025, require visitors to stay in accommodation businesses that hold a license under the regulations. The rule is addressed to the visitor. An unlicensed room in a private house, however pleasant, is outside what the circular allows.

How to check

We have no official public register to point you to, so the practical step is to ask. Request the property's business license details in writing before you book, and note the answer. A professionally run villa will answer without difficulty. Hesitation or a change of subject tells you something as well.

Why it matters to you

The circular says violations by foreign tourists will be dealt with firmly under Indonesian law, and the US Consular Agency in Bali reports a current enforcement campaign by Indonesian authorities. We have no source on how the accommodation rule is enforced in practice, so we describe no penalty. The simple course is to stay somewhere that is licensed.

Is the listing real?

A rental listing is a set of photographs and a promise. The State Department warns that financial scams happen in Indonesia and advises caution with anyone asking for money. The classic rental fraud is a listing copied from a real property, offered at a low price, with payment requested outside any platform.

Signs to treat with caution

These are our own suggestions, not an official list.

  • A price far below similar villas in the same area.
  • A request to pay by bank transfer or cryptocurrency instead of through the platform.
  • Pressure to decide today.
  • No reviews, or reviews that all appeared in the same week.
  • Photographs that turn up on other listings under another name.

How to verify

Search for the property's name and photographs outside the listing. Look for it on a map and in street-level imagery. Ask the host for a short video call from the property. A real owner can show you the pool; someone using stolen photographs cannot.

What the price really is

A villa's nightly rate is rarely the final figure. In Badung regency, which covers Seminyak, Canggu, Uluwatu and Nusa Dua, the local tax on accommodation is 10%, and a business may add a service charge. Platforms add their own fees. Compare properties on the total for your dates.

What is included

Ask what the rate covers. Daily cleaning, breakfast, staff, electricity, pool maintenance and airport transfers are included in some rentals and charged separately in others. For longer stays, electricity in particular is sometimes billed by the meter. Get the list in writing.

Currency

Bali's rules for visitors say transactions should be made in rupiah. If a price is quoted in dollars, ask what exchange rate applies on the day of payment. The State Department advises checking with your card issuer about international transaction fees.

Deposits and cancellation

Deposits are where rental disputes begin. There are two kinds: a booking deposit, paid in advance to hold the dates, and a security deposit, held against damage. Ask how much each is, when it is taken, how it is returned and within how many days. We have no official source on local practice, so we describe none.

The security deposit

On arrival, walk through the property with the manager and photograph anything already damaged, with the date visible. Do the same at departure. A written inventory, signed by both sides, is the best protection either of you has.

Cancellation

Read the cancellation terms before paying. A long-haul trip from the United States has several ways to go wrong, and a non-refundable villa is a large sum to lose. Check whether your travel insurance covers cancellation and on what grounds. The State Department highly recommends travel insurance for Indonesia.

Checking the property itself

A villa is a house, and the things that matter in a house matter here. Location comes first: look at what is within walking distance, since a villa down a long lane means a ride for every meal. Then ask about the practical matters a listing leaves out.

Questions to ask

  • Is the bedroom enclosed and air-conditioned, or open to the garden?
  • Is there a fence or gate around the pool?
  • How is drinking water supplied?
  • Is there staff on site at night, and a phone number that is answered?
  • How far is the nearest road a car can reach?

Why those questions

Each has a reason. The CDC advises sleeping in air-conditioned or screened rooms to avoid mosquito bites. An unfenced pool is a hazard for children. The State Department says tap water in many areas of Indonesia is not safe to drink. And some villas are reached only by a footpath.

Villa or hotel

A villa gives space, privacy and a private pool. A hotel gives a front desk, security, a restaurant and someone to call when something breaks. Our view is that a first-time visitor on a short trip is often better served by a hotel, and that villas come into their own for groups, families and longer stays.

Where the villa is matters more than the villa

A beautiful villa in the wrong area is still in the wrong area. Which part of the island suits your trip comes before which property, and that choice is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

Are short-term rentals legal in Bali?

Bali's rules require foreign tourists to stay in licensed accommodation. Ask the property for its business license details before booking.

How do I avoid fake villa listings in Bali?

Book through a platform, pay by card, be wary of prices far below the market, and verify the property on a map and by video call.

Is tax included in Bali villa prices?

Not always. In Badung regency the local tax on accommodation is 10%, and a service charge may be added. Compare totals.

Should I pay a villa deposit by bank transfer?

Our advice is to pay by card through a platform where you can. A transfer to an individual is hard to recover.

For most trips of a week or more, two bases in Bali work better than one. The island's beach areas and its cultural center are in different regions, and a single base means a long day trip to reach whichever one you did not choose. One base suits a short trip or a traveler who wants to unpack once. Two suit anyone who wants both the sea and Ubud.

Why the question comes up

Bali's main attractions are split between the coast and the hills. The resort beaches are in the south. The rice terraces, temples and arts are around Ubud, which is about 22 miles from the airport to Ubud by road and about 20 from Seminyak. No single address puts both on your doorstep.

The day-trip alternative

The alternative to a second base is a day trip. From a southern base, Ubud and back is a full day with a long drive at each end. We give no drive times, because they vary by hour. One such day is manageable. Three of them is a commute, and the reason people split their stay.

The case for one base

One base has real advantages. You unpack once, you learn the neighborhood, and you lose no day to moving. It suits a trip of five nights or fewer, a traveler recovering from a long flight, and families with young children, for whom every hotel change is work.

Choosing the single base

With one base, choose the one that matches the larger part of what you want. A trip mostly about beach, food and rest belongs in the south, with a day trip to Ubud. A trip mostly about culture and landscape belongs in Ubud, with a day at the coast, which is about 14 miles away at Sanur.

What one base costs you

The cost of one base is whatever lies far from it. From Seminyak, the eastern temples and the mountain lakes are long days. From Ubud, a swim in the sea is a drive. Accept that plainly when you book, and resist the urge to fix it with back-to-back day trips.

The case for two bases

Two bases let each part of the trip happen where it belongs. Beach days are spent at the beach, and temple and terrace days start a short ride from the temples and terraces. The transfer between the two takes part of one day, and it can include sightseeing stops on the way.

What a move costs

A move costs a morning of packing, a drive and a check-in. Our view is that a move is worth it when you gain at least two full days in the new place. A base of two nights gives one full day, which is rarely enough to justify the change; three nights is our working minimum.

Turning the transfer into a day out

The drive between bases is a chance to see what lies between them. A driver hired for the transfer can stop at a temple, a viewpoint or a lunch place on the way. That turns a moving day into a useful one and saves a separate outing.

Three bases and more

A third base makes sense on a longer trip. With ten nights or more, a traveler can add one of the quieter regions: Sidemen or Amed in the east, Munduk in the highlands, or one of the Nusa islands. Each is far enough from the south that a day trip is impractical.

When to stop adding

Every added base costs a moving day. Our advice is to count nights, not places: divide the nights you have by three, and treat the result as the upper limit on bases. A two-week trip with five bases is a trip spent in cars.

Islands are a special case

An island base adds boat crossings, which depend on the sea. Travel guides give the crossing from Sanur to the Nusa islands as roughly 30 to 45 minutes. An island leg is a base with a weather-dependent link at each end, and it needs slack around it.

How to decide for your trip

The decision between one base and two comes down to three questions. How many nights do you have? Do you want both the coast and the hills, or mainly one? And how much do you mind packing? These are our own suggestions for a starting point.

  • Up to 5 nights: one base.
  • 6 to 9 nights: two bases, one on the coast and one in Ubud.
  • 10 nights or more: two or three bases.

Travelers who should stay put

Some travelers are better off with one base whatever the length of the trip: families with small children, anyone with limited mobility, and those who came to rest. A day trip or two with a driver covers the highlights without a hotel change.

Practical notes on splitting

Book a driver for the transfer day in advance and agree the stops and the price in writing. Check checkout and check-in times, since a gap between them is where the sightseeing stops fit. Confirm that each place is licensed accommodation, as Bali's rules for visitors require.

What this decision actually sits inside

Notice what happened across all of this. One base or two was a question about nights, and nights were not the only thing in play. The order of the bases matters, since the first one follows a long flight and the last one precedes another. An island leg brings the sea into it. The split is where the whole shape of a trip gets decided.

FAQ

Should I stay in one place or two in Bali?

For a trip of a week or more, two bases usually work better: one on the coast and one in Ubud. For five nights or fewer, stay in one.

How many nights should I spend in each base?

Our working minimum is three nights per base, which gives two full days.

Can I see Ubud on a day trip from Seminyak?

Yes. It is about 20 miles each way, and a full day with the drive. Several such days are a good reason to split the stay.

How do I get between bases in Bali?

By car with a driver, who can stop at sights on the way. Agree the stops and the price in writing beforehand.

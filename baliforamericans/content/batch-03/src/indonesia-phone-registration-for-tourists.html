No. A tourist visiting Bali for a normal vacation does not need to register a phone with Indonesian customs. Indonesia does have a phone registration system that ties lasting use of its mobile networks to a registered device, but customs itself says short-stay visitors are outside it in the two ways that matter. The confusion comes from rules written for residents.

What the registration system is

Indonesia runs an IMEI registration system for phones brought in from abroad, and its customs service publishes the rules. The IMEI is the identifying number of a phone. Customs sets out two cases in which a short-stay visitor does not need to register at all.

Why it exists

The system is about imported devices and the duty owed on them. Indonesian customs lists IMEI registration among the matters a passenger declares on arrival, alongside goods above the duty-free value. A resident bringing a new phone home from abroad is the case it was built for.

What customs says about tourists

Indonesian customs answers the tourist question directly in its own guidance. For a person staying only temporarily, such as a tourist or business visitor, IMEI registration is not required in two situations: when the phone keeps using its home SIM on roaming, and when the visitor uses an Indonesian SIM registered at a mobile operator's outlet.

If you roam on your US plan

A phone that keeps its American SIM and connects through your US carrier's roaming is not affected. The registration rule concerns devices using an Indonesian signal provider, and a roaming phone is still a customer of its home network. Check your carrier's international rates before you rely on this for a whole trip.

If you buy a local SIM

A visitor who wants a local number registers the SIM at an operator's outlet, and customs says this gives network access for up to 90 days. That covers any trip on a 30-day tourist visa, including one that is extended. Our advice is to bring your passport to the outlet.

Where a travel eSIM fits

A travel eSIM bought from an international provider before you leave works like roaming from the phone's point of view: the plan belongs to a foreign provider and connects through partner networks in Indonesia. Customs' guidance names roaming on a home SIM and a local SIM registered at an outlet; it does not mention travel eSIMs by name.

Our reading

We read a foreign travel eSIM as falling on the roaming side of that line, since it is not an Indonesian subscription. That is an inference from the wording, not a statement by customs. If you want certainty, the two cases customs does name are the safe ones: your own carrier's roaming, or a local SIM set up at an operator's shop.

Check that your phone is unlocked

A local SIM or a travel eSIM only works in a phone that is not locked to your US carrier. This is a matter between you and your carrier, separate from Indonesian rules. Ask your carrier before you travel.

What to do at the airport

An American tourist on a short stay does not need to visit the customs registration desk for a personal phone. If you plan to roam or to use a local SIM from an operator's outlet, customs' own guidance says registration is not required, so you can walk past the counter with a clear conscience.

When the answer is different

The picture changes for someone staying long term. The 90-day access that comes with an outlet-registered tourist SIM is a limit as well as an allowance. Anyone planning to stay beyond that, or to leave a phone behind in Indonesia, is in the situation the registration system was designed for and should read the customs rules for residents.

Bringing a new, boxed phone

A brand-new phone in a sealed box is a different matter from the one in your pocket. Customs allows personal goods up to USD 500 per person free of duty and treats goods that are not for personal use as a declaration matter. A device you are bringing as a gift for someone who lives in Indonesia is outside the two visitor cases above, so ask customs at arrival how it should be handled.

Choosing how to stay connected

The registration rule leaves an American tourist three workable options for mobile service in Bali: roaming on a US plan, a travel eSIM bought in advance, or a local SIM bought at an operator's outlet. None of them requires dealing with customs. The choice comes down to cost, convenience and whether you want a local number.

Roaming

Roaming is the least effort, and the cost depends entirely on your US plan, so check its international rates. It keeps your US number working for calls and verification texts, which matters for banking apps that send codes by text.

Travel eSIM

A travel eSIM is set up before you fly and connects when you land, with no shop visit. Many such plans provide data only, in which case your US number remains on your physical SIM for texts if you leave that line switched on.

Local SIM

A local SIM gives you an Indonesian number, which is useful for apps and services that expect one. Buy it at an operator's own outlet, since that is the route customs names, and expect to show your passport.

FAQ

Do tourists need to register their phone in Bali?

No. Indonesian customs says IMEI registration is not required for short-stay visitors who roam on their home SIM or who use an Indonesian SIM registered at a mobile operator's outlet.

Will my phone be blocked in Indonesia?

Not on a normal vacation. A tourist SIM registered at an operator's outlet gives network access for up to 90 days, and a phone roaming on its home SIM is not affected.

Do I have to pay tax on my phone when entering Bali?

A personal phone you are carrying for your own use on a short visit does not need registration. A new, boxed device brought as a gift is an import and is treated differently.

Does a travel eSIM need IMEI registration in Indonesia?

Customs' guidance does not mention travel eSIMs by name. It exempts short-stay visitors roaming on a home SIM; we read a foreign travel eSIM as falling on that side, which is our inference.

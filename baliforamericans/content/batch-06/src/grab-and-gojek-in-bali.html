Grab and Gojek both operate in Bali, and for a visitor they are the simplest way to book a car or a motorbike ride at a price shown in advance. The State Department lists both as available options in Indonesia and notes that the apps show the driver, the vehicle and the fare. Grab runs airport pickups from its own lounge at the international terminal.

What Grab and Gojek are

Grab and Gojek are ride-hailing apps, used in Indonesia much as Uber and Lyft are used in the United States. The State Department describes licensed taxis and ride-hailing services as widely available in major Indonesian cities, and says the two apps provide driver identification, vehicle information and fare transparency through the phone.

Why the fare display matters

The fare shown in the app before you confirm is the practical advantage for a newcomer. There is no meter to watch and no price to negotiate in a language you do not speak. The State Department's advice for Indonesia is to arrange transportation through reputable taxi companies or ride-hailing applications.

Cars and motorbikes

Both apps offer rides on the back of a motorbike as well as in a car. Grab's own airport page lists car, premium car, electric car and motorbike options, along with advance booking. A motorbike ride is a ride on a motorcycle in Bali traffic, and Indonesian law requires a helmet for the passenger as well as the rider.

Using the apps as an American

Using Grab or Gojek as an American visitor needs a working data connection and a way to pay. Our advice is to set the app up before you leave home, in case it verifies your account by text message to your US number. Rides are booked and tracked on the phone, so a local SIM, an eSIM or roaming has to be working when you land.

Paying

Our advice is to check in the app at home which payment methods it offers you, to add a card if it accepts yours, and to carry small rupiah notes as a backup. Indonesia's cross-border QR payment scheme does not include the United States, so do not plan on paying by QR from a US banking app.

On Nyepi

Ride apps need mobile data, and on Nyepi, Bali's day of silence, mobile data is switched off for 24 hours, from 6 a.m. to 6 a.m. Plan any ride you need around the edges of that window, on the evening before and the morning after.

Grab and Gojek at Bali airport

Grab operates pickups at Bali's airport from a GrabCar Lounge at the International Arrival Terminal, with rides booked in the app. That gives an arriving American an alternative to negotiating a fare in the arrivals hall after a long journey. We have found no equivalent official page for Gojek at the airport, so we describe only Grab's arrangement.

What we could not confirm

Grab's page gives no fees or opening hours for the lounge and does not describe a pickup point at the domestic terminal. Check the app on the day. If the lounge is closed or the wait is long, the alternatives are a licensed airport taxi or a transfer arranged with your hotel before you fly.

Avoiding the touts

The State Department's advice is to avoid unlicensed taxis that solicit passengers directly, particularly in airports, tourist areas and transportation hubs. A driver who approaches you in the arrivals hall is the case it describes. Walk to the lounge or the official taxi counter instead.

Where the apps work less well

Grab and Gojek are city services, and the State Department's description of them refers to major Indonesian cities. In Bali that points to the built-up south. We have no official source describing coverage area by area, so we are not going to draw a map of where the apps work and where they do not.

Local restrictions

We have found no official source on whether any area of Bali restricts app pickups, so we state no rule. Our advice is to ask your accommodation what applies on its own street before you rely on an app to get home.

Our advice for remote areas

Our advice is to assume that an app ride out to a remote temple, waterfall or beach may not be matched by a ride back. For day trips away from the south, a driver booked for the day is the reliable arrangement. Ask your hotel how guests usually get back before you set out.

Staying safe in a ride

The State Department says most taxi and ride-hail trips in Indonesia occur without issues, and it gives simple checks. Confirm the driver and vehicle information before entering the vehicle, stay aware of your surroundings, and share your trip details through the application when possible. Match the license plate and the driver's photo to the app every time.

Taxis as the alternative

When an app has no cars, a licensed taxi is the fallback. The State Department describes Blue Bird taxis as generally considered the most reliable, and advises making sure there is a working meter and matching the photo on the dashboard ID with the driver before getting in.

Phones

The State Department warns of snatch-and-grab crime near roadways, and a phone held out at the curb while you watch for your car is an easy target. Stand back from the road while you wait, and put the phone away once the car has arrived.

What ride apps cannot fix

Ride apps make short trips in Bali simple to book, and they do nothing about how long those trips take. A car booked in seconds still sits in the same traffic as every other car. How much of a day a drive consumes is the larger planning question, and it is worked through in the complete Bali trip guide.

Arriving at Bali's airport means clearing four things in order: the visa, immigration, baggage and customs, with Bali's tourist levy handled before the trip or at a counter. Travelers who bought an e-VOA and filled in the arrival card before flying have little left to do at the airport. The last step is transport, where a ride app or an official taxi beats an offer in the hall.

What to have ready before you land

An American landing in Bali needs a passport, a visa or the money for one, an arrival card QR code and proof of an onward flight. The passport must be valid for 6 months beyond your arrival date and have 2 blank pages, and immigration expects an outbound ticket from Indonesia.

The arrival card

Every traveler submits Indonesia's arrival card online or in the official app, and it can only be completed within 72 hours of arrival. It produces a QR code, which the State Department says to save and show to immigration and customs. Keep a screenshot, since airport Wi-Fi is a poor thing to depend on at that moment.

The tourist levy

Bali's tourist levy is IDR 150,000 per person and is separate from the visa. It can be paid online before travel, with the voucher sent by email, or at payment counters at the airport. Paying it in advance leaves one less line to stand in after a long journey.

The visa counter and immigration

The visa comes first. A US citizen needs a tourist visa and can buy it online in advance or at the airport on arrival. The State Department says a visa can be purchased on arrival at the airport, and warns that travelers who do so may need to wait in long lines and that the process may take longer.

If you already have an e-VOA

A traveler with an e-VOA skips the payment counter and goes to immigration with the passport and the visa record. Keep the e-VOA confirmation on your phone and on paper. The official fee is IDR 500,000 whichever way the visa is bought.

If you buy the visa at the airport

A traveler without a visa pays at the visa on arrival counter before joining the immigration line. Have a payment card ready and, as a backup, cash. We have no official list of the currencies and cards the counter accepts on a given day, so carrying two ways to pay is our advice.

At the immigration desk

Immigration checks the passport, the visa and the arrival card. The visa allows a stay of 30 days, extendable once for another 30 days. Look at the stamp or the record you are given before you walk away, and note the date your stay ends.

Baggage and customs

Customs is the last official step, and the arrival card already carries your declaration. Personal goods are free of duty up to USD 500 per person per arrival, and an adult may bring 1 liter of alcohol. Alcohol or tobacco above the allowance is destroyed on the spot by the customs officer.

Medicine

Medicines are on the list of items that send a passenger to the customs inspection channel. The State Department says all prescription medication must be declared on the arrival card, carried in its original packaging with the prescription and a doctor's letter. Keep those papers in your hand luggage, not in the checked bag.

Cash

Cash worth IDR 100,000,000 or more must be declared. Few vacationers carry that much. The rule is a duty to declare and not a limit on what you may bring.

Money and a phone connection

The two things most travelers want before leaving the terminal are rupiah and mobile data. The State Department says Indonesia has ATMs that accept US-issued bank cards, which may charge a conversion or transaction fee, and it advises using ATMs in secure locations such as major bank branches and checking machines for tampering.

How much cash to take out

Our advice is to withdraw enough rupiah at the airport for the first day's transport, a meal and small purchases, and to do the rest later at a bank branch. Bali's rules for visitors say to change money only at authorized changers, which display a license number and a Bank Indonesia logo with a QR code.

Data

A ride app needs data the moment you want a car. A travel eSIM set up at home works on landing; a local SIM has to be bought and registered at an operator's counter first. Either way, a visitor's phone does not need to be registered with customs.

Getting out of the airport

Transport from Bali's airport comes in three forms: a ride booked in an app, an official airport taxi, and a car arranged in advance with your hotel or a driver. Grab operates pickups from a GrabCar Lounge at the International Arrival Terminal, with rides booked in the app.

The offers in the hall

The State Department's advice for Indonesia is to avoid unlicensed taxis that solicit passengers directly, particularly in airports. A person who approaches you with a price is the case it describes. Its further advice is to confirm the driver and vehicle before getting in and to make sure a taxi has a working meter.

A pre-arranged car

Our advice for a first arrival after a journey of this length is a car arranged before you fly, with the driver's name and a meeting point in writing. It costs more than an app ride and removes every decision from the moment you are least able to make one.

The first evening

The first evening in Bali follows a journey with at least one connection and a time difference of 12 hours or more from the United States. The CDC's advice on jet lag is to follow the destination's schedule and stay in well-lit areas during the day. Our advice is to plan nothing for arrival day beyond reaching your room.

How far to go on day one

The distance between the airport and your first bed is a choice you make when you book, long before you land, and it interacts with the hour your flight arrives. How the arrival time and the first night's location fit together is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

What do I need when I land in Bali?

A passport valid for six months with two blank pages, a tourist visa or the means to buy one, the arrival card QR code, and an onward ticket.

Can I buy a visa at Bali airport?

Yes. The State Department says a visa can be purchased on arrival and warns of long lines. Buying the e-VOA in advance avoids the payment counter.

Is there Grab at Bali airport?

Yes. Grab operates pickups from a GrabCar Lounge at the International Arrival Terminal, booked in the app.

Are there ATMs at Bali airport?

The State Department says Indonesia has ATMs that accept US-issued bank cards and advises checking any machine for tampering. We have no official list of the machines in the terminal.

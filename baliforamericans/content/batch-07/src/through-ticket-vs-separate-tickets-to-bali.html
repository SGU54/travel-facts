A through ticket puts both flights to Bali on one booking; separate tickets are two unrelated bookings that happen to meet at a hub. For a first trip, our advice is the through ticket. Separate tickets can cost less, and they move the risk of a missed connection and a stranded bag from the airline's booking to you.

What the two options are

A through ticket is a single booking that covers the whole journey from your US airport to Bali, with a connection on the way. Separate tickets are two bookings: one from the United States to a hub such as Singapore or Tokyo, and a second, bought independently, from that hub to Bali. No US airline flies to Bali nonstop, so one of the two applies to every traveler.

Why people split the ticket

The reason to buy separate tickets is price. A long-haul sale fare to an Asian hub combined with a regional flight into Bali can come to less than a single through fare. We quote no figures, because fares change daily. The saving is real on some dates and absent on others.

What a connection is, officially

The US Department of Transportation describes a connection as a change of planes, nearly always with a separate flight number for each flight. Its consumer guide ranks a connection between two different airlines as the least secure arrangement for checked bags, behind a nonstop, a through flight, and a connection on one airline.

What changes when a flight is late

A late first flight is where the two kinds of ticket differ. The Department of Transportation's advice for any connection is to ask yourself what will happen if the first flight is delayed, and to pick another flight or build a longer connection if you do not like the answer. With two separate bookings, that question has two airlines to answer it.

Ask before you buy

We have no official source on how any particular airline treats a passenger who misses a separately booked flight because another airline's flight was late, so we will not describe it as a rule. Our advice is to ask the second airline directly, before buying, what its policy is for a passenger who does not reach the gate in time.

What the law offers

The Department of Transportation notes that on international itineraries passengers may be able to recover expenses resulting from a delayed or canceled flight under Article 19 of the Montreal Convention, by filing a claim with the airline. That is a claim made afterward, with receipts. It is not a seat on the next flight.

Bags

Checked baggage is the practical test of a ticket. The Department of Transportation advises that if you have a connection involving two airlines, you should ask whether your bags will be transferred. On two separate bookings, plan on the possibility that the answer is no, which means collecting the bag at the hub and checking it in again.

What collecting a bag involves

Collecting a checked bag at a hub usually means passing through that country's immigration and customs, then checking in for the second flight as a new passenger. You are then entering the layover country, and its entry rules apply to your passport. Check the State Department's page for that country before you book this way.

Carry-on only

Traveling with carry-on bags only removes the baggage question from a split itinerary. It does not remove the missed-flight question. Check the cabin allowance of the second airline as well as the first, since the two may differ.

How much time to leave

The time between two separately booked flights is yours to choose, and no airline has checked it for you. The Department of Transportation's advice is to check the amount of time between flights on any connection. On separate tickets, our advice is to leave far more than you would accept on a through ticket.

An overnight at the hub

The safest way to use separate tickets is to stop at the hub overnight. A night between the two flights absorbs most delays, lets you collect and re-check your bag without hurry, and breaks the journey for sleep. It costs a hotel night and requires that you can enter the layover country.

The return journey

The same exposure exists on the way home, in reverse: the short flight out of Bali has to arrive in time for the long flight to the United States. A missed long-haul flight is the more expensive of the two to replace. If you split the ticket, give the return connection at least as much room as the outbound one.

Paperwork that depends on the ticket

Indonesia's entry rules are indifferent to how the ticket was bought, with one exception worth knowing. A visitor is expected to hold an outbound ticket from Indonesia. If your flight out of Bali is on a separate booking from your flight in, carry the confirmation of that second booking where you can show it.

The arrival card

The arrival card that every traveler submits in the 72 hours before landing asks for the flight on which you arrive in Indonesia. On a split itinerary, that is the second flight, the one from the hub to Bali. Enter that flight's details, not the long-haul flight's.

Our recommendation

For a first trip to Bali, our recommendation is a through ticket, bought as one booking from your home airport to Bali. It keeps both flights on one record and makes the baggage question simple. Separate tickets are a reasonable choice for an experienced traveler who has priced the saving against an overnight at the hub.

If you do split the ticket

These are our own suggestions.

  • Stop overnight at the hub, or leave many hours between the flights.
  • Ask the second airline about its policy for late passengers before you buy.
  • Confirm you can enter the layover country.
  • Check what your travel insurance says about missed connections on separate bookings.

Where this fits

The ticket type decides how much slack the first and last days of the trip need, and those are the days most plans leave thin. How the ticket, the layover and the arrival day fit together is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

What is a through ticket?

A single booking that covers the whole journey, including the connection. Both flights sit on one reservation.

Is it cheaper to book separate tickets to Bali?

Sometimes. A sale fare to an Asian hub plus a regional flight can cost less than a through fare, but the price difference changes daily.

Will my bags be checked through on separate tickets?

Do not assume so. The Department of Transportation advises asking whether bags will be transferred on any connection involving two airlines.

What happens if I miss a separately booked flight?

We have no official source on any airline's policy, so ask the airline before you buy. A claim for delay expenses under the Montreal Convention is made afterward.

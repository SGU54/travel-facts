Nyepi is Bali's day of silence, the Balinese New Year. For 24 hours regular flights stop, mobile data is switched off, and residents and visitors are asked to stay indoors. For a traveler it is either a quiet day spent at the hotel or a day that collides with a flight. Which one depends on knowing the date.

What Nyepi is and when it falls

Nyepi is the Balinese Hindu New Year under the Saka calendar, observed as a full day of silence across Bali, and it is an Indonesian national holiday. The date moves every year. The next Nyepi falls on Monday, March 8, 2027, according to Indonesia's official holiday decree.

Why the date moves

Nyepi follows a lunar-based calendar, so it lands on a different day of the Western calendar each year, usually in March. In 2026 it fell on March 19. Do not rely on a date from an old blog post: check the current year's Indonesian holiday list before you book flights anywhere near March.

What shuts down on Nyepi

Nyepi brings a full day of official closures in Bali, and they include things a traveler depends on. Mobile data and television and radio broadcasts are switched off for 24 hours, from 6 a.m. to 6 a.m., under a circular from Indonesia's communications ministry, with hospitals and other vital services exempt.

The airport

Bali's airport stops regular commercial flights for the same 24 hours. For Nyepi 2026, the airport's general manager announced that all regular commercial flights would halt from 6 a.m. on the day until 6 a.m. the next morning, according to Indonesian press reports of the statement. Hours for 2027 have not been announced, so confirm your own flight with your airline.

Staying indoors

The annual joint appeal issued by Bali's religious and provincial authorities asks residents and tourists alike not to travel or leave their accommodation during Nyepi, and prohibits fireworks, loudspeakers and other noise, as reported by Indonesian media. Visitors observe the day inside the place they are staying.

Phones and internet

The ministry's circular covers mobile data and broadcast television and radio; operators text customers in the days beforehand to warn them. It does not cover fixed-line connections. Our advice: download maps, tickets, books and anything else you need the day before, and tell people at home that you will be hard to reach.

What the 6 a.m. to 6 a.m. window does to your flights

The Nyepi closure runs from early morning to early morning, so it reaches into the nights on both sides. A flight due to land late the evening before, or to leave early the morning after, sits close to the edge of the window. An arrival or departure on Nyepi itself is not possible on a regular commercial flight.

If you are flying from the United States

American itineraries are the ones most exposed to this. The journey crosses the date line and takes more than a day, so the calendar date you leave home is not the date you land. Work out your arrival date in Bali time, then check it against Nyepi. A connection that slips by a few hours can turn a safe arrival into one on the wrong side of the closure.

Do not plan to arrive the night before

Our advice is to land at least a full day before Nyepi, not the evening before. That leaves room for a delayed connection, and it gives you time to reach your hotel and buy what you need before the island closes.

Where to be when the island goes quiet

Nyepi is spent wherever you are staying, so the choice of accommodation for that night matters more than usual. You will be on the property for a full day and night with no option of going out to eat, to a beach or to another town. Choose a place where you would be content to do exactly that.

What to arrange

Ask your accommodation before you book how it handles Nyepi: what meals are served, which facilities stay open, and what is expected of guests after dark. Buy snacks and water the day before, since you will not be able to go out for them.

Is it worth being there?

Some travelers plan a trip to include Nyepi on purpose: a day with no traffic and no aircraft is rare anywhere. Others would rather not give up a day of a short trip. Both are reasonable, as long as it is a choice.

Nyepi 2027 sits next to another holiday

Nyepi 2027 falls two days before Eid al-Fitr, a major national holiday in Indonesia. The official holiday decree puts Nyepi on Monday, March 8, with Eid al-Fitr on March 10 and 11 and collective leave days on March 9, 12 and 15, which makes most of that week a holiday across the country.

What that means for booking

Our advice, if your dates fall in that week: book flights and rooms early, and treat any side trip to another island with more care than usual, since a national holiday week sits on both sides of the closure.

What this decision actually sits inside

Notice what happened across all of this. Nyepi looked like a single date to avoid or enjoy. In practice it fixed several other things at once: which day you can land, where you sleep that night, whether a side trip fits, and how much slack your return flight needs. A trip is a chain of dates, and one immovable day changes the links on either side of it.

Nyepi is the strictest of Bali's calendar days, and there are others that close roads or fill hotels. How to lay a trip over the Balinese calendar is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

Is the Bali airport closed on Nyepi?

Can tourists go outside on Nyepi?

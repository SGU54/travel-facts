The Bukit is the limestone peninsula at Bali's southern tip, and Uluwatu is its best-known corner, named for the temple that stands on a cliff above the sea. The area is known for surf breaks, white sand beaches below cliffs, and sunsets. It is about 13 miles from the airport, and it suits surfers and travelers who want scenery and space more than a walkable town.

What the Bukit is

The Bukit peninsula hangs below the rest of Bali, joined to it by a narrow neck of land where the airport sits. Travel guides describe it as the rugged southernmost part of the island. Uluwatu is on its southwestern corner, Jimbaran at its neck on the west side, and Nusa Dua on its eastern shore.

How it differs from the rest of the south

The Bukit is limestone, and its coast is cliffs with beaches at the bottom, reached by steps. That geography gives the views the area is known for, and it means that a beach day here usually involves a climb.

The regency

The peninsula is part of Badung regency, like Seminyak and Canggu. The local tax on restaurant food and drink and on hotel stays is 10%, with a service charge added separately where a business chooses to add one.

Uluwatu Temple

Uluwatu Temple is the reason the area's name is known. Travel guides describe it as perched on a cliff about 75 meters above the waves. It is a working temple, and Bali's rules for visitors apply: modest dress is required in sacred places, and tourists may not enter the inner courtyards unless they are there to pray in Balinese traditional dress.

Visiting

The temple grounds along the cliff edge are the part visitors walk through. Our advice is to wear clothing that covers shoulders and knees and to accept the sarong and sash offered at the entrance. Keep sunglasses, phones and food secure if there are monkeys about.

Sunset and the dance

The temple is on the west-facing coast, so sunset is the popular hour. A kecak dance is staged for visitors at the temple in the evening; we have not sourced its times or ticket prices, so check locally on the day.

Beaches and surf

The Bukit's beaches are white sand coves at the foot of cliffs, which sets them apart from the long dark-sand beaches further north. Travel guides name Padang Padang among them. Surf guides list the breaks around Uluwatu and Padang Padang among the west-facing spots that are at their best in the dry season.

When the surf works

Surf guides describe the dry season, from about April to October, as the time when southeast trade winds blow offshore on this coast, with the largest swells arriving from June to August. We rate no break for difficulty. Many of the Bukit's waves break over reef, so our advice to a beginner is to take lessons elsewhere first.

Swimming

Reef, tide and swell decide whether a Bukit beach is swimmable on a given day. We have no official source on conditions at each beach, so we describe none as safe. Ask at your accommodation about the tide before you walk down, since some coves have little sand at high water.

The steps

Most Bukit beaches are reached by long flights of steps down the cliff. That is easy going down and hot work coming back. Travelers with small children, heavy bags or limited mobility should ask how many steps a beach involves before choosing it.

Jimbaran and Nusa Dua

The Bukit has two gentler shores. Jimbaran, on the west side near the airport, is described by travel guides as having a sheltered beach and seafood restaurants on the sand. Nusa Dua, on the east side, is an enclave of high-end resorts with a long beach. Uluwatu is about 8 miles from Jimbaran and about 12 from Nusa Dua.

Getting there and around

Uluwatu is about 13 miles from the airport by road, about 17 from Seminyak and about 23 from Canggu. Ubud is about 34 miles away. The peninsula has few through roads, and the drive in and out passes the airport area. We give no drive times; check a live map for your hour.

Getting around the peninsula

The Bukit is spread out, and little of it is walkable. Beaches, restaurants and viewpoints are a drive apart. A car and driver for the day, or a ride app where one is available, is the practical way to move. A scooter requires a license matching the type of vehicle driven.

Ride apps here

We have no official source on ride-app coverage in the Bukit, and it is less built-up than Seminyak or Kuta. Our advice is to arrange your ride back before you set out for a remote beach or the temple at sunset, when many people are leaving at once.

Who the Bukit suits

The Bukit suits surfers, couples and anyone who wants cliff views, quiet and a villa with space around it. It is a weaker fit for travelers who want to walk to a choice of restaurants, for families needing calm shallow water, and for anyone planning many day trips to central Bali.

How long to stay

Our advice is two to four nights for most trips: enough for the temple, a few beaches and some unhurried time. As a day trip from Seminyak or Canggu, the Bukit is possible and involves a long return drive in the evening.

Medical care

The State Department says an injured traveler in Indonesia will likely need to go to a major city for appropriate medical care, and that ambulances can take 45 minutes or longer. On a reef-break coast reached by cliff steps, that is worth knowing before you paddle out.

FAQ

Where is Uluwatu?

On the southwestern corner of the Bukit peninsula at Bali's southern tip, about 13 miles by road from the airport.

Is Uluwatu good for beginner surfers?

We rate no individual break. Many Bukit waves break over reef, so our advice to a beginner is to take lessons at a sandy beach first.

Can you swim at Uluwatu's beaches?

It depends on tide, reef and swell on the day. Ask locally before walking down, since conditions vary by cove.

How far is Uluwatu from Seminyak?

About 17 miles by road. Check a live map for the time, which varies with traffic.

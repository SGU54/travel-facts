Sanur and Nusa Dua are the calm side of south Bali: two resort areas on the east-facing coast, away from the surf and the nightlife of Kuta and Seminyak. Sanur is a long-established beach town; Nusa Dua is an enclave of large resorts. Both suit families and travelers who want quiet, and Sanur is the main port for boats to the Nusa islands.

Where they are

Sanur and Nusa Dua both face east, on the opposite shore from Bali's sunset beaches. Sanur is about 11 miles from the airport by road, on the edge of the capital, Denpasar. Nusa Dua is about 8 miles from the airport, on the eastern side of the Bukit peninsula. The two are about 13 miles apart.

The east-facing coast

Facing east changes the day. The sun rises over the sea here and sets behind the land, so this coast is for sunrise walks, not sunset drinks on the beach. Surf guides describe the east-facing coast as the side with cleaner conditions in the wet season, from about November to March.

Two regencies

Sanur belongs to the city of Denpasar and Nusa Dua to Badung regency. In Badung the local tax on restaurant food and drink and on hotel stays is 10%. We have not sourced Denpasar's rate, so check the foot of the menu in Sanur for what is added.

Sanur

Sanur is an established resort town on the edge of Denpasar. Travel guides describe it as a mature tourist town that appeals mostly to families and older visitors, with well-priced accommodation and easy beach access. Our view is that it is the most relaxed of the southern bases.

What Sanur is like

Our description of Sanur, drawn from how guides present it, is of a compact town that is easy on foot. The pace is slower than on the west coast, and the evenings are quiet. Travelers looking for beach clubs and late nights will find little of either. Travelers who want to read a book under a tree by the sea will find exactly that.

The harbor

Sanur's harbor is the main departure point for boats to Nusa Lembongan and Nusa Penida. Travel guides give the crossing as roughly 30 to 45 minutes; check with the operator, since we have no official schedule. Staying in Sanur the night before a boat removes a cross-town drive from the morning.

Nusa Dua

Nusa Dua is a purpose-built resort area. Travel guides describe it as an enclave of high-end resorts with a long golden sand beach and very little traffic. The hotels are large and self-contained, and it sits about 8 miles from the airport on the eastern side of the Bukit peninsula.

What Nusa Dua is like

Nusa Dua feels different from the rest of Bali, by design. It is orderly, quiet and separate from village life. Our view is that it suits travelers who want a resort vacation with reliable standards, and that it will disappoint travelers who want to step out of the hotel into a Balinese street.

Eating out

Because the area is built around resorts, most meals are eaten inside one. Resort restaurant prices carry the 10% local tax and often a service charge. Travelers on a budget should compare the final price including both, or plan some meals outside the enclave.

Sanur or Nusa Dua

The choice between Sanur and Nusa Dua comes down to a town or a resort. Sanur is a real place with a walkable center and a range of small hotels. Nusa Dua is a collection of large resorts with their own grounds. Sanur is the better position for Ubud; Nusa Dua is the better position for Uluwatu.

Distances from each

From Sanur it is about 14 miles to Ubud, about 12 to Seminyak and about 21 to Uluwatu. From Nusa Dua it is about 12 miles to Uluwatu and about 26 to Ubud. We give no drive times. Sanur's position on the east side of the island makes it a practical base for day trips into the hills and along the east coast.

For families

Both areas are popular with families, for the calm and the space. We have no official source on sea conditions or lifeguards at either beach, so we call neither one safe for swimming. Ask your hotel about the tide and about where it is safe to swim.

Getting there and around

Both areas are a short distance from the airport, which makes either a comfortable first or last stop. A pre-arranged car is the easy way to arrive. The State Department's advice is to avoid unlicensed taxis that approach passengers at airports and to use reputable taxi companies or ride-hailing apps.

Around Sanur

Within Sanur, our advice is to walk. For trips beyond the town, ride apps and taxis are available; the State Department describes Blue Bird taxis as generally the most reliable in Indonesia.

Around Nusa Dua

Inside the resort area, the grounds and the beach path are walkable. Anything outside it needs a car. Our advice is to book a driver for the day when you want to see Uluwatu or the temples, and to agree the price and hours in writing first.

Who this coast suits

The east-facing coast suits families with young children, older travelers, and anyone who wants a quiet beach base without surf crowds or nightlife. It also suits a traveler heading to the Nusa islands. It is a weaker fit for surfers in the dry season and for those who want the restaurant and bar scene of Seminyak.

How long to stay

Our advice is that Sanur works well for three or four nights as a base for day trips, and for one night before a boat. Nusa Dua works as a resort stay of any length, on the understanding that you will see little of Bali from inside it.

FAQ

Is Sanur or Nusa Dua better?

Sanur is a walkable beach town with small hotels; Nusa Dua is an enclave of large resorts. Sanur is better placed for Ubud and the island boats, Nusa Dua for Uluwatu.

Is Sanur good for families?

Travel guides describe Sanur as appealing mostly to families and older visitors. It is flat, compact and quiet.

Where do boats to Nusa Penida leave from?

Mainly from Sanur's harbor. Travel guides give the crossing as roughly 30 to 45 minutes; check with the operator.

Can you see the sunset in Sanur?

Sanur faces east, so the sun rises over the sea and sets behind the land.

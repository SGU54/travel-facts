Nusa Lembongan is a small island off Bali's southeast coast, reached by boat from Sanur in roughly 30 to 45 minutes, and Nusa Ceningan is the tiny island joined to it by a yellow suspension bridge. Together they offer surf, snorkeling, cliffs and a slower pace than the mainland, with no cars to speak of. They suit a stay of two or three nights in the middle of a trip.

What the two islands are

Nusa Lembongan is one of three islands off Bali's southeast coast, alongside tiny Nusa Ceningan and the much larger Nusa Penida. Lembongan measures about 8 square kilometers, roughly 3 square miles. Travel guides describe it as known for surfing, diving and snorkeling, and as a good place to relax.

How it differs from Bali

The island is small enough to cross in minutes, and travel guides note that personal cars are not allowed without special village permission. People get around on foot, by bicycle, by motorbike or in small pickup trucks. The effect is a place with far less traffic than any resort area on the mainland.

Ceningan

Nusa Ceningan sits between Lembongan and Penida. A yellow suspension bridge links it to Lembongan and carries walkers, bicycles and motorbikes. It is the smallest and quietest of the three islands, usually visited as a short outing from Lembongan.

The areas of Lembongan

Lembongan has a handful of distinct areas, and where you stay shapes the visit. Jungutbatu, in the northwest, is the main village, with a beach where many boats arrive. Mushroom Bay, in the southwest, is a sheltered cove with white sand. The south coast has limestone cliffs, with Dream Beach and Sunset Beach.

Jungutbatu

Jungutbatu is the island's hub, with the largest choice of places to stay and eat. The surf breaks lie offshore here. It is the practical base for a first visit, since boats land nearby and everything is within a short ride.

Mushroom Bay

Mushroom Bay is a sheltered cove on the southwest side, described by travel guides as having white sand. It is quieter than Jungutbatu. Some boats from Bali arrive here by floating pontoon, so check where your boat lands before choosing where to sleep.

The mangroves

The northern tip of the island is covered by a mangrove forest, described by travel guides as a mature ecosystem. It is a calm contrast to the cliffs of the south coast and a common short outing.

What to do

The islands are about the water. Travel guides list surfing at the offshore breaks, snorkeling and scuba diving among coral reefs, and trips to see manta rays. On land there are the cliff viewpoints of the south coast and the ride across the bridge to Ceningan. Two full days cover it without hurry.

Snorkeling and manta rays

Snorkeling trips leave from Lembongan by small boat, and manta rays are seen in the waters around neighboring Nusa Penida. We have no official source on how often mantas are seen, so we promise nothing. The State Department notes that diving in Indonesia can be dangerous because of minimal safety regulations; choose an operator carefully and ask about life jackets.

Surfing

The surf breaks off Lembongan are reef breaks reached by paddling or by boat. We rate none for difficulty. Our advice to anyone who is not an experienced reef surfer is to watch from the shore or take local advice before paddling out.

Places to be careful

Two spots on Lembongan carry specific warnings in travel guides. At Dream Beach, guides warn visitors to be very careful about entering the water because of fierce rip currents. At Devil's Tear, a rocky outcrop where waves explode against the cliff, guides report that tourists have fallen to their deaths.

Devil's Tear

Barriers have been put up at Devil's Tear, and they are there for a reason. Stay behind them, stay off wet rock, and do not turn your back on the sea for a photograph. A wave larger than the ones before it gives no warning.

Motorbikes

Renting a motorbike is the usual way to see the island, and the same law applies as on the mainland. Indonesia's traffic law requires a license matching the type of vehicle driven, and a helmet. Our advice for anyone without a motorcycle license is to walk, cycle or hire a ride.

Getting there

Boats to Nusa Lembongan leave mainly from Sanur, with other services from Serangan and Padang Bai. Travel guides give the crossing from the south as roughly 35 to 45 minutes. We have no official schedule and name no operator; check times directly, and book a boat that leaves in daylight.

The wet landing

Travel guides note that most boats to Lembongan involve a wet landing: passengers wade through water that can be knee-deep or higher to board and to get off. Wear shorts and sandals, keep your phone and passport in a dry bag, and travel with luggage you can lift over your head.

The sea decides

The crossing is open water in a small fast boat, and it depends on sea conditions. We have no official source on cancellations and state no rate. Our advice is to keep at least one full day between your boat back to Bali and your flight home.

Practical notes

Lembongan is a small island with small-island services. Travel guides report several ATMs. We have no official source on medical facilities there; the State Department says an injured traveler in Indonesia will likely need to go to a major city, which from here begins with a boat. Bring the medicines you rely on.

How long to stay

Our advice is two or three nights. One night is spent mostly on boats and transfers. Three nights allows a snorkeling trip, a day on the south coast and Ceningan, and a slow morning. Lembongan also works as a base for a day on Nusa Penida, which is about ten minutes away by local boat.

Who it suits

The islands suit couples, surfers, snorkelers and anyone who wants a beach stay with little traffic. They are less suited to travelers with limited mobility, because of the wet landing and the rough paths, and to anyone uneasy in small boats.

FAQ

How do you get to Nusa Lembongan?

By boat, mainly from Sanur. Travel guides give the crossing as roughly 35 to 45 minutes; check times with the operator.

Are there cars on Nusa Lembongan?

Travel guides note that personal cars are not allowed without special village permission. People use motorbikes, bicycles and small pickup trucks.

How many days do you need on Nusa Lembongan?

Our advice is two or three nights.

Is Devil's Tear dangerous?

Travel guides report that tourists have died after falling there. Stay behind the barriers and off wet rock.

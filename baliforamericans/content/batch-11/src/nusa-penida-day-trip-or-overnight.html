Nusa Penida is better with a night than as a day trip, if your schedule allows it. The island is large, its roads are rough and its famous viewpoints are far apart, so a day trip from Bali covers one side of it in a hurry. An overnight stay gives you the cliffs early and late, without the day-trip crowd. A day trip still makes sense when time is short.

What Nusa Penida is

Nusa Penida is the largest of the three islands off Bali's southeast coast, at about 247 square kilometers, roughly 95 square miles. That is some thirty times the size of neighboring Nusa Lembongan. It is known for dramatic coastal cliffs and beaches, including Kelingking, Angel's Billabong, Broken Beach, Crystal Bay, Atuh Beach and Diamond Beach.

Why its size matters

Travel guides describe the roads on Nusa Penida as rough, and hilly away from the north coast. The best-known sights are split between the west side and the east side of the island. Reaching both in one day means spending most of it in a car on poor roads.

Getting there

Boats leave mainly from Sanur, with a slower ferry from Padang Bai. Travel guides give the speedboat crossing from Sanur as roughly 30 minutes, arriving at harbors on the north coast such as Toyapakeh, Buyuk and Sampalan. We have no official schedule; check times with the operator.

The case for a day trip

A day trip to Nusa Penida is the choice most visitors make, and it works for seeing one side of the island. Travel guides note that most visitors arrive on day trips from Bali. You leave Sanur in the morning, tour by car, and return in the afternoon, with no need to move your luggage.

What a day covers

A day realistically covers one side: the west, with Kelingking, Angel's Billabong, Broken Beach and Crystal Bay, or the east, with Atuh and Diamond Beach. We give no drive times, since we have no source for them. Our advice is to pick a side and not to let a tour promise both.

The drawbacks

Day trips share the same hours on the island, so our expectation is that the viewpoints are busiest in the middle of the day. The day also depends on two boat crossings fitting around it, and a late morning boat leaves less time for sightseeing.

The case for staying overnight

Staying overnight on Nusa Penida changes what the visit is. Accommodation exists along the north coast between Toyapakeh and Sampalan. With a night or two, you can reach a viewpoint early in the morning or late in the afternoon, and you can see both sides of the island on separate days.

What two days allow

Two days allow one side each, with time to walk down to a beach instead of only looking at it from above. They also leave room for a snorkeling trip; Manta Point, off the island's coast, is a known site for manta rays. We promise no sightings.

What it costs you

An overnight means carrying your luggage across on the boat and accepting simpler services than on Bali. Travel guides describe the roads as rough. The State Department says an injured traveler in Indonesia will likely need to reach a major city, which from Penida begins with a boat.

Safety on the island

Nusa Penida's scenery comes with real hazards, and travel guides say so. They describe the path down to Kelingking Beach as a clifftop descent of about 400 meters, and Peguyangan Spring as reached by about 700 stairs on a cliff edge. They also warn of heat, and against swimming at Broken Beach at high tide because of the waves.

The beaches below the cliffs

The beaches under the viewpoints are open to ocean swell. We have no official source on currents at any of them and call none safe for swimming. Our advice is to admire most of them from the sand, and to ask local people before entering the water anywhere.

Motorbikes

Travel guides describe Penida's roads as rough and hilly. Indonesia's traffic law requires a license matching the type of vehicle driven. Our advice is to hire a car with a local driver.

Insurance

The State Department strongly recommends insurance that covers medical evacuation in Indonesia. Check before the trip how your policy treats motorbike riding and boat travel, and keep the insurer's assistance number in your phone.

How to choose

The choice between a day trip and an overnight on Nusa Penida depends on how many days you have and what you want from the island. Choose a day trip if your whole stay is a week and you want a look at the famous cliffs. Choose an overnight if you have ten days or more, or care about photographs without crowds.

A middle way

A third option is to stay on Nusa Lembongan and visit Penida from there. Travel guides give the crossing between the two islands as about ten minutes by local boat. That gives you Lembongan's easier accommodation with Penida as a short hop.

Book the boat with daylight to spare

Whichever you choose, our advice is to take a morning boat out and a return that lands in Bali well before dark. Travel guides advise against boarding any boat you have doubts about, whether because of the vessel, overloading or the weather.

What this decision actually sits inside

Notice what happened across all of this. Day trip or overnight turned out to be a question about the rest of the itinerary. A day trip starts and ends in Sanur, so it favors a base on that side of Bali. An overnight uses two travel days. And every version has a boat in it, which has to sit somewhere in the trip that a delay would not hurt.

FAQ

Is one day enough for Nusa Penida?

One day covers one side of the island, west or east. Seeing both without rushing takes two days.

How long is the boat from Sanur to Nusa Penida?

Travel guides give the speedboat crossing as roughly 30 minutes. Check times with the operator.

Can you swim at Kelingking Beach?

We have no official source on conditions and call no Penida beach safe for swimming. Travel guides describe the path down as a clifftop descent of about 400 meters.

Is it worth staying overnight on Nusa Penida?

Yes, if you have ten days or more. It lets you see the viewpoints early and late and cover both sides of the island.

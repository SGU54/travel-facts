Adding the Gili Islands or Lombok to a Bali trip is worth it on a stay of two weeks or more, and hard to justify on a week. The Gilis are three small car-free islands off Lombok, reached from Bali by fast boat or by a short flight to Lombok and a transfer. Each way uses most of a day in each direction, and the boat depends on the sea.

What the Gili Islands are

The Gili Islands are three small islands off the northwest coast of Lombok, the island east of Bali. Gili Trawangan is the largest and is known as the party island. Gili Air is the closest to Lombok and has an established local community. Gili Meno is the quietest. None of them has cars or motorbikes.

Why people add them

The Gilis offer something Bali's main island does not: small sandy islands with no traffic, where the day is spent walking, cycling, swimming and snorkeling. For travelers who find south Bali too busy, that contrast is the appeal.

Lombok

Lombok is a large island in its own right, with its own airport. We have sourced little about Lombok beyond its role as the route to the Gilis, so this page treats it as a gateway and does not describe its sights. It is a separate province from Bali, so Bali's tourist levy and provincial rules do not extend to it.

Getting there by fast boat

Fast boats run from Bali to the Gili Islands. Travel guides name Padang Bai, in East Bali, as the main port, with a crossing of roughly an hour and a half; boats from the south of Bali take longer, and Amed, on the northeast coast, has the shortest sea route. We have no official schedule and name no operator.

Getting to the port

The port is part of the journey. Padang Bai is about 35 miles from the airport by road and about 23 from Ubud. Add that drive to the crossing, and a morning boat means an early start from most bases. Staying in East Bali the night before shortens the day.

What travel guides warn about

Travel guides are direct about the boats. They describe the fast boats as small high-speed craft, note that crew training and safety standards vary considerably, and advise travelers who have concerns about the vessel, the operator, overloading or the weather not to board. They describe sea conditions as roughest in July and August.

Getting there by air

The alternative to the boat is a flight from Bali to Lombok's international airport, followed by a drive to the harbor area at Bangsal and a short boat to the islands. Travel guides put the drive at well over an hour and a half. The flight is short; the whole journey is not.

When flying makes sense

Flying suits travelers who are uneasy on small boats, anyone prone to seasickness, and trips in the months when the sea is roughest. It also suits a trip that includes time on Lombok itself. It still ends with a boat, though a much shorter one across sheltered water.

Domestic flights

A domestic flight is a separate booking from your international ticket, with its own baggage rules, and it does not connect to your flight home. Leave a wide margin if you fly back from Lombok to Bali on the same day as an international departure. Our advice is not to do that at all.

How much time it takes

A side trip to the Gilis costs two travel days, one each way, whatever route you use. On a seven-night trip that leaves five days for everything else, on two islands. On a fourteen-night trip it leaves twelve. Our advice is at least three nights on the islands, so the days there outnumber the days spent getting there.

Which Gili

The three islands differ mainly in how lively they are. Trawangan has the most going on, Meno the least, and Air sits between them with more village life. Small boats link the three, so the choice of where to sleep does not stop you seeing the others.

What to bring

Our advice is to carry cash, since small islands have few ways to get more, and any medicine you rely on. The State Department says an injured traveler in Indonesia will likely need to reach a major city for appropriate care. From a small island, that journey starts with a boat.

The alternative closer to Bali

Travelers who want small-island days without the long crossing have a nearer option. Nusa Lembongan lies off Bali's own coast, roughly 35 to 45 minutes from Sanur by boat according to travel guides. It has no cars to speak of, surf and snorkeling, and takes far less of the trip.

Gili or Nusa

The Nusa islands are closer and more rugged, with cliffs; the Gilis are further, flatter and sandier. Our view is that a first trip of ten days or fewer is better served by Lembongan, and that the Gilis earn their place on a longer trip or a return visit.

Where to place it in the trip

A Gili side trip belongs in the middle of an itinerary, never at the end. The boat depends on sea conditions, and we have no official source on how often crossings are cancelled, so we state no rate. Our advice is to be back on Bali with at least one full day, and better two, before your flight home.

The return leg

The way back is where plans fail, since it is the leg with a deadline behind it. How an island leg, a boat and an international flight are spaced across a trip is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

How do you get from Bali to the Gili Islands?

By fast boat, mainly from Padang Bai, or by flying to Lombok and continuing by road and a short boat. Each route takes most of a day.

Are the Gili Islands worth it on a one-week Bali trip?

Hard to justify. The side trip uses two travel days, so it fits better on a stay of two weeks or more.

Are fast boats to the Gili Islands safe?

Travel guides note that safety standards vary and advise not boarding if you have concerns about the vessel, overloading or the weather.

Are there cars on the Gili Islands?

No. There are no cars or motorbikes on any of the three islands.

Some medication can come to Bali with you and some cannot, and which is which depends on the class of drug, not on whether you hold a valid US prescription. Indonesia bans one whole class of medicine outright, limits another, and allows ordinary prescriptions with paperwork. Check which class yours falls in before you pack anything.

The rule in one paragraph

Medication for personal use can enter Indonesia in a traveler's own baggage without advance approval from the health authorities, subject to conditions: a doctor's prescription, a declaration form, and limits that depend on the class of drug. Indonesia's food and drug agency allows a supply of up to 90 days' use for prescribed medicine, with a shorter limit for one class and an outright ban on another.

The agency responsible is BPOM, Indonesia's equivalent of the FDA. Its guidance is written by class of drug, not by brand name, and that is the detail that matters most for Americans, as you will see below.

What "personal use" means here

The allowance covers medicine you carry with you, in your own bags, for your own treatment. The rule described here is for medicine carried in a passenger's baggage, not for anything mailed or couriered. Whatever you need for the trip should travel with you from the first day.

What counts as acceptable paperwork

Prescription medicine brought into Indonesia from abroad must be supported by a doctor's prescription or a hospital recommendation, according to the food and drug agency; over-the-counter products have a small separate allowance, covered below. The agency also describes a special form with supporting documents for personal-use medicine, so the prescription is the document to have ready.

What to carry

The documented requirements are the prescription and the agency's form. Beyond that, our advice is practical, not official: keep each medicine in its original pharmacy packaging with your name on the label, carry a copy of the prescription for each item, and ask your doctor for a short letter listing each drug by its generic name, the dose and the condition treated. US brand names are often different abroad.

Keep everything in your carry-on, together, where you can hand it over in one motion. Loose pills in a weekly organizer are the worst case: nothing ties them to you or to a prescription.

Over-the-counter medicine

Ordinary non-prescription medicine has its own small allowance. BPOM permits over-the-counter products without a prescription for at most three days' need. For a longer trip, that means a large stock of non-prescription products is outside the allowance.

The three classes and their limits

Indonesia sorts medicine into classes with different limits. Ordinary prescription medicine is allowed for up to 90 days of use. Psychotropic medicine is allowed for up to 60 days of need. Narcotic-class medicine and precursors may not be brought in from abroad at all, with or without a prescription.

Why this is the part to check first

The ban on the narcotic class has no exception for a valid American prescription. A drug that your doctor prescribes and your pharmacy fills without comment can still be one Indonesia will not admit. The State Department says the same thing from the other side: some prescription medications, including those for ADHD, are illegal in Indonesia.

No official list exists to look your drug up

Here is the uncomfortable part. Indonesian customs does not publish a list of prohibited medicines, and the food and drug agency's guidance names classes, not products. We are not going to tell you that a particular US prescription is allowed or banned, because no official source we could open says so drug by drug.

What you can do is find out which class your own medication falls into. BPOM publishes a contact for individual questions about bringing medicine in for personal use, and your prescribing doctor can tell you whether a drug is a controlled substance and what the non-controlled alternatives are. Do this well before you travel.

What happens at customs

Medicines are one of the items that send an arriving passenger to the red channel at Indonesian customs, where baggage can be physically inspected. Declaring your medication is the expected behavior, not an admission of anything. Medicine that does not meet the rules can be seized and destroyed.

Declare it

The arrival card you complete before landing includes the customs declaration. Answer the medicine question honestly, then go where the officers direct you with your prescriptions in hand. Be ready to say what each medicine is and to show the prescription for it.

If your medicine is refused

The agency's wording is that non-compliant medicine can be seized and destroyed. That is why the realistic backup plan has to exist before you fly: know the generic name of what you take, ask your doctor what you would switch to if it were unavailable, and do not count on buying a replacement for a banned drug once you are there.

Why the stakes are higher than a lost bottle

Indonesia's drug laws are strict, and the State Department's warning covers prescriptions too: some medications that are legal with a US prescription are illegal in Indonesia. The State Department warns that convictions can bring heavy fines and long jail sentences. Marijuana, cannabis and CBD products are illegal in Indonesia, including products that are legal in your home state.

CBD and medical marijuana

This catches Americans more than any pill does. A CBD gummy for sleep or a cannabis vape cartridge is illegal there, and a prescription or medical card from a US state does not change what the State Department says about Indonesian law. Leave all of it at home, and check jacket pockets and toiletry bags before you pack.

FAQ

Can I bring prescription medication to Bali?

Yes, for personal use, in your own baggage, with a doctor's prescription, for up to 90 days of use. Narcotic-class medicines are the exception and may not be brought in at all.

Is Adderall legal in Bali?

The State Department says some prescription medications, including those for ADHD, are illegal in Indonesia. Speak with your doctor about alternatives before you travel.

Do I need a doctor's letter for medication in Bali?

Indonesia's food and drug agency requires a doctor's prescription or hospital recommendation for medicine brought from abroad. A letter listing generic names and doses makes the customs check faster.

Can I bring CBD to Bali?

No. Marijuana, cannabis and CBD products are illegal in Indonesia.

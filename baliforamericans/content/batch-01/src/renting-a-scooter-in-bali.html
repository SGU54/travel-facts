Renting a scooter in Bali is easy, and that ease hides the real question. Being handed the keys says nothing about whether you are legally allowed to ride, and the gap between "they rented it to me" and "I was licensed to ride it" decides what a police stop or a crash will cost you.

What an American legally needs to ride a scooter in Bali

An American riding a scooter in Bali needs an International Driving Permit carried together with a US license, and Indonesian law requires that the license match the type of vehicle. The State Department says a US driver's license alone is not recognized in Indonesia, and a rental agreement does not change that.

The permit

The International Driving Permit is a booklet that translates your license; it is issued in the United States before you leave, and AAA, which issues it, lists Indonesia among the countries that honor it. Arrange it before you leave home. The permit has no force on its own, so the plastic license from your state travels with it.

The part most visitors miss: the license class

Indonesia's traffic law requires every driver to hold a license matching the type of vehicle driven, and it treats motorcycles as their own license class, separate from cars. A scooter is a motorcycle under that law. As we read the statute, a US license that covers cars only does not match a scooter, and a permit translates your license; it does not add a class.

No official page we could find says a car-only license is enough for a scooter in Bali, and the statute points the other way. Our advice, which is an interpretation and not an official ruling: if your US license has no motorcycle endorsement, treat yourself as unlicensed on a scooter.

What the rental shop will and will not check

A rental agreement is not a license check. Do not read being handed a scooter as evidence that you may legally ride it. Your license status, your insurance and your riding experience are yours to sort out before you reach the counter.

What to do before you ride away

Photograph the scooter from every side, including existing scratches. Ask for the vehicle registration, since drivers must carry it. Test both brakes and the lights, and get a helmet that fits and fastens for you and any passenger.

Never leave your passport as a deposit

If a shop asks to hold your passport, decline and offer a cash deposit or a photocopy instead. Your passport is the one document you cannot replace quickly, and handing it over weakens your position in any dispute about damage.

Police stops and what the law says about fines

Police checks on foreign riders in Bali are part of a current enforcement campaign that the US Consular Agency describes as focused on traffic and vehicle violations, among other offenses. Indonesia's traffic law sets a maximum of 4 months' detention or a fine of IDR 1,000,000 for riding without the required license, with lower maximums for not carrying one and for riding without a helmet.

What officers look for

The rules themselves are clear: a helmet for rider and passenger, a license and permit, the registration, and sober riding. Bali's 2025 rules for foreign visitors repeat that list and add that riders must not carry more passengers than the vehicle allows.

If you are fined

The State Department's advice is to ask for an official receipt and follow the local procedure; it notes that travelers occasionally report requests for unofficial payments. The amounts above are legal maximums, not a roadside price list.

If you crash

A crash is the risk that matters most on a scooter in Bali, and the legal position of a foreign rider is weaker than most expect. The State Department's guidance is that drivers involved in a traffic accident should stay at the scene and notify the police.

Who gets blamed

The State Department notes that in collisions between cars and motorcycles, the driver of the larger vehicle is often presumed responsible. That presumption is no shield for a rider who was not licensed to be on the scooter in the first place.

The question to put to your insurer before you go

Ask your travel insurer, in writing, one specific question: "Am I covered for injuries while riding a rented scooter in Indonesia if my US license has no motorcycle endorsement?" Policies differ, and only your own insurer can tell you where you stand. Get the reply by email and keep it.

Whether you should rent one at all

A scooter makes sense in Bali for a rider who already holds a motorcycle license at home, has ridden in traffic, and is staying somewhere the distances are short. For a first-time rider, Bali's roads are a poor place to learn; ride-hailing apps and cars with drivers are the alternatives.

If you ride

Wear a helmet with the strap fastened and closed shoes. Do not ride after drinking; Bali's own rules for visitors prohibit it. Avoid riding at night on roads you do not know.

FAQ

Can I ride a scooter in Bali with a US driver's license?

Not on a US license alone. The State Department says foreign drivers must carry an International Driving Permit, and Indonesian law requires a license matching the vehicle type, with motorcycles as their own class.

Do I need a motorcycle license to rent a scooter in Bali?

Shops will rent without one, but Indonesia's traffic law treats motorcycles as a separate license class. Without a motorcycle endorsement, assume you are not licensed for a scooter.

Is a helmet required on a scooter in Bali?

Yes. Helmets are required by Indonesian law for riders and passengers.

Where do I get an International Driving Permit?

In the United States, before you travel. AAA issues it and lists Indonesia as a country that honors it.

Bali charges foreign tourists a one-time levy, separate from the visa, and you can pay it online before you leave home. The amount is small. What matters is knowing that it exists, paying it on the real website, and keeping the proof, because a tourist who has not paid can be refused service at the island's attractions.

What the Bali tourist levy is

The Bali tourist levy is a fee the provincial government charges international visitors to fund the protection of Balinese culture and the natural environment. The Bali tourist levy costs IDR 150,000 per person and is set in rupiah, so its dollar value shifts with the exchange rate on the day you pay.

The levy is a Bali rule, not a national one. It rests on provincial regulations and applies to international tourists who travel to Bali, apart from a short list of exemptions, whichever airport or port that tourist used to enter Indonesia. Flying into Jakarta first does not change it.

How it differs from the visa

The visa is issued by Indonesia's national immigration service and lets you into the country. The levy is collected by Bali's provincial government and has nothing to do with your right to enter. They are paid on different websites, to different authorities, and paying one does not cover the other. Many first-time visitors pay for the visa and assume they are done.

Who has to pay the Bali tourist levy

International tourists who travel to Bali for tourism pay the levy, with a short list of exemptions, and the official guidance says plainly that cruise passengers who come ashore are included. The levy is paid once per trip, not once per day or per hotel, so a three-week stay costs the same as a long weekend.

Who is exempt

The exemptions are narrow and none of them describes an ordinary vacationer. The official list covers holders of diplomatic and official visas, transport crew, holders of Indonesian stay permits, and holders of family unification, student and golden visas. If you are flying in on a tourist visa, you pay.

Leaving and coming back

The official wording is that the levy is paid one time while traveling in Bali, before the person leaves Indonesia. Read plainly, a side trip from Bali to Lombok or the Gili Islands and back sits inside the same trip, because you have not left the country. A separate vacation next year is a new trip and a new payment.

How to pay the Bali tourist levy

The Bali tourist levy is paid cashless through the province's Love Bali system, and the simplest time to do it is online before you fly. Travelers can also pay at counters at Bali's airport and seaport on arrival, or through hotels, travel agents and attractions that are registered in the system. Cash is not accepted anywhere.

Paying online before you go

On the Love Bali website you enter your passport details and arrival date, then pay. Accepted methods include Visa, Mastercard, American Express and JCB cards. For an American, a credit card is the practical choice; the site also lists bank transfer and Indonesia's QR payment standard, and the United States is not among the countries connected to that QR scheme.

Use a card that does not charge a foreign transaction fee if you have one. The charge is processed in rupiah, and a card with a three percent foreign fee adds a little to every such payment on the trip.

Your proof of payment

After paying, you receive a levy voucher with a QR code by email. Save it somewhere you can reach without signal: a screenshot in your photos works. The levy is charged per person, so check that you have a voucher for each traveler in your group before you close the browser.

Paying when you land

If you forget, there are payment counters at the airport. This works, but it adds one more line at the end of a very long travel day, right after immigration and baggage claim. Paying from home removes the step.

What happens if you do not pay

A foreign tourist who has not paid the Bali levy can be refused service at tourist attractions under the Bali governor's 2025 circular on visitor conduct. In practice that means the voucher is something you may be asked to show at a temple or a ticket booth, not only at the airport.

Checks happen away from the airport

Because the stated consequence is being refused service at tourist attractions, the realistic place to be asked is at the entrance to somewhere you have driven an hour to see. Having the QR code on your phone turns that into a ten-second check.

Nothing official says how often the check happens, and that is a poor reason to skip the payment. The levy is small, the rule is clear, and the same circular that sets the penalty also tells residents how to report tourists who break the rules.

Avoiding fake levy websites

The only official place to pay the Bali tourist levy online is the provincial government's Love Bali site, at lovebali.baliprov.go.id. Search results for the levy also show third-party sites that take the payment on your behalf and add their own fee. The official address ends in baliprov.go.id, the domain of the Bali provincial government.

How to tell the real one

Check the address bar before you type a passport number. The official site charges the official amount. If a site quotes a higher total than the figure above, you are either on a third party's page or paying a third party's fee.

The same caution applies to the visa and the arrival card, which have their own official sites and their own imitators. Three small government payments, each with a separate website, is the kind of chore that is easy to get slightly wrong, and it is set out in order in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

How much is the Bali tourist tax?

The Bali tourist levy is IDR 150,000 per person, paid once per trip. It is charged in rupiah, so the dollar amount depends on the exchange rate when you pay.

Do children pay the Bali tourist levy?

The official exemption list is organized by visa type, and we found no exemption for children on it.

Can I pay the Bali levy in cash at the airport?

No. Payment is cashless only, online in advance or by card at the airport and seaport counters.

Is the Bali levy the same as the visa?

No. The visa is a national immigration document. The levy is a separate provincial charge paid on a different website.

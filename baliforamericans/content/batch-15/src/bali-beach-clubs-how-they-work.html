A Bali beach club is a venue with a pool, loungers, a restaurant and a bar on or above the beach, where you pay through what you eat and drink. Many clubs set a minimum spend for a daybed or lounger instead of charging a fee for it. Policies differ from club to club and change with the season, so the way to avoid a surprise is to ask four questions before you book.

What a beach club is

A beach club in Bali is a day venue built around a pool and a view of the sea. Guests come for the afternoon, order food and drinks, swim, and stay for sunset. The clubs are concentrated in the busy south of the island: on the west-facing coast at Seminyak and Canggu, and on the cliffs of the Bukit peninsula.

What we can and cannot tell you

We have no official source on any club's prices, minimum spends or entry rules, and they change often, so we quote none and name no venue. What follows describes how the arrangement is commonly structured and what to check. The club's own website or a message to its reservations desk is the authority.

How the money works

Paying at a beach club usually takes one of three forms. Some clubs let you walk in and pay only for what you order. Some charge an entry fee, which may be credited against your bill. And many set a minimum spend for the better seats, meaning you commit to ordering at least a stated amount.

Minimum spend

A minimum spend is not a fee on top of your bill. It is a floor: you are given a daybed or a cabana on the understanding that your food and drinks will come to at least that figure. If you order less, you are charged the difference. Ask whether the figure is per person or per bed.

Tax and service on top

The figure that matters is the one after additions. In Badung regency, which covers Seminyak, Canggu and the Bukit, the local tax on restaurant food and drink is 10%, and a venue may add a service charge. Ask whether the minimum spend is counted before or after tax and service.

The entertainment tax question

Bars and nightclubs fall under a separate tax category. The 2023 tax regulations of Badung set that category at 40%, in force from January 2024. The rate was publicly contested and may have been amended, and we cannot tell you how any particular club is classified. Look at how the venue states its prices.

Seats, daybeds and cabanas

Where you sit decides what you pay. A club typically has several kinds of seating: restaurant tables, bar stools, loungers by the pool, daybeds for two or more, and private cabanas. The minimum spend rises with the comfort and the view. A seat at the bar or in the restaurant often carries no minimum at all.

What to ask about a daybed

These are our own suggestions for a message to the reservations desk.

  • What is the minimum spend, and is it per person or per bed?
  • Is it counted before or after tax and service?
  • How many people may use the bed?
  • From what time, and until when, is the bed ours?

Time limits

A daybed may be yours for the whole day or for a set session, with a separate booking for the sunset hours. Ask. The hours before sunset are the most sought after on a west-facing coast, and a booking that ends before then is worth less to most people.

Booking

Booking ahead matters most for sunset, for weekends and for the dry season from about May to October, when the south is busiest. Our advice is to reserve through the club's own website or official messaging account and to get the terms in writing. Walk-in guests can often still use the restaurant and bar.

Deposits and cancellation

Some clubs ask for a deposit or a card guarantee to hold a daybed. Ask what happens to it if you cancel or if it rains. The wet season, from about November to March, brings about 9 to 14 inches of rain a month in south Bali, and a pool day is weather-dependent.

Paying

Ask which cards are accepted and whether the bill is in rupiah. The State Department advises checking with your card issuer about international transaction fees. When a card terminal offers to charge you in dollars, our advice is to choose rupiah and let your own bank convert.

Rules and etiquette

Beach clubs set their own dress codes and house rules, such as whether outside food and drink is allowed and whether children are admitted at all hours. Ask before you go if you are bringing children. Outside the club, Bali's rules for visitors ask for appropriate dress on the street, so cover up for the ride home.

Drinks

The State Department warns of drink spiking in Bali and of alcohol mixed with methanol in Indonesia, and advises making sure drinks are prepared in your sight and never leaving one unattended. An established venue with sealed, branded bottles lowers the risk. Keep your drink with you.

Getting home

Many guests leave at the same time after sunset. Our advice is to arrange the ride home before you arrive, particularly on the Bukit, which is spread out. Riding a scooter after drinking is illegal as well as dangerous; Bali's rules for visitors prohibit driving under the influence of alcohol.

Is a beach club worth it?

A beach club suits travelers who want a pool, service and a sunset in one place, and who would spend a similar sum on lunch and drinks anyway. It suits a group splitting a daybed. Our view is that it is poor value for someone who wants only a swim.

Cheaper ways to get the same sunset

These are our own suggestions. Book a table in the club's restaurant instead of a daybed. Go for a late lunch and stay. Or watch the sunset from the beach in front, which belongs to everyone, and come in for one drink afterward.

One day is enough for most

Our view is that one beach club afternoon is plenty on a trip of a week or two. A second one costs as much and adds little, and the same hours could go to a temple at sunset or a walk on a quieter beach.

FAQ

What is a minimum spend at a Bali beach club?

A floor on your bill in exchange for a daybed or cabana. If you order less than the figure, you pay the difference. Ask whether it is per person or per bed.

Do Bali beach clubs charge an entry fee?

Policies differ by club and change often. We quote none; check the club's own website or reservations desk.

Is tax included in beach club prices?

Often not. In Badung regency the local tax on food and drink is 10%, and a service charge may be added.

Do I need to book a beach club in Bali?

For a daybed at sunset, on weekends or in the dry season, booking ahead is wise. Restaurant and bar seats are easier to get.

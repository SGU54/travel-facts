Choosing a yoga retreat in Ubud comes down to five things the listings rarely make clear: where the place actually is, what the price includes, who teaches and how much, what happens if you cancel, and whether the accommodation is licensed. We name and rank no retreats. This page gives you the questions to ask, and the facts about Ubud and Bali's rules that bear on the answers.

Why Ubud

Ubud is the center of yoga and wellness travel in Bali. It is an inland town in the central hills, at about 200 meters above sea level, cooler than the coast and regarded as the island's cultural center. It is about 22 miles from the airport to Ubud by road, and it has no beach.

What "Ubud" means in a listing

Ubud is made up of fourteen villages, and many places advertised as "Ubud" are in the surrounding countryside. The center has three main streets and is congested with traffic. A retreat in the rice fields is quiet and may be a ride from town; a retreat in the center is walkable and less peaceful.

Ask for the exact location

Our advice is to ask for the address and look at it on a map before booking. Check the distance to central Ubud and how you would get there. A shuttle that runs twice a day shapes your week differently from a ten-minute walk.

What the price includes

A retreat price can cover anything from the classes alone to accommodation, all meals, excursions and airport transfers. Listings vary in how clearly they say which. We quote no prices. Ask for a written list of what is included and what costs extra, and for the total you will pay.

Tax and service

In Gianyar regency, which includes Ubud, the local tax on hotel stays and on restaurant food and drink matches the 10% charged in the southern resorts, and a business may add a separate service charge. Ask whether the price you have been quoted includes both.

Paying

Ask which payment methods are accepted and in which currency you will be charged. Bali's rules for visitors say transactions should be in rupiah. A deposit paid by bank transfer is harder to recover than one paid by card, so prefer a card where you can.

The teaching

The teaching is what you are paying for, and it is where listings are vaguest. Ask who the teachers are, what style they teach, how many hours of practice there are each day, and how many students share a class. A retreat and a teacher training are different things with different intensity.

Level

Ask what level the program is aimed at. A beginner in a class built for experienced students, or the reverse, has a poor week. A good retreat will ask you about your experience and any injuries before you arrive, and that question is a good sign.

Free time

Ask for a sample daily schedule. Some retreats fill the day from dawn; others leave afternoons open. If you want to see the temples and rice terraces around Ubud, you need free half-days, or extra nights before or after.

Health and the body

A retreat is a week of physical practice in a tropical climate, often with a changed diet. Tell the organizers about medical conditions and injuries before you book. The State Department says routine medical care is available in Indonesia's major cities and that hospitals often require payment or a deposit upfront.

Food and water

Ask how drinking water is supplied and how food is prepared. The State Department says tap water in many areas of Indonesia is not safe to drink. Tell the kitchen about allergies in writing before arrival, and ask how they are handled.

Cleanses and treatments

Some retreats offer fasting, cleanses or other treatments. We give no medical advice on them. Talk to your own doctor before committing to anything that restricts food or involves supplements, particularly if you take regular medication, and bring that medication within Indonesia's import rules.

Insurance

The State Department highly recommends travel insurance for Indonesia. Check that your policy covers the activities on the program, and that it covers cancellation if you have to withdraw before the retreat begins.

Rules that apply

Two of Bali's rules bear directly on a retreat booking. The first is that visitors must stay in accommodation that holds a license. The second concerns the visa: the tourist visa does not allow selling goods or services, or being paid by anyone in Indonesia.

If you are attending

Attending a retreat as a paying guest is tourism. An American needs the tourist visa, which allows a stay of 30 days, extendable once for another 30 days. A program longer than 30 days needs an extension, which involves a visit to an immigration office.

If you plan to teach or assist

Teaching, assisting or working in exchange for a place is a different matter from attending. The tourist visa prohibits being paid by anyone in Indonesia, and the US Consular Agency in Bali reports stepped-up enforcement against illegal work and visa violations. Get proper advice before agreeing to any such arrangement.

Cancellation and changes

Cancellation terms matter more for a retreat than for a hotel, because the sums are larger and are paid earlier. Ask for the policy in writing: what you get back if you cancel, by what date, and what happens if the retreat itself is cancelled. A long journey from the United States has more ways to go wrong than a short one.

Dates to check

Check the retreat dates against Bali's calendar. Nyepi, the day of silence, falls on Monday, March 8, 2027, which brings island-wide restrictions; some travelers seek that out and others would not want to arrive or leave near it.

FAQ

How do I choose a yoga retreat in Ubud?

Ask for the exact location, a written list of what the price includes, the teachers and daily schedule, the cancellation policy, and whether the accommodation is licensed.

Do I need a special visa for a yoga retreat in Bali?

Attending as a paying guest is tourism and uses the tourist visa. That visa does not allow being paid by anyone in Indonesia.

Is Ubud a good place for yoga?

Ubud is Bali's inland cultural center, cooler than the coast. It has no beach, and the town center is congested.

Are yoga retreat prices in Bali all-inclusive?

It varies. Ask for a written list of what is included and whether tax and service are added.

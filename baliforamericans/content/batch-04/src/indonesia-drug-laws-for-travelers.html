Indonesia's drug laws are among the strictest an American traveler will meet, and they apply in Bali exactly as they do in the rest of the country. Marijuana, cannabis and CBD products are illegal, and the State Department adds that some prescription medications, including those for ADHD, are illegal in Indonesia. It warns that convictions can bring heavy fines, long jail sentences and, in extreme cases, the death penalty.

What is illegal in Indonesia

Indonesia bans the drugs an American would expect and several products that are legal at home. The State Department lists marijuana, cannabis and CBD products as illegal in Indonesia, with no exception for medical use or for a product bought legally in a US state. Indonesia's Narcotics Law places the cannabis plant, all of its parts and its products in Class I, the most strictly controlled group.

Cannabis in every form

The Narcotics Law's Class I list names the cannabis plant, every plant of the cannabis genus, and all parts of it, including seeds, resin and hashish. THC is listed separately in the same class. A vape cartridge, an edible or a pre-rolled joint bought legally in the United States falls inside that definition once it is in Indonesia.

CBD is not an exception

CBD oil, gummies and creams are sold openly in much of the United States, which is why they end up in luggage by accident. The State Department names CBD products among the things that are illegal in Indonesia. Our advice is to go through your toiletry bag and carry-on before you pack and leave every CBD product at home.

What the Narcotics Law provides

Indonesia's Narcotics Law sets prison terms that start high. For unlawful possession of a Class I narcotic the law provides 4 to 12 years in prison and a fine of between 800 million and 8 billion rupiah. Those are the ranges written in the statute; what a court does in a given case is for the court.

Bringing drugs into the country

Importing a Class I narcotic is a more serious offense than possessing one. The law provides 5 to 15 years in prison for importing, and where the amount is more than 1 kilogram of plant material, or more than 5 grams in other forms, the penalty can be death or life imprisonment. Carrying something through an airport is importing it.

Personal use

The law treats personal use separately, and it is still a crime. A person who uses a Class I narcotic can be sentenced to up to 4 years in prison. The same article allows a court to order medical and social rehabilitation for someone shown to be a victim of drug abuse, but that is a decision made inside a criminal case.

Prescription medication is a separate risk

Prescription medication is where a careful traveler can still go wrong in Indonesia. The State Department says some prescription medications, including those for ADHD, are illegal there, and that the Indonesian customs office does not share a list of prohibited drugs. A US prescription does not make a banned substance legal in another country.

What the medicine rules allow

Indonesia's food and drug agency sets limits by class of medicine. A traveler may bring 90 days' use for prescribed medicine, backed by a doctor's prescription, and a smaller supply of psychotropic medicines. Narcotic-class medicines may not be brought in at all. Medicine that does not comply can be seized and destroyed.

Check your own prescription

Because there is no published list, the work falls to you. Find out which class your medication belongs to, carry it in its original packaging with the prescription, and ask your doctor about an alternative if it is a stimulant or a strong painkiller. The full rules by class are in our guide to bringing medication into Bali.

How enforcement reaches tourists

Drug enforcement in Bali is active, and visitors are not outside it. The US Consular Agency in Bali reports a current enforcement campaign by Indonesian police, immigration, transport and tourism officials, with increased enforcement against illegal drug possession among other offenses. An American arrested in Indonesia is subject to Indonesian law and Indonesian courts.

What the consulate can and cannot do

The US Consular Agency in Bali can be reached at +62-361-233-605. A consular agency is not a court and has no power over an Indonesian criminal case. Our advice, if you are arrested, is to ask at once for the US Consular Agency to be notified.

Do not carry anything for anyone

Under the Narcotics Law, importing is the offense with the heaviest penalties, and the person carrying the bag is the person who imported it. Pack your own luggage, keep it with you, and do not carry a package, a gift or a bag for someone else through an airport.

Offers on the street

A visitor to Bali's nightlife areas may be offered drugs. The possession article of the Narcotics Law is written for anyone, visitors included, and the minimum sentence it provides for possessing a Class I narcotic is four years. Our advice is the obvious one: say no and keep walking.

Drinks are a different danger

The State Department also warns of drink spiking in Bali, Lombok and the Gili Islands. Its advice is to make sure drinks are prepared in your sight, to accept none from strangers, and to leave none unattended. A spiked drink is a safety problem first, and it is a reason to stay with people you trust on a night out.

Alcohol is legal

Alcohol is legal in Bali for adults, and none of the above applies to a beer at dinner. The legal line an American needs to remember is simple: alcohol is permitted; cannabis, CBD and every recreational drug are not; and prescription medicine needs checking before the trip.

FAQ

Is weed legal in Bali?

No. Marijuana and cannabis are illegal throughout Indonesia, including Bali, and the Narcotics Law puts cannabis in its most strictly controlled class.

Can I bring CBD oil to Bali?

No. The State Department lists CBD products among the things that are illegal in Indonesia.

Can I bring ADHD medication to Bali?

The State Department says some prescription medications, including those for ADHD, are illegal in Indonesia. Check your specific medication before you travel and ask your doctor about alternatives.

Does Indonesia have the death penalty for drugs?

Yes. The State Department says the death penalty may apply in extreme cases, and the Narcotics Law provides for it in serious import cases.

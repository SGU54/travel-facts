One week in Bali is enough for two areas done properly: Ubud for culture and rice terraces, and one beach base in the south. A realistic first-timer route is three nights in Ubud and four by the sea, or the reverse. Seven nights do not stretch to the north, the east and an island as well, and the best one-week trips are the ones that accept that.

What a week can hold

A week in Bali, coming from the United States, is shorter than it looks. The journey has at least one connection and crosses 12 or more time zones, so the first day or two are spent adjusting. The CDC advises allowing at least two days before anything important. Plan five good days and treat the rest as recovery and travel.

Two bases, not four

Bali's beach areas and its cultural center are about 20 miles apart by road, and the drive between them is slow. Our view is that two bases fit a week comfortably and three do not, since each move costs half a day. One coast base and Ubud cover what most first-time visitors come for.

A realistic one-week route

This route gives a first-time visitor the two sides of Bali with one hotel change. It is a suggestion to adapt, with no drive times and no prices, since both vary. It assumes seven nights on the island and a morning or midday arrival.

  • Day 1: arrive, transfer to Ubud, rest.
  • Day 2: central Ubud on foot; an early night.
  • Day 3: rice terraces and temples north of Ubud with a driver.
  • Day 4: transfer to the coast, with a stop on the way.
  • Days 5 and 6: beach days, with one outing.
  • Day 7: a free day near your hotel.
  • Day 8: depart.

Days 1 to 3: Ubud

Ubud is about 22 miles from the airport to Ubud by road. Spend the first full day on foot in the center: the market, the palace and the Sacred Monkey Forest. On the second, hire a driver for the Tegallalang rice terraces, about 6 miles north, and a temple such as Tirta Empul.

Day 4: the transfer

The move from Ubud to the coast takes part of a day. A driver hired for the transfer can stop on the way, which turns a moving day into a sightseeing one. Agree the stops and the price in writing beforehand, and arrive at the beach in time for the sunset.

Days 5 to 7: the coast

Which coast base depends on what you want. Seminyak suits dining and beach walks, Canggu surfing, the Bukit peninsula cliffs and quiet, and Sanur a calm stay with children. Give these days to the sea, with one outing: Uluwatu Temple from a southern base, or Tanah Lot from Canggu.

Ubud first or beach first?

The order of the two bases is a real choice. Starting in Ubud puts the sightseeing early, while jet lag has you awake at dawn, and ends the trip with rest by the sea, close to the airport. Starting at the beach gives an easy landing after the flight and ends the trip inland, about 22 miles from the terminal.

Our preference

Our view is that Ubud first works better for most first-time visitors. Early mornings suit temples and rice terraces, and the last night falls near the airport. A traveler who arrives late at night may prefer the reverse, to avoid a drive inland in the dark after a long journey.

What to leave out

A one-week trip is defined by what it omits. The north coast and Munduk are about 51 miles or more from the airport. Amed, in the east, is about 61. The Nusa islands need boat crossings that depend on the sea. Each of these deserves its own nights, and a week cannot spare them.

The Mount Batur question

The sunrise trek on Mount Batur is the one long outing that fits a week, because Kintamani is about 27 miles from Ubud. It starts in the middle of the night. Do it on your second or third Ubud morning, never on the first, and keep the rest of that day free.

The island day-trip question

A day trip to Nusa Penida from the south is possible in a week, at the cost of a full day and two boat crossings. Our advice is to choose between that and a rest day, and to place it mid-trip. A boat the day before a long-haul flight is a risk with nothing to gain.

Practical points for a week

A few rules bear on any one-week plan. An American needs a tourist visa, at IDR 500,000, and pays Bali's tourist levy of IDR 150,000 per person. The arrival card can only be filled in during the 72 hours before landing.

Check the calendar

Check your week against Nyepi, the day of silence, which in 2027 falls on Monday, March 8, 2027. Press reports of the 2026 arrangements describe regular flights halting for 24 hours. A day lost that way is a seventh of a one-week trip.

Getting around

Use a driver for the day trips and the transfer, and ride apps for short hops in the south. A scooter requires a license that covers motorcycles under Indonesian law, which most Americans do not hold.

What this decision actually sits inside

Notice what happened across all of this. The route was simple, and almost every line of it depended on something outside the route: the hour your flight lands, the month you travel, whether your coast base suits the season, and how far the last night is from the airport. A day-by-day plan is the visible part. The choices underneath it are what make it work or not.

FAQ

Is one week enough for Bali?

Yes, for two areas: Ubud and one beach base. It is not enough to add the north, the east and an island as well.

How should I split a week in Bali?

Our suggestion is three nights in Ubud and four on the coast, with one hotel change.

Should I go to Ubud first or the beach first?

Our view is Ubud first for most visitors, so the last night is near the airport. Late-night arrivals may prefer the beach first.

Can I visit Nusa Penida on a one-week trip?

As a day trip from the south, yes, at the cost of a full day. Place it mid-trip, not the day before your flight.

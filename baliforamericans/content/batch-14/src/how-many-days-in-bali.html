Coming from the United States, plan on at least ten days in Bali, and treat a week as the minimum worth the journey. The trip involves at least one connection each way and a time difference of 12 to 16 hours, so two days are lost to travel and two more to adjusting. Seven nights give about five good days; ten give eight; fourteen give a proper trip.

Why the answer is different for Americans

The number of days Bali needs depends on where you start. There is no nonstop flight from the United States, so every itinerary has a connection. Bali is 12 or 13 hours ahead of New York and 15 or 16 ahead of Los Angeles. A visitor from Australia or Singapore can make a long weekend of it; an American cannot.

The travel days

The journey out crosses the International Date Line, so the calendar jumps ahead on the way to Bali. Depending on the routing, a traveler leaving on a Friday evening can land on Sunday. The return gives a day back on the calendar. Either way, a trip described as "a week" on the vacation request is shorter on the ground.

The adjustment days

The CDC says jet lag can affect mood, concentration and physical performance, and advises arriving at least two days before any important event. With day and night nearly reversed, the first two days in Bali are best kept light. They count as days on the island and deliver less than the later ones.

What each length gives you

The useful way to compare trip lengths is by good days on the ground, after travel and adjustment are taken out. The figures below are our own estimate, not a measurement. They assume the first two days are slow and the last day is partly spent leaving.

  • 5 nights: about three good days. One base only.
  • 7 nights: about five good days. Two bases.
  • 10 nights: about eight good days. Three bases.
  • 14 nights: about twelve good days. Four bases.

Five nights

Five nights is too short for most Americans. The long-haul flight is the largest fixed cost of the trip, and it buys three good days. Our view is that five nights make sense only as part of a longer Asian trip, when you are already in the region and the time zone.

Seven nights

A week is the practical minimum. It covers Ubud and one beach base with one hotel change. It leaves no room for the north, the east or an island stay, and no slack if a day is lost to weather or illness. Travelers with only a week should plan a simple trip.

Ten days and two weeks

Ten nights is the length we recommend for a first trip from the United States. It gives room for a third stop, such as a few nights on the Nusa islands, and one spare day. Fourteen nights add a fourth base, which opens the central highlands and East Bali.

The case for ten

Ten nights spread the cost and effort of the flight over enough days to justify it. They allow three nights in each of three places, the minimum we suggest for a base. By our estimate, ten nights is also roughly what two weeks off work turns into once the travel days and a day to recover at home are counted.

The case for fourteen

Two weeks suit travelers who want to see beyond the south. Munduk is about 51 miles from the airport and Amed about 61; neither works as a day trip. With fourteen nights you can stay in such places instead of driving to them.

Longer stays

Stays of three or four weeks are common among remote workers and retirees, and Bali rewards them. The tourist visa allows a stay of 30 days, extendable once for another 30 days. An extension costs IDR 500,000 per application and requires a visit to an immigration office.

Work and the tourist visa

A longer stay raises a question a short one does not. The tourist visa does not allow selling goods or services, or being paid by anyone in Indonesia. Anyone planning to work while in Bali should get proper advice on the right visa before traveling.

How to decide

The right length for your trip follows from three things: how much time off you have, what you want to see, and how you handle long flights. Start from the time off, subtract a day at each end for travel, and subtract one more at home before work. What remains is your nights in Bali.

If you have one week off

One week off work gives about six nights in Bali from most of the United States. Our advice is to keep it to one or two bases and to accept a narrow trip, or to wait until you can take longer. A rushed week at the far end of a very long flight is hard to enjoy.

If you have two weeks off

Two weeks off gives ten to twelve nights, which is the comfortable range. Use the weekends at both ends. Leave a day at home before returning to work, since the CDC's two-day guideline for jet lag applies to your first meeting as much as to your first temple.

What the length does not settle

The number of days is the first decision and not the last. Two trips of ten nights can be very different, depending on where the nights are spent and in what order. How to turn a number of nights into a set of bases is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

How many days do you need in Bali from the US?

At least ten nights for a first trip. A week is the practical minimum, since travel and jet lag take about four days in all.

Is 5 days in Bali worth it from the US?

Rarely. Five nights give about three good days after travel and adjustment, unless you are already in Asia.

Is a week in Bali enough?

Enough for Ubud and one beach base. It leaves no room for the north, the east or an island stay.

How long can Americans stay in Bali?

Thirty days on the tourist visa, extendable once for another 30 days.

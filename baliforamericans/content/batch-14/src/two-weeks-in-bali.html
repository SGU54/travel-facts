Two weeks in Bali is enough to leave the south behind for part of the trip. With fourteen nights, a traveler can pair Ubud and a beach base with two of the quieter regions: the central highlands around Munduk, East Bali at Sidemen or Amed, or the Nusa islands. A sensible shape is four bases of three to four nights, moving in one direction around the island.

What two weeks makes possible

Fourteen nights change the arithmetic of a Bali trip. A week covers two areas and ten days three. Two weeks cover four, which is enough to include regions that are too far from the airport for a short stay: Munduk is about 51 miles away by road, and Amed about 61.

The rule of three nights

Our working rule is at least three nights per base, which gives two full days in each. Fourteen nights divided that way gives four bases. A fifth is possible and makes the trip feel like a relay. Our view is that four well-chosen bases beat six hurried ones.

A two-week route

The two-week route below makes a loop through the center, the highlands and the east before finishing on the coast. It is a suggestion to adapt, with no prices and no drive times. Each move is a half-day drive with a driver, and the distances between bases are short in miles.

  • Nights 1 to 4: Ubud.
  • Nights 5 to 7: Munduk and the central highlands.
  • Nights 8 to 10: East Bali, at Sidemen or Amed.
  • Nights 11 to 14: a beach base in the south.

Nights 1 to 4: Ubud

Ubud opens the trip, about 22 miles from the airport. Four nights allow for jet lag, a day on foot in the center, a day of rice terraces and temples, and a sunrise trek on Mount Batur for those who want it, since Kintamani is about 27 miles away.

Nights 5 to 7: Munduk

Munduk is about 39 miles from Ubud by road, and the drive passes the mountain lakes and the temple of Ulun Danu Bratan. Travel guides describe the area as known for waterfalls, coffee and clove plantations and walking, at more than 700 meters of altitude. Pack a sweater.

Nights 8 to 10: the east

From the highlands the route turns east. This is the longest transfer of the trip, about 93 miles to Amed, and it deserves an early start. Sidemen, a valley of rice terraces under Mount Agung, is the alternative for travelers who prefer walking to snorkeling.

Nights 11 to 14: the coast

The trip ends by the sea, close to the airport. From Amed it is about 51 miles to Sanur. Four nights here are for rest, a beach and a last temple visit, with no long drives and no boats before the flight home.

Swapping in an island leg

The Nusa islands can replace either the highlands or the east in this route. Boats leave from Sanur, and travel guides give the crossing as roughly 30 to 45 minutes. An island leg fits best between Ubud and the final coast base, where a delayed boat costs a beach day and nothing more.

Which to swap out

These are our own suggestions. Keep the highlands and swap the east for the islands if you want mountains and sea. Keep the east and swap the highlands for the islands if diving and snorkeling are the point of the trip. Doing all three is possible and leaves little time anywhere.

Why the route runs one way

The order of this route follows the map. Ubud, Munduk, the east and the south form a rough circle, so each drive covers new ground and none doubles back through the busy south. A route that zigzags, from Ubud to Uluwatu and back north to Munduk, spends days on the road.

Transfers as sightseeing

Each transfer is a chance to see what lies between bases. A driver hired for the day can stop at a temple, a waterfall or a viewpoint. Agree the stops and the price in writing beforehand. This is how a two-week trip sees the island without separate day trips.

Start inland, end at the sea

Beginning in Ubud and ending on the coast has two advantages. The early waking of jet lag is put to use among temples and terraces, and the last night falls close to the airport. Reversing the route is workable for travelers who want the beach first.

What two weeks still cannot do

Two weeks are generous and still finite. The far west of the island, with its national park, is a long drive from everything else. The Gili Islands and Lombok need two travel days of their own. Our advice is to leave both for another trip unless one of them is the reason you are coming.

Leave a spare day

A fourteen-night plan with every day assigned is fragile. Rain, a stomach bug or a road closed for a ceremony will take a day from somewhere. Our advice is to leave one day unplanned in the second half, where it can absorb whatever happens.

Practical points for a longer stay

Two weeks are inside the 30 days the tourist visa allows, so no extension is needed. The visa costs IDR 500,000. Check the trip against the calendar: Nyepi falls on Monday, March 8, 2027, and Galungan on January 13 and August 11 that year.

Season

Season shapes this route more than a shorter one, since it visits mountains and two coasts. South Bali's climate record shows about 1 inch of rain in July and about 14 in January. The mountains have their own weather, and the east-facing and west-facing coasts trade places for surf between the seasons.

Medical care and cash

The State Department says adequate medical care may be harder to find in rural areas of Indonesia. Carry the medicine you rely on and enough cash for the quieter regions. How the bases, the transfers and the spare day are ordered for your dates is set out in the complete Bali trip guide.

FAQ

Is two weeks too long for Bali?

No. Fourteen nights allow four bases, including quieter regions such as Munduk and East Bali that a shorter trip cannot reach.

How many places should I stay in two weeks in Bali?

Our suggestion is four bases of three to four nights each.

What is a good two-week Bali route?

Ubud, then Munduk in the highlands, then East Bali at Sidemen or Amed, finishing at a beach base in the south.

Do I need a visa extension for two weeks in Bali?

No. The tourist visa allows a stay of 30 days.

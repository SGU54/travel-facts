TITLE: The Perfect 10-Day Thailand Itinerary
CATEGORY: two-weeks

<p>Ten days is the sweet spot for a first trip to Thailand. It's enough to see all three of the country's headline regions — Bangkok, the northern mountains around Chiang Mai, and the southern islands — without the forced march that a shorter trip becomes. This is the classic first-timer route, and it works because each leg is a short domestic flight from the last.</p>

<p>One housekeeping note before you book: Thailand's visa-free rules for U.S. passport holders have been changing in 2026, and the permitted length of a visa-exempt stay has been in flux. Ten days sits comfortably inside any version of the rule, so this trip is unaffected — but confirm the current entry requirements for your travel date, and file your free Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online before you fly.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: A montage-feel hero — Bangkok temple, northern mountains, and an island beach -->

<h2>Days 1–3: Bangkok</h2>

<p>Most flights from the U.S. connect through East Asia or the Middle East and land at Suvarnabhumi (BKK) late, so treat Day 1 as arrival and recovery — you're 11–12 hours ahead of the U.S. East Coast. Base yourself in <strong>Sukhumvit</strong> for the easiest first-timer experience (it's on the BTS Skytrain) or the <strong>Riverside / Old Town</strong> if you want to be among the temples.</p>

<p>Spend Day 2 on the temple core: the <strong>Grand Palace</strong> and <strong>Wat Pho</strong> in the morning (shoulders and knees covered), then <strong>Wat Arun</strong> across the river at sunset, capped by a <strong>Chinatown</strong> street-food crawl. On Day 3, hit <strong>Chatuchak Weekend Market</strong> if it's a weekend (or the Jim Thompson House midweek), and consider a half-day cooking class or canal boat ride before you fly north.</p>

<h2>Days 4–6: Chiang Mai and the north</h2>

<p>Fly to <strong>Chiang Mai</strong> (CNX) on the morning of Day 4 — about 1 hour 15 minutes. The pace up north is gentler than Bangkok's, and the city is walkable. Spend the afternoon on the <strong>Old City temples</strong> (Wat Chedi Luang, Wat Phra Singh) and the evening at a night market.</p>

<p>Day 5 is for the mountain temple <strong>Wat Phra That Doi Suthep</strong>, which overlooks the city, paired with a Thai <strong>cooking class</strong> — Chiang Mai is one of the best places in the country to take one. Reserve Day 6 for an <strong>ethical elephant sanctuary</strong>: the observation-only kind where you don't ride the animals. Book a reputable one in advance; it's usually a full day including transport.</p>

<h2>Days 7–9: the southern islands</h2>

<p>On Day 7, fly south — Chiang Mai connects to <strong>Phuket</strong> (HKT) and <strong>Krabi</strong> (KBV) directly or via Bangkok. For this itinerary, Krabi is the easier, calmer landing; Phuket if you want more nightlife and resorts. Check in and spend the afternoon decompressing on the beach.</p>

<p>Day 8 is your big island-hopping day: a longtail or speedboat trip to the <strong>Phi Phi Islands</strong>, <strong>Phang Nga Bay</strong>, or the <strong>Hong Islands</strong> lagoons. Go early to beat the crowds. Day 9 is yours to do nothing in particular — a quiet beach, a snorkeling trip, a massage, a sunset longtail to a hidden cove.</p>

<h2>Day 10: Bangkok and home</h2>

<p>Fly back to Bangkok on Day 10. Most long-haul U.S. flights leave late at night, so you'll likely have a few bonus hours for last-minute shopping — a mall like ICONSIAM, or one more market — and a final plate of mango sticky rice before the airport.</p>

<h3>If you only care about beaches</h3>
<p>Skip the north. Do Bangkok for three days, then give a full week to two southern islands — one lively (Phuket or Phi Phi), one calm (Krabi, Koh Lanta, or a Gulf island like Koh Samui if you're traveling mid-year when the Andaman coast is wet).</p>

TITLE: Where to Stay in Phuket: Best Areas and Resorts
CATEGORY: stay-islands

<p>Phuket is big — Thailand's largest island, roughly the size of Singapore — and where you base yourself completely changes the trip. Pick the wrong beach and you'll either be stuck in a party zone you didn't want or marooned far from the action. The island's beaches each have a distinct personality, so the move is to match the area to the kind of trip you're after. Here's how.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: A sweeping Phuket coastline with a beach and headland, daytime -->

<h2>Patong — best for nightlife and action</h2>

<p><strong>Patong</strong> is the loud, bright heart of Phuket: the biggest beach, the most hotels, and the famous (or infamous) Bangla Road nightlife strip. It's crowded, commercial, and not for everyone — but if you want everything within walking distance, a buzzing scene, and the widest range of budget-to-midrange hotels, this is the spot. Choose it for energy and convenience; avoid it if you want calm.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Patong hotels -->

<h2>Kata and Karon — best for families and a calmer beach</h2>

<p>Just south of Patong, <strong>Kata</strong> and <strong>Karon</strong> offer the same lovely Andaman beaches with a fraction of the chaos. They're more relaxed, more family-friendly, and still have plenty of restaurants and shops — a comfortable middle ground between Patong's intensity and the island's quieter corners. This is the easy default for most first-time visitors who want a good beach without the party.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Kata / Karon hotels -->

<h2>Bang Tao and Surin — best for upscale resorts</h2>

<p>On the northwest coast, <strong>Bang Tao</strong> and <strong>Surin</strong> are where Phuket's luxury sits — the long beach at Bang Tao is lined with high-end resorts and the polished "Laguna" complex, while Surin is a smaller, chic stretch. Come here for a resort-focused holiday with space, style, and quieter sand, and expect to rely on taxis or Grab to reach the busier parts of the island.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Bang Tao / Surin resorts -->

<h2>Phuket Old Town — best for culture and food (not the beach)</h2>

<p>Here's the one many people miss: <strong>Phuket Old Town</strong> isn't on a beach at all, but it's the most characterful part of the island — colorful Sino-Portuguese shophouses, the best local food, cafés, and street art. It's a great base for a night or two if you care more about atmosphere and eating well than rolling out of bed onto the sand. Pair it with a beach base, or use it as a day trip from one.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_2: Colorful Sino-Portuguese shophouses of Phuket Old Town -->

<h2>Getting to and around Phuket</h2>

<p>Phuket has its own international airport (HKT) in the north of the island, with direct flights from Bangkok (about 1.5 hours) and some international routes. From the airport, most resorts are 30 minutes to an hour away by taxi or pre-arranged transfer, depending on which beach you've chosen — another reason to pick your base before you book.</p>

<p>Getting around once you're there is the island's weak point. Local taxis have long resisted ride-hailing apps, so fares can be high and negotiation common; Grab and Bolt do operate but availability varies by area. Many visitors rent a scooter for short hops, though Phuket's traffic and hills make that riskier than on flatter islands — and your travel insurance may not cover a crash without a motorcycle license. The simplest approach for most: choose a base near the things you most want to do, and budget for taxis when you venture out.

<h2>How to choose, quickly</h2>

<p>For a first trip, <strong>Kata or Karon</strong> is the safe, comfortable default — a real beach without the madness. Pick <strong>Patong</strong> if nightlife and walkable convenience are the priority, <strong>Bang Tao or Surin</strong> for an upscale resort holiday, and add a night in <strong>Old Town</strong> if food and culture matter to you. Many travelers split their stay: a few nights resort-side, a night or two in Old Town.</p>

<p>One island-wide note: getting around Phuket can be pricier and more awkward than elsewhere in Thailand, since local taxis have historically resisted ride-hailing apps. Grab and Bolt do operate; factor transport into your choice of base, and consider staying near the things you most want to do.</p>

TITLE: Where to Stay in Krabi: Ao Nang, Railay, and Beyond
CATEGORY: stay-islands

<p>Krabi province, on Thailand's Andaman coast just across the bay from Phuket, is where you go for the dramatic limestone-cliff scenery without Phuket's scale and intensity. But "Krabi" covers several very different bases — a beach-town hub, a cliff-ringed peninsula reachable only by boat, and quieter islands offshore. Where you stay shapes whether your trip is convenient, scenic, or secluded. Here's how to choose.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: Krabi's limestone karsts rising from turquoise water with longtail boats -->

<h2>Ao Nang — best for convenience and first-timers</h2>

<p><strong>Ao Nang</strong> is Krabi's main beach town and the practical hub for most visitors. It has the widest range of hotels at every price, plenty of restaurants and shops, and — crucially — it's the launch point for boats to Railay and the islands. The beach itself is pleasant rather than spectacular, but you trade that for genuine convenience: you can walk to dinner, book a tour next door, and hop a longtail in the morning. For a first trip to Krabi, this is the easy choice.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Ao Nang hotels -->

<h2>Railay — best for scenery and a car-free escape</h2>

<p><strong>Railay</strong> is the postcard: a small peninsula hemmed in by sheer limestone cliffs, reachable only by longtail boat (no roads). That isolation is the appeal — no cars, world-class rock climbing, and some of Thailand's most beautiful beaches, including Phra Nang. Railay West has the best swimming and sunsets and the nicer resorts; Railay East is the mangrove-lined boat-landing side with cheaper stays. It's more scenic and more secluded than Ao Nang, at the cost of convenience — everything arrives by boat, so prices run a little higher.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_2: Railay West beach at sunset, longtail boats and towering cliffs -->

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Railay resorts -->

<h2>Koh Lanta — best for a quiet, family-friendly island</h2>

<p>South of Krabi town, <strong>Koh Lanta</strong> is a laid-back island of long, flat beaches and a slower pace — popular with families and anyone who wants to settle in one calm spot rather than chase sights. It's less dramatic than Railay but far more relaxed, with a string of beaches running down the west coast that get quieter the further south you go. Reach it by ferry or minivan-and-ferry from the mainland; it rewards a stay of several days rather than a quick stop.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Koh Lanta hotels -->

<h2>Klong Muang and Tubkaek — best for upscale quiet</h2>

<p>A short drive north of Ao Nang, <strong>Klong Muang</strong> and <strong>Tubkaek</strong> are where Krabi's higher-end resorts cluster — calm beaches, sea views toward the offshore islands, and a peaceful setting. Choose this area for a resort-focused stay where you want quiet and comfort over a walkable town, accepting that you'll drive or taxi to Ao Nang for more dining and nightlife.</p>

<h2>Getting to Krabi and getting around</h2>

<p>Krabi has its own airport (KBV) with direct flights from Bangkok (about 1.5 hours) and some regional international routes; many travelers also arrive overland from Phuket or by ferry from the islands. From the airport it's roughly 30–45 minutes to Ao Nang, the main hub.</p>

<p>Once you're based, getting around is a mix of songthaews (shared pickup trucks), tuk-tuks, and longtail boats — the boats are how you reach Railay and the offshore islands, since there are no roads to Railay at all. Grab availability is more limited here than in Bangkok, so for island and beach hopping you'll lean on boats and arranged tours. If you're staying on Koh Lanta, factor in the ferry or minivan-and-ferry connection from the mainland when planning arrival and departure days.

<h2>How to choose, quickly</h2>

<p>Book <strong>Ao Nang</strong> for your first Krabi trip — it's the convenient hub that connects to everything. Choose <strong>Railay</strong> for scenery, climbing, and a car-free escape; <strong>Koh Lanta</strong> for a quiet, family-friendly island stay; and <strong>Klong Muang/Tubkaek</strong> for an upscale, peaceful resort base. A popular combination is a few nights in Ao Nang or Railay for the scenery, then a few on Koh Lanta to unwind.</p>

TITLE: The Best Island Resorts in Thailand
CATEGORY: stay-islands

<p>Thailand does island resorts as well as anywhere on earth, from honeymoon hideaways on stilts to family-friendly beach clubs. But "best" depends entirely on which island, which coast, and what kind of trip you want — and the right answer changes with the season, because Thailand's two coasts have opposite weather. This guide breaks down where to look for a great resort stay across the country's main island destinations, all within the areas this site covers.</p>

<p>First principle: <strong>match the coast to your dates.</strong> The Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi) is at its best roughly November through April; the Gulf coast (Koh Samui) often shines mid-year. Book the coast that's dry when you travel, then pick the island and area.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: A luxurious Thai beachfront resort pool overlooking the sea -->

<h2>Phuket — the widest range of resorts</h2>

<p><strong>Phuket</strong> has more resorts, at more price points, than anywhere else in Thailand, and its own international airport makes it the easiest to reach. For upscale stays, look at the quieter northwest beaches — <strong>Bang Tao</strong> and <strong>Surin</strong> — where long stretches of sand are lined with high-end properties. Families do well in <strong>Kata</strong> and <strong>Karon</strong>, which pair good beaches with a relaxed scene. Phuket is the safe pick when you want resort choice and easy logistics in one place.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Phuket resorts -->

<h2>Krabi — dramatic scenery, fewer crowds</h2>

<p>For resorts framed by limestone cliffs rather than a busy strip, <strong>Krabi</strong> delivers. The upscale enclaves around <strong>Klong Muang</strong> and <strong>Tubkaek</strong> offer calm beaches and island views in a peaceful setting, while a stay on the <strong>Railay</strong> peninsula puts you among the cliffs with no cars and boat-only access — about as scenic as a Thai resort gets. Krabi suits travelers who want beauty and quiet over nightlife.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Krabi resorts -->

<h2>Koh Samui — the Gulf-coast resort island</h2>

<p>Over on the Gulf coast, <strong>Koh Samui</strong> is the polished resort island, with its own airport and a comfortable, well-developed scene. <strong>Chaweng</strong> is the main beach with the liveliest mix; <strong>Bophut</strong> and its charming Fisherman's Village lean boutique and romantic; <strong>Choeng Mon</strong> is quieter and good for families. Samui is the natural choice for a resort holiday from roughly mid-year, when the Andaman coast is wetter.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_2: A serene Koh Samui beach resort with palms and calm water -->

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Koh Samui resorts -->

<h2>Koh Lanta — relaxed island resorts</h2>

<p>If your idea of a great resort stay is a long, quiet beach and nowhere you need to be, <strong>Koh Lanta</strong> (reached from Krabi) is a strong, lower-key option. Its west-coast beaches host a string of comfortable resorts, with the southern end the quietest. It's less about luxury polish and more about unhurried, settle-in relaxation — ideal for families and couples who want calm.</p>

<h2>What to look for when booking</h2>

<p>A few things separate a great Thai island resort stay from a frustrating one. <strong>Beach access and quality</strong> vary hugely even within one island — read recent reviews to confirm the beach in front of the resort is swimmable (some are rocky or tidal). <strong>Location relative to a town</strong> matters: a remote luxury resort is blissful but means taxi rides for every meal out, while a town-adjacent property trades some quiet for convenience. And <strong>transfer time from the airport or pier</strong> can be an hour or more on the bigger islands, so check it before you book a place that looks close on a map.</p>

<p>For honeymooners and anyone after privacy, look specifically for the words "beachfront" or "pool villa" and confirm what's actually included; for families, prioritize calm, shallow water and on-site dining so you're not commuting with kids at dinnertime.

<h2>What kind of resort stay are you after?</h2>

<p>For a <strong>honeymoon</strong>, lean toward Bophut on Samui or a boutique Krabi/Railay property for romance and scenery. For <strong>families</strong>, Kata/Karon on Phuket, Choeng Mon on Samui, or Koh Lanta offer space and calm. For <strong>maximum choice and easy logistics</strong>, Phuket. For <strong>drama and quiet</strong>, Krabi. And always let the season steer you to the right coast first.</p>

TITLE: Krabi vs. Phuket: Which Should You Pick?
CATEGORY: islands-beaches

<p>Krabi and Phuket sit across the same bay on Thailand's Andaman coast, reach the same famous islands, and share the same turquoise-water-and-limestone-cliff scenery. Yet they feel completely different to travel in, and choosing between them is one of the most common decisions first-timers face. The short version: <strong>Phuket</strong> is the bigger, busier, more convenient resort island; <strong>Krabi</strong> is the calmer, more scenic mainland province with a mellower pace. Here's how to decide.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: Side-by-side feel — a busy Phuket beach and a quiet Krabi karst bay -->

<h2>The quick answer</h2>
<p>Choose <strong>Phuket</strong> if you want maximum convenience, the widest range of hotels and resorts, lively nightlife, and an international airport with direct flights. Choose <strong>Krabi</strong> if you want dramatic limestone scenery, a calmer and more affordable scene, and don't mind trading some convenience for beauty. Many travelers with enough time do <em>both</em> — they're close enough to combine.</p>

<h2>Getting there and around</h2>
<p>Both have their own airport with flights from Bangkok (about 1h25 each). <strong>Phuket</strong> is a large island connected to the mainland by road, with more international flights and a bigger, more developed transport network — though local taxis are notoriously pricey and resistant to ride-hailing apps. <strong>Krabi</strong> is mainland province; its hub, Ao Nang, is compact and walkable, and you reach Railay and the islands by longtail boat. Neither has great public transport, so budget for taxis, songthaews, and boats.</p>

<h2>The vibe and the beaches</h2>
<p><strong>Phuket</strong> ranges from the loud party energy of Patong to the calmer family beaches of Kata and Karon and the upscale resorts of Bang Tao and Surin — there's a beach for every style, but the island is busy and built-up. <strong>Krabi</strong> feels more low-key and scenic: Ao Nang is a pleasant beach town, Railay is a stunning cliff-ringed peninsula reachable only by boat, and Koh Lanta offers quiet, family-friendly sand. For sheer drama and calm, Krabi edges it; for variety and buzz, Phuket.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_2: Railay's longtail boats and cliffs vs a lively Phuket beach scene -->

<h2>Nightlife and dining</h2>
<p>This is where Phuket pulls clearly ahead. <strong>Phuket</strong> has Thailand's most famous beach nightlife (Patong's Bangla Road), a huge range of restaurants, and the characterful Old Town with the island's best food. <strong>Krabi</strong>'s nightlife is mellow by comparison — beach bars and relaxed restaurants in Ao Nang rather than clubs. If a buzzing night scene matters, Phuket; if you want quiet dinners and early boats, Krabi.</p>

<h2>Day trips and islands</h2>
<p>Here they're nearly even, because they share the same waters. From <strong>both</strong> you can boat to the <strong>Phi Phi Islands</strong>, <strong>Phang Nga Bay</strong>, and the <strong>Hong Islands</strong>. Krabi has a slight edge for the Hong Islands and Railay's climbing; Phuket for Phang Nga Bay and the Similan Islands (seasonal). Whichever base you choose, the headline boat trips are within reach.</p>

<h2>Cost</h2>
<p>Krabi generally runs a little cheaper than Phuket across accommodation and dining, though both span budget to luxury and prices swing hard by season (peak is roughly November–April on the Andaman coast). Rather than trust any fixed figure, compare current rates and convert with a live tool:</p>

<h2>So which should you pick?</h2>
<p>For a <strong>first trip focused on scenery and calm</strong>, Krabi — base in Ao Nang or Railay. For <strong>convenience, nightlife, and resort choice</strong>, Phuket. For <strong>families</strong>, both work (Kata/Karon on Phuket, Ao Nang or Koh Lanta on Krabi). And if you have a week or more on the Andaman coast, do both: a few nights in Krabi for the cliffs, a few in Phuket for the resorts and food, linked by a short ferry or drive.</p>

TITLE: Thailand for Honeymooners: The Romantic Itinerary
CATEGORY: itineraries-by-interest

<p>Thailand is one of the world's great honeymoon destinations: it pairs genuine culture and adventure with some of the most beautiful — and surprisingly affordable — luxury resorts anywhere. The ideal honeymoon here isn't a non-stop sightseeing march; it's a blend of a little city and culture up front, then a slow, indulgent stretch on the islands. Here's how to build it.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: A romantic beachfront villa or infinity pool at sunset in Thailand -->

<h2>The shape of a great Thailand honeymoon</h2>
<p>For a 10-to-14-day honeymoon, the winning structure is roughly: <strong>two or three days in Bangkok</strong> to land and soak up the energy, <strong>two or three days in the north</strong> around Chiang Mai for culture and a shared adventure, then <strong>five-plus days on the islands</strong> to unwind in a beautiful resort. The cities are the appetizer; the islands are the main course. If you only have a week, cut the north and do Bangkok plus a longer island stay.</p>

<h2>Days 1–3: Bangkok, in style</h2>
<p>Base in a riverside hotel for romance and views, see the headline temples — the <strong>Grand Palace</strong>, <strong>Wat Pho</strong>, <strong>Wat Arun</strong> at sunset — and have a special dinner at a rooftop bar overlooking the skyline or a fine-dining room. Keep it to a couple of days; Bangkok is thrilling but intense, and you'll want energy for what's next.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Bangkok riverside hotels -->

<h2>Days 4–6: the north, for a shared adventure</h2>
<p>Fly to <strong>Chiang Mai</strong> for a change of pace. Wander the old-city temples, take a <strong>couples' cooking class</strong>, visit the mountain temple <strong>Doi Suthep</strong>, and — the honeymoon highlight for many — spend a day at an <strong>ethical elephant sanctuary</strong> (observation-only, no riding). It's the kind of shared experience you'll both remember long after the beach.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_2: A couple-friendly scene — elephants in a green sanctuary or a misty northern temple -->

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: getyourguide — Chiang Mai elephant sanctuary / cooking class -->

<h2>Days 7–12: island luxury</h2>
<p>This is where the honeymoon truly begins. Fly south and settle into a standout resort for five nights or more. For <strong>romance and boutique charm</strong>, look at Bophut and its Fisherman's Village on Koh Samui, or a boutique property on Krabi's Railay peninsula. For <strong>dramatic scenery</strong>, a cliffside or beachfront villa on Krabi. For <strong>pure resort polish with easy logistics</strong>, Phuket's quieter northwest beaches (Bang Tao, Surin).</p>
<p>Match the coast to your season: the Andaman islands (Phuket, Krabi) are best roughly November–April; Koh Samui and the Gulf shine mid-year. A private pool villa is the classic honeymoon splurge, and in Thailand it costs a fraction of what the equivalent would run in the Maldives or Caribbean.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — island honeymoon resorts / pool villas -->

<h2>Romantic touches worth booking</h2>
<p>A private longtail-boat sunset cruise, a couples' spa day (Thai massage is world-class and inexpensive), a private beach dinner, and an island-hopping day to a quiet lagoon all elevate the trip without much planning. Many resorts arrange honeymoon extras — flag that you're celebrating when you book.</p>

<h2>Budgeting the splurge</h2>

TITLE: 7 Days in Northern Thailand
CATEGORY: one-week

<p>Not everyone comes to Thailand for beaches. If you're drawn to temples, cooler mountain air, Thai cooking, and ethical elephant experiences, a week in the north is a richer, more cultural trip than the standard beach run. This route pairs a taste of Bangkok with the highlands around Chiang Mai. Here's how to spend seven days up north.</p>

<p>As always, confirm your entry requirements before booking — Thailand's visa-free rules for Americans have been changing in 2026, though a week is within any version. File the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online before you fly.</p>

<h2>Days 1–2: Bangkok</h2>
<p>Land in the capital and give it two days. Cover the essentials — the <strong>Grand Palace</strong>, <strong>Wat Pho</strong>, and <strong>Wat Arun</strong> at sunset — plus a <strong>Chinatown</strong> street-food crawl. Base near the BTS Skytrain in Sukhumvit, or by the river for atmosphere. Two days is enough to feel the city before you head for calmer ground.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: getyourguide — Bangkok temple tour -->

<h2>Days 3–5: Chiang Mai</h2>
<p>Fly to <strong>Chiang Mai</strong> (about 1h15) — or take the atmospheric overnight sleeper train if you'd rather trade a night for the scenery. The northern capital is walkable, relaxed, and packed with character.</p>
<p><strong>Day 3:</strong> explore the <strong>Old City temples</strong> (Wat Chedi Luang, Wat Phra Singh) and a night market. <strong>Day 4:</strong> the mountain temple <strong>Doi Suthep</strong> for sweeping city views, plus a <strong>Thai cooking class</strong> in the afternoon. <strong>Day 5:</strong> a full day at an <strong>ethical elephant sanctuary</strong> — the observation-only kind — which is the highlight of the north for many visitors.</p>

TITLE: The Best Rooftop Hotels in Bangkok
CATEGORY: stay-bangkok

<p>Bangkok does rooftops better than almost any city on earth — the skyline is dramatic, the warm evenings are made for it, and a sunset drink high above the traffic is one of the city's signature experiences. Staying in a hotel with its own rooftop (or near the famous ones) turns Bangkok's intensity into something magical. Here's where to look and how to choose a rooftop base.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_1: A Bangkok rooftop bar or infinity pool overlooking the glittering skyline at dusk -->

<h2>Why a rooftop hotel is worth it in Bangkok</h2>
<p>Bangkok at street level is hot, loud, and frenetic. From a rooftop, it transforms: the chaos becomes a glittering carpet of lights, the temperature drops with the breeze, and the Chao Phraya River winds through it all. A hotel with a rooftop pool or bar gives you that view on demand — a place to decompress after a day of temples and markets. Even if your room doesn't have the view, staying near the landmark sky bars makes sunset drinks effortless.</p>

<h2>Best areas for rooftop hotels</h2>

<h3>Silom and Sathorn — the rooftop heartland</h3>
<p>This business district is home to some of Bangkok's most famous sky bars and the hotels that house them. It's well connected (on both the BTS and MRT), making it an excellent base generally, and the concentration of high-rise hotels means the best skyline views. If rooftops are your priority, start here.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Silom / Sathorn rooftop hotels -->

<h3>Sukhumvit — rooftops plus convenience</h3>
<p>Bangkok's modern dining-and-nightlife spine also has plenty of rooftop hotels, especially around Asok and Thonglor. You get skyline views <em>and</em> the easy access to restaurants, malls, and the Skytrain that makes Sukhumvit the easiest first-timer base.</p>

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Sukhumvit rooftop hotels -->

<h3>Riverside — views of the river, not just the skyline</h3>
<p>The riverside luxury hotels offer a different kind of rooftop experience — sweeping views over the Chao Phraya and the temples along it, rather than the corporate skyline. It's the most romantic option, and the riverside properties are among the most celebrated hotels in Asia, though the area is less connected to the metro.</p>

<!-- IMAGE_SLOT_2: A riverside Bangkok hotel pool overlooking the Chao Phraya River and temples -->

<!-- AFFILIATE_SLOT: booking — Riverside hotels -->

<h2>How to choose a rooftop hotel</h2>
<p>Decide what view you want — <strong>skyline</strong> (Silom/Sathorn or Sukhumvit) or <strong>river</strong> (Riverside). Confirm the rooftop is actually accessible to guests (some famous sky bars are separate venues, and some hotel rooftops have dress codes or minimum spends). And don't sacrifice location for the view alone: a rooftop hotel that's a long taxi from the Skytrain will cost you time daily. The best choice pairs a great view with a walkable transit connection.</p>

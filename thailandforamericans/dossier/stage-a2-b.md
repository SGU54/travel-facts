# Stage A2 + B — Thailand, Covered. The spine and the eight decisions

## A2 · The organising fact

> **Four islands — Koh Phangan, Koh Tao, Koh Lanta, Koh Chang — have no airstrip,
> clinics instead of hospitals, three separately-booked legs to reach them, and
> the crossings most often stopped by weather. It is the same four every time.
> The place where help is most likely to be needed is the place where it is
> slowest to arrange — and the plan is made as if the islands were interchangeable.**

**This is not an inference. The corpus states it twice, independently.**

> "Read those two lists together and the geography does something specific: the
> islands with clinic-level care rather than hospitals are largely the same
> islands with no airstrip. The place where a transfer is most likely to be
> needed is the place where it is slowest to arrange."
> — `medical-evacuation-from-thai-islands` (draft)

> "These are the same four islands that have clinics rather than hospitals, and
> Koh Tao and Koh Phangan are among the crossings most frequently suspended in
> rough weather. Three separate pieces of information, arrived at independently,
> describing the same set of places."
> — `getting-to-the-southern-islands-by-air` (draft)

Four systems — air access, medical capability, booking structure, weather
exposure — draw the **same line** through the same places. That is why this is a
spine and not a theme: it is one threshold visible in four unrelated registers.

### The boundaries — where the spine does NOT govern

The review of the previous three books found the organising fact being stretched
into a master explanation. So this one is bounded in writing, before a word is
written around it:

- **It does not govern Bangkok or Chiang Mai.** Those are the serviced side by
  definition; the spine is about what changes when you leave it.
- **It does not rank the islands.** Koh Tao is not "worse" than Samui. It is
  differently exposed, and the corpus does not score them.
- **It is not a size gradient** — and the corpus supplies the counterexample
  itself: *"Koh Tao has hyperbaric chamber facilities … the facilities follow the
  diving, not the population. This is the one category where a small island has
  specialist capability that a larger one may not."* The small island beats the
  large one on the emergency it actually generates.
- **The serviced side has its own inversion.** *"Koh Samui airport is privately
  owned and operated by Bangkok Airways, which limits which carriers serve it.
  The island with the most direct air access has the most constrained set of
  operators on that access."* Best access, fewest alternatives when it fails.

Those two counterexamples are in the book, near the front. A spine that cannot
show where it stops is a slogan.

## B · The eight decisions

Order is corpus weight. Each carries the `compare` block required by
`b-decide.md`, and `scope` / verdict / reversal are three separate fields.

### 01 · RIDE — what may you drive, and what are you insured on?
**STRONG · 13,737w · 15 articles** — `city-transport` + `health-and-safety`
(sources incl. drafts `renting-a-car-in-thailand`, `grab-and-taxi-coverage-thailand`,
`getting-around-thai-islands-without-a-scooter`, `thailand-road-conditions-by-region`)

- **compare** · traveller: two adults, one island week · alternatives: scooter /
  car / Grab / songthaew-and-taxi · unit: **what each requires, what each
  excludes** — not a price · includes: licence requirement, insurance position,
  availability by place · excludes: comfort, fun · uncertainty: fares are
  reported ranges, not tariffs.
- **scope:** you intend to move around the island at all.
- The corpus supports a *requirement and exclusion* comparison, not a cost
  comparison — fares differ by island and are reported as ranges. **The ledger
  will not carry a price column**, because inventing one is the Rome p12 failure.
- Ties to the spine: Grab runs on the three serviced places and **on none of the
  four**. The island where a scooter is most tempting is the island where the
  alternative does not exist and the clinic cannot treat the fall.

### 02 · SEQUENCE — how many islands actually fit, and in what order?
**STRONG · 11,444w · 13 articles** — the whole itinerary set, 7/10/14/21 days
plus the by-interest variants. **The previous book never wrote this at all.**

- **compare** · traveller: first trip, 7–14 nights from the US · alternatives:
  one island / two / three · unit: **nights actually spent on an island**, after
  transfer legs are deducted · includes: the flight, the road transfer, the boat,
  and the return of each · excludes: the international flight · uncertainty:
  crossing times vary with season.
- **scope:** you are choosing how many places to sleep in.
- Ties to the spine: a hop onto an un-serviced island is **three legs each way**,
  each separately booked. The cost of a second island is not the fare; it is the
  nights it removes and the recovery options it gives up.

### 03 · BASE — which island, and which side of the line?
**STRONG · 11,136w · 13 articles** — `islands-beaches` + `stay-islands` + `beaches-islands`

- **compare** · traveller: as above · alternatives: the seven places in FIG. 1 ·
  unit: **the four threshold axes** — airstrip, medical, Grab, walkability ·
  includes: what the corpus documents · excludes: beauty, price, "vibe" ·
  uncertainty: none of these are ranked, and this book will not rank them.
- **scope:** the trip includes an island at all.
- Carries FIG. 1. The decision is **not "which is best"** — it is *which side of
  the line your trip can afford to be on, given what else you have planned.*

### 04 · TRANSIT — how do the legs connect, and who owns the failure?
**VIABLE · 8,388w · 10** — `flights-trains` + `ferries-islands` (drafts:
`domestic-flights-vs-overnight-train-thailand`, `bangkok-airport-transfer-bkk-dmk`,
`thailand-domestic-flight-baggage-and-changes`, `getting-to-the-southern-islands-by-air`)

- **compare** · alternatives: one ticket / separate tickets; flight / sleeper
  train; BKK / DMK · unit: **who is responsible when a leg is missed** · includes
  the two-airport gap (≈50 km) · uncertainty: **last departure times from the
  southern airports are not reliably documented** — the corpus says so, and the
  book will say so rather than supply a number.
- **scope:** any trip with a domestic leg.
- The corpus's own words: *"a leg booked separately from the international ticket
  carries no protection at all."*

### 05 · INSURE — what are you exposed to, and what does a policy answer?
**VIABLE · 8,069w · 9** — `health-and-safety` (drafts:
`what-travel-insurance-actually-covers-thailand`, `medical-evacuation-from-thai-islands`,
`medical-care-in-thailand-for-visitors`)

- **compare** · alternatives: the coverage categories a policy bundles · unit:
  **which specific Thai scenario each one pays for** · excludes: named insurers,
  prices, any recommendation of a product · uncertainty: policy wordings differ
  and the corpus documents categories, not contracts.
- **scope:** you are going to an island with clinic-level care.
- The sharpest corpus line: *"evacuation and repatriation are separate benefits"*
  — a policy with evacuation but no repatriation "will get someone from Koh Tao
  to a Bangkok hospital" and no further.

### 06 · CITY — Bangkok: where to sleep, and how to cross it
**VIABLE · 5,957w · 7** — `bangkok` + `stay-bangkok`

- **compare** · alternatives: the districts the corpus describes · unit:
  **transit line access**, the same instrument for each · scope: you are spending
  nights in Bangkok rather than transiting.
- Ties to the spine as its inversion: Bangkok is where every system works, which
  is exactly why arriving there tells you nothing about the rest of the trip.

### 07 · CULTURE — temples, etiquette, and the wildlife question
**VIABLE · 5,859w · 7** — `temples-culture` + `etiquette-culture` + `ethical-wildlife`

- **compare** · the wildlife decision is the real one and it is an **ethics**
  comparison, not a price one: what a venue does, not what it calls itself.
- **scope:** you are considering an elephant or animal attraction at all.

### 08 · EAT — ordering well without a reservation system you can use
**VIABLE · 5,781w · 7** — `street-food-markets` + `dishes-what-to-order` + `drinks-nightlife`

- **compare** · alternatives: market / street stall / restaurant · unit: **what
  each is reliably good at** · excludes: **named restaurants** (fleet rule).
- **scope:** every trip.

## Page budget

97 articles × 0.5 ≈ **48 pages**. Eight decisions at 250–350 words of body each,
the essays, FIG. 1 plus the figures the corpus supports (the leg-count diagram,
the coverage map, the policy-category grid), and the Field Sheet.

The previous book was 23pp at 0.24 pp/article and fails all three depth signals.
The gap is closed by **writing the unmined veins**, not by padding — `audit-book.py`'s
page-shape check fails inflated type and air, and `a-mine.md` names all four
padding levers explicitly.
